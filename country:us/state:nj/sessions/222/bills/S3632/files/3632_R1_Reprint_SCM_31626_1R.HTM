SYNOPSIS

     Increases and modifies certain fees and penalties regarding weighing, measuring, labeling, packaging, and inspection of certain commodities and commercial products.  

 

CURRENT VERSION OF TEXT

     As reported by the Senate Commerce Committee on March 16, 2026, with amendments.

  


An Act concerning weights and measures, supplementing Title 51 of the Revised Statutes and amending various sections of statutory law. 

 

     Be It Enacted by the Senate and General Assembly of the State of New Jersey:

 

     1.    (New Section)  The Legislature finds and declares that:

     a.     The primary purpose of Weights and Measures enforcement is to protect consumers by ensuring accurate measurement of goods and services and the truthful disclosure of those measurements. Weights and measures officers are responsible for overseeing compliance with laws and regulations governing weights, measures, packages, containers, pricing accuracy, weight certificates, delivery tickets, invoices, or any other documents setting forth the quantity or value of certain commodities or services.

     b.    Effective enforcement of strong weights and measures laws fosters honest dealing in the marketplace, which benefits both consumers and businesses.  Fair trade practices enable legitimate businesses to compete fairly and effectively against those entities that might engage in fraudulent or deceptive practices.  Consumer confidence is bolstered when they know there are robust protections in place to deter fraud and ensure the accuracy of information about the goods and services they are being offered.

     c.     Toward this end, weights and measures laws are in place to ensure that devices such as scales and fuel pumps are accurately calibrated and sufficiently reliable, and that they are used appropriately and consistently.  Over the years, the Legislature has enacted numerous laws establishing fines and civil penalties designed to promote compliance with these requirements.  However, the majority of these penalties were established prior to 1970, and most of these fines have not been increased in nearly 40 years.  In many instances, the threat of imposition of these fines may be viewed as providing insufficient economic incentive to motivate businesses to update or maintain their equipment or to ensure its accuracy, as the increased profits and compliance expenses avoided far outweigh the fiscal impact of any fines that may be levied against them for noncompliance.

     d.    Similarly, fees are intended to support enforcement activities, such as conducting inspections and field investigations and bringing enforcement actions against violators, to increase compliance by providing an appropriate level of enforcement.  Most of the applicable fees in this area were established by statute decades ago, and have not been adjusted.  Providing the Superintendent of Weights and Measures with the flexibility to increase these fees through the rulemaking process to better cover the costs of administration and enforcement will enhance enforcement efforts both at the county level and Statewide.

     e.     Therefore, it is necessary and appropriate to revise the various laws establishing licensing fees and applicable penalties for weights and measures violations to increase these amounts to a level commensurate with todays economic realities, to better protect consumers and more effectively promote fair trade practices.
