S3632 SENATE, No. 3632 STATE OF NEW JERSEY 222nd LEGISLATURE INTRODUCED FEBRUARY 19, 2026 Sponsored by: Senator  M. TERESA RUIZ District 29 (Essex and Hudson) Senator  NICHOLAS P. SCUTARI District 22 (Somerset and Union) SYNOPSIS Increases and modifies certain fees and penalties regarding weighing, measuring, labeling, packaging, and inspection of certain commodities and commercial products. CURRENT VERSION OF TEXT As introduced. An Act concerning weights and measures, supplementing Title 51 of the Revised Statutes and amending various sections of statutory law. Be It Enacted by the Senate and General Assembly of the State of New Jersey: 1.    (New Section)  The Legislature finds and declares that: a.    The primary purpose of Weights and Measures enforcement is to protect consumers by ensuring accurate measurement of goods and services and the truthful disclosure of those measurements. Weights and measures officers are responsible for overseeing compliance with laws and regulations governing weights, measures, packages, containers, pricing accuracy, weight certificates, delivery tickets, invoices, or any other documents setting forth the quantity or value of certain commodities or services. b.    Effective enforcement of strong weights and measures laws fosters honest dealing in the marketplace, which benefits both consumers and businesses.  Fair trade practices enable legitimate businesses to compete fairly and effectively against those entities that might engage in fraudulent or deceptive practices. Consumer confidence is bolstered when they know there are robust protections in place to deter fraud and ensure the accuracy of information about the goods and services they are being offered. c.    Toward this end, weights and measures laws are in place to ensure that devices such as scales and fuel pumps are accurately calibrated and sufficiently reliable, and that they are used appropriately and consistently.  Over the years, the Legislature has enacted numerous laws establishing fines and civil penalties designed to promote compliance with these requirements.  However, the majority of these penalties were established prior to 1970, and most of these fines have not been increased in nearly 40 years.  In many instances, the threat of imposition of these fines may be viewed as providing insufficient economic incentive to motivate businesses to update or maintain their equipment or to ensure its accuracy, as the increased profits and compliance expenses avoided far outweigh the fiscal impact of any fines that may be levied against them for noncompliance. d.    Similarly, fees are intended to support enforcement activities, such as conducting inspections and field investigations and bringing enforcement actions against violators, to increase compliance by providing an appropriate level of enforcement.  Most of the applicable fees in this area were established by statute decades ago, and have not been adjusted.  Providing the Superintendent of Weights and Measures with the flexibility to increase these fees through the rulemaking process to better cover the costs of administration and enforcement will enhance enforcement efforts both at the county level and Statewide. e.    Therefore, it is necessary and appropriate to revise the various laws establishing licensing fees and applicable penalties for weights and measures violations to increase these amounts to a level commensurate with todays economic realities, to better protect consumers and more effectively promote fair trade practices. 2.    R.S.51:1-17 is amended to read as follows: 51:1-17.  All articles of food, other than liquids, which are capable of being measured by dry STATEMENT This bill increases and modifies fees and penalties under Title 51 of the Revised Statutes pertaining to the weighing, measuring, labeling, packaging, and inspection of certain commodities and standards for certain commercial products including, but not limited to, food sold by weight, lumber and lumber products; gold, silver, and alloys thereof; platinum and alloys thereof; precious metals; liquid fuel; liquefied gas; and soil enriching and correcting products. The fees and penalties under Title 51 are in place to ensure that devices such as scales and fuel pumps are accurate and used correctly and consistently.  The majority of these fees and penalties have not been increased in decades.  The sponsor intends to update these licensing fees and penalties to make them commensurate with todays economic realities in order to promote compliance by businesses and better cover the costs of conducting enforcement activities, such as inspections, field investigations, and bringing enforcement actions against violators.
