SYNOPSIS

     Expands requirements for bidders of certain public work projects.

 

CURRENT VERSION OF TEXT

     As introduced.

  


An Act concerning the prevailing wage and amending P.L.2021, c.301.

 

     Be It Enacted by the Senate and General Assembly of the State of New Jersey:

 

     1. Section 1 of P.L.2021, c.301 (C.34:11-56.27a) is amended to read as follows:

     1. a. If a [person] bidder makes the lowest bid for a contract with a public body for public work subject to the provisions of the "New Jersey Prevailing Wage Act," P.L.1963, c.150 (C.34:11-56.25 et seq.) and that bid is ten percent or more lower than the next lowest bid for the contract [,] :

     (1) the [person] bidder making the lowest bid shall [certify] provide to the public body [that the prevailing wage rates required by that act shall be paid. If the bidder does not provide the certification prior to award of the contract,] and the project architect the full estimate, including, but not limited to material quotes, subcontractor and sub-tier quotes, supervision, insurances, and allowances showing the total bid amount, and issuing a schedule of value;

     (2) the bidder shall provide to the public body and the project architect:

     (a) a certification that the prevailing wage rates required by the New Jersey Prevailing Wage Act," P.L.1963, c.150 (C.34:11-56.25 et seq.) shall be paid, including base wage and fringe benefits, based on applicable wage determinations for the appropriate locality, craft, and classification; a certification that under the penalty of perjury the bidder affirms the statements concerning the bid are true and that the bidder is aware that false statements are subject to criminal penalties;

and

     (c) a certified contract schedule;

     (3) the bidder and any project subcontractor set forth in section 16 of P.L.1971, c.198 (C.40A:11-16) shall provide to the public body and the project architect an affidavit for all of the specifications, drawings, addenda, and clarifications; certificates of insurance coverage including builders risk and any bonding for the project; a certification that there shall be no substitutions or material changes in the bid; a certification of participation in an approved apprenticeship program; and signed non-debarment documents; and

     (4) the bidder shall provide to the public body any notice of  violation issued by the department that the bidder has received and information concerning any of the bidders projects that were stopped by the department and the reasons for the stoppage. 

     The bid information required by paragraph (1) of this subsection shall be submitted within three days of receiving notice that the bidder submitted a bid 10 percent or more lower than the next lowest bid.  Failure to provide the information within three days shall be cause for the bid to be rejected by the public body.  The certifications and information required by paragraphs (2) through (4) of this subsection shall be submitted within five days of receiving notice that the bidder submitted a bid 10 percent or more lower than the next lowest bid.  Failure to provide the information within five days shall be cause for the bid to be rejected by the public body.  If a bid is rejected for failure to submit certifications or bid information, the public body shall award the contract to the next lowest responsible and responsive bidder. [This certification]  The bidder may withdraw a bid from consideration at any time during the process without penalty.  Submission of certifications and bid information shall be required only when a public body is engaging in competitive bidding for public work.  A public body may engage a third party cost estimator for an evaluation of the bid.

     b.    The Commissioner of Labor and Workforce Development, in consultation with the Division of Local Government Services in the Department of Community Affairs, shall promulgate rules and regulations concerning the standardization of the [certification] certifications and bid information necessary to effectuate the provisions of subsection a. of this section.

     c. If a public work project is not completed pursuant to the certified contract schedule submitted pursuant to paragraph (2) of subsection a. of this section, all liquidated damages contained in the bid documents shall be charged.

     d. If a bidder violates any provision of the New Jersey Prevailing Wage Act," P.L.1963, c.150 (C.34:11-56.25 et seq.) while engaged in a public work project, the bidder shall be debarred from bidding on a public work project for one year.

(cf: P.L.2021, c.301, s.1)

 

     2.  This act shall take effect immediately.

 

 

STATEMENT

 

     This bill expands requirements concerning information and certifications that the lowest bidder of a public work project must submit when the bid is 10 percent or more lower than the next lowest bid.

     Under current law, when a bidder makes the lowest bid for a contract with a public body for public work subject to the New Jersey Prevailing Wage Act, (act) and the bid is 10 percent or more lower than the next lowest bid, the bidder is required to certify to the public body that the prevailing wage will be paid. Failure to certify results in the contract being awarded to the next lowest responsible and responsive bidder. This bill requires a lowest bidder to provide additional certifications and bid information under these circumstances.

     Under the bill, the bidder is required to provide to the public body and the project architect the full estimate, including, but not limited to material quotes, subcontractor and sub-tier quotes, supervision, insurances, and allowances showing the total bid amount, and issuing a schedule of value.

     The bill additionally provides that a bidder is required to provide to the public body and the project architect: (1) a certification that the prevailing wage rates required by the act will be paid, including base wage and fringe benefits, based on applicable wage determinations for the appropriate locality, craft and classification; (2) a certification that under the penalty of perjury the bidder affirms the statements concerning the bid are true and that the bidder is aware that false statements are subject to criminal penalties; and (3) a certified contract schedule.

     The bill further requires the bidder and certain project subcontractors to provide to the public body and the project architect: (1) an affidavit for all of the specifications, drawings, addenda, and clarifications; (2) certificates of insurance coverage including builders risk and any bonding for the project; (3) a certification that there will be no substitutions or material changes in the bid; (4) a certification of participation in an approved apprenticeship program; and (5) signed non-debarment documents.

     The bill also requires the bidder to submit to the public body any notice of violation issued by the Department of Labor and Workforce Development that the bidder has received and information concerning any of the bidders projects that were stopped by the department and the reasons for the stoppage. Failure to submit the full quote within three days and the rest of the certificates and information required by the bill within five days will result in the bid being rejected and the contract awarded to the next lowest responsible and responsive bidder.

     The bill provides that the bidder may withdraw a bid from consideration at any time during the process without penalty. A public body may engage a third party cost estimator for an evaluation of the bid. Additionally, if a public work project is not completed pursuant to the certified contract schedule submitted pursuant to the bill all liquidated damages contained in the bid documents will be charged. Finally, the bill provides that if a bidder violates any provisions of the act while engaged in a public work project, the bidder will be debarred form bidding on a public work project for one year.
