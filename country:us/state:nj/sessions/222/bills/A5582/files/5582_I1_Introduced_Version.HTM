SYNOPSIS

     Strengthens detection of, and support measures for individuals diagnosed with, pancreatic cancer.

 

CURRENT VERSION OF TEXT

     As introduced.

  


An Act concerning pancreatic cancer and supplementing and amending various parts of the statutory law.

 

     Be It Enacted by the Senate and General Assembly of the State of New Jersey:

    

     1.  a.  The Department of Health shall develop partnerships with hospital systems, research and higher education institutions, and cancer centers in the State to develop high-risk pancreatic cancer screenings programs in each county in the State.  The department shall develop screening guidelines for such pancreatic cancer screenings programs.

     b.  The department shall establish a program to provide grants to an entity that:

     (1)  establishes a high-risk pancreatic cancer screening program pursuant to the guidelines developed pursuant to subsection a. of this act; or

     (2) supports pancreatic cancer patients, survivors, and their caregivers, including an entity that provides the following services to pancreatic cancer patients:  transportation; childcare; caregiver respite; housing assistance during treatment of pancreatic cancer; or mental health counseling.

     c.  An entity who elects to participate in the grant program shall submit an application to the Commissioner of Health on a form and in a manner prescribed by the commissioner.  Upon receipt of an application, the commissioner shall review the application and, subject to the availability of funds, award a grant to each approved grant applicant in an amount to be determined by the commissioner.

     d.  The Commissioner of Health shall adopt rules and regulations, pursuant to the Administrative Procedure Act, P.L.1968, c.410 (C.52:14B-1 et seq.), as may be necessary to implement the provisions of this section.

    

     2.  The Department of Health shall develop guidelines for health care providers to support patients diagnosed with pancreatic cancer navigate the healthcare system, such guidelines, at a minimum, shall provide that the patient, within a designated timeframe and when such services are applicable to the patient, is to receive the following services:  services for a nurse navigator; services from a social worker; nutrition consultation services; mental healthcare services; financial assistance guidance services; and information on clinical trials related to pancreatic cancer.

 

     3.  The State Board of Medical Examiners shall require that the number of credits of continuing medical education required of each person licensed as a physician, as a condition of biennial registration pursuant to section 1 of P.L.1971, c.236 (C.45:9-6.1), include one credit of educational programs or topics concerning the detection of symptoms of pancreatic cancer, including an in-depth understanding of pancreatic cancers connection to persistent upper abdominal pain, back pain, jaundice, unexplained weight loss, new-onset diabetes after the age of 50, and the patients family history.  The continuing medical education requirement in this subsection shall be subject to the provisions of section 10 of P.L.2001, c.307 (C.45:9-7.1).

 

     4.  a.  The New Jersey Board of Nursing shall require that the number of credits of continuing education required of each person licensed as a professional nurse or a practical nurse, as a condition of biennial license renewal, include one credit of educational programs or topics concerning the detection of symptoms of pancreatic cancer, including an in-depth understanding of pancreatic cancers connection to persistent upper abdominal pain, back pain, jaundice, unexplained weight loss, new-onset diabetes after the age of 50, and the patients family history.

     b.    The board may, in its discretion, waive the continuing education requirement in subsection a. of this section on an individual basis for reasons of hardship, such as illness or disability, retirement of the license, or other good cause.  A waiver shall apply only to the current biennial renewal period at the time of board issuance.

     c.    The New Jersey Board of Nursing, pursuant to the "Administrative Procedure Act," P.L.1968, c.410 (C.52:14B-1 et seq.), shall adopt such rules and regulations as are necessary to effectuate the purposes of this section.

STATEMENT

 

     This bill strengthens the detection of, and support measures for individuals diagnosed with, pancreatic cancer in this State.

     The bill requires the Department of Health (DOH) to develop partnerships with hospital systems, research and higher education institutions, and cancer centers in the State to develop high-risk pancreatic cancer screenings programs in each county in the State.  DOH will develop screening guidelines for such pancreatic cancer screenings programs.

     The bill requires DOH to establish a program to provide grants to an entity that establishes a high-risk pancreatic cancer screening program or an entity that supports pancreatic cancer patients, survivors, and their caregivers.

     The bill requires DOH to develop guidelines for health care providers to support patients diagnosed with pancreatic cancer navigate the healthcare system, such guidelines, at a minimum, will provide that the patient, within a designated timeframe and when such services are applicable to the patient, is to receive the following services:  services for a nurse navigator; services from a social worker; nutrition consultation services; mental healthcare services; financial assistance guidance services; and information on clinical trials related to pancreatic cancer.

     The bill establishes continuing education requirements for physicians and nurses to require one credit of educational programs or topics concerning the detection of symptoms of pancreatic cancer.

     The bill requires health insurance carriers, the State Health Benefits Commission, and the School Employees Health Benefits Commission to cover genetic testing, biomarker testing, genetic counseling, and nutrition-related supports for a person diagnosed with pancreatic cancer and annual screening for individuals with a high risk of developing pancreatic cancer.

     The bill revises the States Law Against Discrimination to include pancreatic cancer under the laws definition of disability requiring employers to provide reasonable accommodations to individuals diagnosed with pancreatic cancer.
