STATEMENT This bill, which is designated as the Consolidated Transportation Capital Planning Act, amends current law to consolidate and revise certain capital planning documents for the States transportation entities.  Specifically, the bill requires the Department of Transportation (DOT), in consultation with the New Jersey Transit Corporation (NJT), the New Jersey Turnpike Authority (NJTA), and the South Jersey Transportation Authority (SJTA), to meet no less than twice each year to collaboratively develop the list of projects to be included in the States Annual Transportation Capital Program (capital program) and to include in the capital program a description of the benefits to the States transportation system from its collaborative development and holistic transportation planning.  The bill specifies that the capital program is to represent a holistic plan to meet the States transportation needs through the complimentary projects proposed by each transportation entity responsible for developing the capital program.  However, nothing in the bill is to be construed to require legislative approval of transportation projects undertaken by the NJTA or the SJTA or to require or otherwise authorize the funding of such projects through the Transportation Trust Fund. In addition, the bill requires the capital program to include projects proposed to be financed in the ensuing fiscal year and for two fiscal years thereafter and requires the DOT to publish information regarding the capital program on its Internet website in a palatable and user-friendly format that promotes public awareness and understanding of the States holistic transportation planning efforts across the States different transportation entities.  The bill also removes a requirement under current law for the DOT to develop a five-year capital plan; requires the SJTA to submit a three-year, rather than a five-year, capital plan to certain individuals; and requires the DOT to include the SJTAs three-year capital plan in the capital program. Finally, the bill requires the DOT, NJT, the NJTA, and the SJTA to appear together before the Senate Budget and Appropriations Committee and the Assembly Budget Committee to discuss the projects proposed in the capital program each year after the Governors budget address but before the end of the fiscal year.  The bill also requires the DOT to submit a report to the Governor and the Legislature describing actions taken by the DOT, NJT, the NJTA, and the SJTA to collaboratively develop the capital program, as outlined in the bill. Under current law, the capital program only includes projects proposed by the DOT and the NJT for the ensuing fiscal year.
