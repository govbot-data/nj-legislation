A5755 ASSEMBLY, No. 5755 STATE OF NEW JERSEY 222nd LEGISLATURE INTRODUCED NOVEMBER 16, 2026 Sponsored by: Assemblywoman  ANNETTE QUIJANO District 20 (Union) SYNOPSIS Directs Division of Local Planning Services in DCA to develop and publish Data Center Planning and Community Engagement Toolkit. CURRENT VERSION OF TEXT As introduced. An Act providing for the development of a Data Center Planning and Community Engagement Toolkit and supplementing chapter 27D of Title 52 of the Revised Statutes. Be It Enacted by the Senate and General Assembly of the State of New Jersey: 1.    a.  As used in P.L.    , c.    (C.        ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill): "Large load data center" or "data center" means a facility that: (1) primarily stores, manages, and processes digital data; (2) is used to house computer and network systems, including associated components such as servers, network equipment and appliances, telecommunications, data storage systems, systems for monitoring and managing infrastructure performance, Internet-related equipment and services, data communications connections, environmental controls, fire protection systems, and security systems and services; and (3) is projected to have a peak electrical load of at least 20 megawatts. b.    Within 120 days of the effective date of P.L.    , c.    (C.        ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill), the Division of Local Planning Services in the Department of Community Affairs, in consultation with the Department of Environmental Protection and the New Jersey Economic Development Authority, shall develop and publish on the divisions Internet website a Data Center Planning and Community Engagement Toolkit for the purpose of providing comprehensive guidance and education to local governments and communities concerning the development of large load data centers.  The toolkit shall include, but not be limited to: (1)   model questions, information requests, and disclosure templates for developers of proposed large load data center projects; (2)   guidance for evaluating anticipated electricity demand, water use, wastewater management, backup electricity generation, air and noise pollution, traffic congestion, and public infrastructure impacts; (3)   model approaches for voluntary and enforceable community benefit agreements; (4)   best practices for early and accessible good-faith community engagement in the large load data center development; (5)   recommendations for planning, design, landscaping, buffering, and negative-impact mitigation practices; (6)   best practices for prioritizing the creation of quality jobs including advancing local workforce opportunities; and (7)   local, sustainable, and responsible procurement best practices for large load data center development and operation. c.     The Director of the Division of Local Planning Services may establish partnerships, pursue grants and philanthropic support, and engage nonprofit organizations, academic institutions, independent experts, and other executive agencies to provide further relevant information for the toolkit. 2.    This act shall take effect immediately. STATEMENT This bill directs the Division of Local Planning Services (DLPS) in the Department of Community Affairs, in consultation with the Department of Environmental Protection and the New Jersey Economic Development Authority, to develop and publish on the DLPS Internet website a Data Center Planning and Community Engagement Toolkit (toolkit) within 120 days of the effective date of the bill.  The toolkit is to provide comprehensive guidance and education to local governments and communities concerning the development of large load data centers, as defined in the bill. The toolkit is to include, but not be limited to: (1)   model questions, information requests, and disclosure templates for developers of proposed large load data center projects; (2)   guidance for evaluating anticipated electricity demand, water use, wastewater management, backup electricity generation, air and noise pollution, traffic congestion, and public infrastructure impacts; (3)   model approaches for voluntary and enforceable community benefit agreements; (4)   best practices for early and accessible good-faith community engagement in the large load data center development; (5)   recommendations for planning, design, landscaping, buffering, and negative-impact mitigation practices; (6)   best practices for prioritizing the creation of quality jobs including advancing local workforce opportunities; and (7)   local, sustainable, and responsible procurement best practices for large load data center development and operation. The bill further provides that the Director of DLPS may establish partnerships, pursue grants and philanthropic support, and engage nonprofit organizations, academic institutions, independent experts, and other executive agencies to provide further relevant information for the toolkit.
