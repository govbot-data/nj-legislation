SYNOPSIS

     Establishes advisory council for veteran suicide prevention; appropriates funds.  

 

CURRENT VERSION OF TEXT

     As introduced.

  


An Act concerning veterans and supplementing Title 26 of the Revised Statutes, and making an appropriation.

 

     Be It Enacted by the Senate and General Assembly of the State of New Jersey:

 

     1.    a. There is created in, but not of, the Department of Health the Veteran Suicide Prevention Advisory Council.  The advisory council shall advise the department on the rules and clinical infrastructure necessary to support veterans access to, and the training of providers for, breakthrough clinical and medication-assisted therapies for veteran suicide prevention.  The council shall advise the department on:

     (1)   developing the criteria and standards for the award of grants and fellowships administered through a veteran suicide prevention program established by the department;

     (2)   the award of grants for breakthrough therapy treatment through the veteran suicide prevention program;

     (3)   developing provisions to ensure justice, equity, diversity, and inclusion are considered in the administration of grants by the department, and ensure that such provisions are included in the councils recommendations made to the department;

     (4)   the development of public awareness and education campaigns related to veteran suicide;

     (5)   the necessary education, training, licensing, and credentialing of providers;

     (6)   patient safety and harm reduction;

     (7)   health care costs, insurance reimbursement, and strategies to safely increase affordable access to care, including the use of group therapy;

     (8)   standards for treatment facilities;

     (9)   relevant federal regulations and guidelines that may be adopted by any relevant State agency;

     (10)  any additional funding needed for subsidized patient access, and provider and therapist training;

     (11)  developing and providing oversight regarding mechanisms for the dissemination of treatment and training data; and

     (12)  the overall budget of the veteran suicide prevention program through submission of quarterly and annual budget reports.

     b.    The council shall comprise nine members.  Of the nine members, three members shall be appointed by the Governor, two members shall be appointed by the President of the Senate, two members shall be appointed by the Speaker of the General Assembly, one member shall be appointed by the Minority Leader of the Senate, and one member shall be appointed by the Minority Leader of the General Assembly.  At least three of the appointed members shall be veterans.  The membership of the council shall include members with expertise in breakthrough therapy research, clinical mental health treatment, public health, access to mental and behavioral health care in underserved communities, veteran mental and behavioral health care and harm reduction. 

     c.     The council shall organize as soon after the appointment of its members as practicable and shall elect a chairperson from the membership.  Vacancies shall be filled in the same manner as the original appointment but for the unexpired term only.  All members shall be appointed for two years and shall be eligible for reappointment.  The department shall appoint a secretary to the council who shall not be a member of the council.

     d.    The members of the council shall serve without compensation but shall be entitled to reasonable and necessary travel expenses, within the limits of any funds appropriated or otherwise made available for that purpose. 

     e.     The Department of Health shall provide such stenographic, clerical, and other administrative assistants, and such professional staff as the council may require to carry out its work.  The council shall also be entitled to call to its assistance and avail itself of the services of any employe of any State, country, or municipal department, board, bureau, commission or agency as it may require and as may be available to it for its purposes.

     f.     As used in this section, breakthrough therapy means a drug or therapy that has been designated as such by the federal Food and Drug Administration pursuant to the provisions of section 506 of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act, 21 U.S.C. s.356.

 

     2.    There is appropriated from the General Fund to the Department of Health such sums as are necessary for veteran suicide prevention to effectuate the purposes of this act.

 

     3.    This act shall take effect 90 days from the effective date of this act, except that the Commissioner of Health may take any anticipatory administrative action in advance as necessary to effectuate the purposes of this act.

 

 

STATEMENT

 

     This bill creates an advisory council in the Department of Health (department) for veteran suicide prevention.  The advisory council would be established in an effort to increase access to certain clinical trials and medication-assisted therapies for veterans.  The duties of the council include advising the department on:

     (1)   developing criteria and standards for the awards of grants and fellowships administered through a veteran suicide prevention program established by the department;

     (2)   the awarding of grants through the program;

     (3)   developing provisions to ensure justice, equity, diversity, and inclusions are considered in the administration of grants by the department, and ensuring such provisions are considered when the advisory council makes recommendations to the department;

     (4)   developing public awareness and education campaigns related to veteran suicide;

     (5)   the necessary education, training, licensing, and credentialing of providers;

     (6)   patient safety and harm reduction;

     (7)   health care costs, insurance reimbursement, and strategies to safely increase affordable access to care, including the use of group therapy;

     (8)   standards for treatment facilities;

     (9)   relevant federal regulations and guidelines that could be adopted by State agencies;

     (10)  any additional funding needed for subsidized patient access, and provider and therapist training;

     (11)  developing and providing oversight regarding the mechanisms used for disseminating treatment and training data;

     (12)  the overall budget of the veteran suicide prevention program through quarterly and annual budget reports.
