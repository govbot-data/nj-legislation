SYNOPSIS

     Regulates appointment of assistant State monitors in certain school districts; revises duties and establishes term limit of State monitor.

     2.    (New section)  a.        The Commissioner of Education may appoint additional staff, including one or more assistant State monitors, to support the State monitor appointed pursuant to section 2 of P.L.2006, c.15 (C.18A:7A-55).  If the commissioner determines it is necessary to appoint one or more assistant State monitors pursuant to this section, the commissioner shall:

     (1)   provide written notice to the board of education explaining the need for the appointment; and

     (2)   obtain approval from the board prior to appointing any additional assistant State monitor, which approval shall be signified by a recorded roll call majority vote of the boards full membership.

     b.    The commissioner shall notify the State Board of Education following the appointment of an assistant State monitor pursuant to subsection a. of this section.  The assistant State monitor shall report directly to the State monitor on a weekly basis.  The assistant State monitor shall assist with the State monitors monthly report pursuant to subsection c. of section 2 of P.L.2006, c.15 (C.18A:7A-55).

     c.     For purposes of the "New Jersey Tort Claims Act," N.J.S.59:1-1 et seq., an assistant State monitor appointed pursuant to this section shall be considered a State officer, but for all other purposes the assistant State monitor shall be considered an employee of the district.

     d.    An assistant State monitor shall serve a term of 24 months and assist with the implementation of the plan required pursuant to paragraph (3) of subsection b. of section 2 of P.L.2006, c.15 (C.18A:7A-55).  The school district shall then have 12 months to ensure that all remedial actions required under the plan have been implemented and the necessary local capacity and fiscal controls have been restored to the district without direct oversight by an appointed assistant State monitor.  At the end of the 12-month period, the commissioner shall determine whether it is necessary to reappoint an assistant State monitor.  If the commissioner determines it is necessary to reappoint an assistant State monitor, the commissioner shall provide the district with a written determination, which shall include a statement of reasons that supports the commissioners determination. After providing the requisite written determination, the commissioner may reappoint an assistant State monitor pursuant to this section.

     e.     The State shall assume the total cost of any assistant State monitor or any additional staff appointed pursuant to this section. 

 

     3. This act shall take effect immediately.

 

 

STATEMENT

 

     This bill regulates the appointment of assistant State monitors in certain school districts, revises the duties of the State monitor, and establishes term limits for both assistant State monitors and State monitors.  

     Under the provisions of the School District Fiscal Accountability Act, the Commissioner of Education has the authority to appoint a State monitor and additional staff, as necessary, to provide direct oversight of a board of educations business operations and personnel matters if certain requirements are met.  Pursuant to the authority to appoint additional staff, the commissioner has appointed assistant State monitors in certain circumstances to support the appointed State monitor.

     This bill clarifies that the commissioner may appoint any additional staff, including one or more assistant State monitors, to support the State monitor.  If the commissioner determines it is necessary to appoint one or more assistant State monitors, the commissioner is required to: (1) provide written notice to the board of education explaining the need for the appointment; and (2) obtain the approval of a roll call majority vote of the board prior to appointing any assistant State monitor.  The bill requires an assistant State monitor to report weekly to the State monitor and assist the State monitor with the State monitors monthly reporting requirement under current law.

     Under current law, the State monitor has the authority to override a chief school administrators action and a vote by the board of education on any matter related to the State monitor being appointed. This bill clarifies that this authority is not to be construed to permit the State monitor to recommend against, or otherwise interfere with, the educational placement decisions provided in a students individualized education program.

     Current law further provides that the State monitor is to provide oversight in the school district until the commissioner determines that all remedial actions required under the plan have been implemented and the necessary local capacity and fiscal controls have been restored to school district operations. 

     This bill amends current law to establish a term limit for both the State monitor and assistant State monitor.  The bill imposes a 24-month term on each monitor. The bill provides that the school district will then have 12 months to ensure that all remedial actions required under the plan have been implemented and the necessary local capacity and fiscal controls have been restored to the district without direct oversight by the State monitor or an appointed State monitor.  At the end of the 12-month period, the commissioner is required to determine whether it is necessary to reappoint a State monitor or assistant State monitor.  If the commissioner determines it is necessary to reappoint a State monitor or an assistant State monitor, the commissioner is required to provide the district with a written determination that includes a statement of reasons that supports the commissioners determination. After providing the requisite written determination, the commissioner is authorized to reappoint a State monitor or an assistant State monitor if the conditions required under current law continue to persist.

     Additionally, under current law, a school district is required to assume the salary and other costs associated with the State monitor and any additional staff appointed by the commissioner, including assistant State monitors.  This bill amends current law to require the State to bear the salary and costs associated with any additional staff, including assistant State monitors. A district is still required under the bill to pay the salary and costs associated with the State monitor.
