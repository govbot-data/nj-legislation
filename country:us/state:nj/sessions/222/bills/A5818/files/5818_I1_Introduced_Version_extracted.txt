A5818 ASSEMBLY, No. 5818 STATE OF NEW JERSEY 222nd LEGISLATURE INTRODUCED NOVEMBER 16, 2026 Sponsored by: Assemblyman  PAUL KANITRA District 10 (Monmouth and Ocean) Co-Sponsored by: Assemblymen Peterson, Scharfenberger, Assemblywoman Flynn, Assemblymen Simonsen and McGuckin SYNOPSIS Prohibits marriages and civil unions between first cousins. CURRENT VERSION OF TEXT As introduced. An Act concerning marriages and civil unions and amending R.S.37:1-1. Be It Enacted by the Senate and General Assembly of the State of New Jersey: 1.    R.S.37:1-1 is amended to read as follows: 37:1-1.  Marriages and civil unions. a.     (Deleted by amendment, P.L.2021, c.343) b.    (Deleted by amendment, P.L.2021, c.343) c.     Laws concerning marriage and civil union shall be read with gender- and race-neutral intent. d.    No person shall marry or enter into a civil union with any of the person's ancestors or descendants, or the person's sibling, or the child of the person's sibling, or the sibling of the person's parent, or the child of the sibling of the persons parent, whether such collateral kindred be of the whole or half blood. e.     A marriage or civil union in violation of subsection d. of this section shall be absolutely void. (cf: P.L.2023, c.154, s.3) 2.    R.S.37:1-8 is amended to read as follows: 37:1-8. Testimony under oath by applicants as to legality of proposed marriage or civil union; witnesses; perjury. A licensing officer shall, before issuing a marriage or civil union license, require the contracting parties or, in the case of persons who intend to be married or to enter into a civil union pursuant to section 7 of P.L.2011, c.179 (C.37:1-17.3), one of the contracting parties and the attorney-in-fact for the other party, to appear before him and subscribe and swear to an oath attesting the truth of the facts respecting the legality of the proposed marriage or civil union as set forth in the form supplied by the State registrar.  Said testimony shall be verified by a witness of legal age.  A licensing officer shall issue a license only if it is thus made to appear before him that no legal impediment to the marriage or civil union exists.  Every licensing officer may administer oaths to the contracting parties or, in the case of persons who intend to be married or to enter into a civil union pursuant to section 7 of P.L.2011, c.179 (C.37:1-17.3), to one of the contracting parties and to the attorney-in-fact for the other contracting party and their identifying witness. [Any] In addition to any other penalty provided by law, any identifying witness, applicant applying for a marriage or civil union license or attorney-in-fact who shall knowingly make false answers to any of the inquiries asked by the licensing officer shall be guilty of perjury pursuant to N.J.S.2C:28-1. (cf: P.L.2011, c.179, s.4) 3.    This act shall take effect immediately and shall apply to marriages and civil unions entered into on or after the date of enactment. STATEMENT This bill prohibits marriages and civil unions between first cousins. New Jersey law prohibits marriages involving minors who are under 18 years of age, as well as close blood relatives and those who are already married to another person. Specifically, current law prohibits marriages between ancestors and descendants (parents, grandparents, children, and grandchildren), siblings (brothers and sisters of whole or half blood), aunts, uncles, nieces, and nephews.  While the majority of states, approximately 30 of them, prohibit marriage between first cousins, New Jersey law does not prohibit such a marriage. Current law also provides that a person who knowingly makes a false statement to a licensing officer in order to obtain a marriage or civil union license is guilty of perjury pursuant to N.J.S.2C:28-1, a crime of the third degree.  A crime of the third degree is punishable by a term of imprisonment of three to five years, a fine of up to $15,000, or both. This bill prohibits a person from marrying or entering into a civil union with the child of the sibling of the persons parent (a persons first cousin). The provisions of the bill apply to marriages and civil unions which are entered into on or after the date of enactment. The bill also clarifies that, in addition to the crime of perjury, a person who knowingly makes false statements to a licensing officer is subject to any other penalty applicable under current law.
