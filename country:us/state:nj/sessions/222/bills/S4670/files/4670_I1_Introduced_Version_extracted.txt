additional 30 days. g.  In the case of a homeless youth from another state who is under the age of 18, a basic center shelter shall notify the Compact Administrator of the Interstate Compact on STATEMENT This bill expands services provided to homeless youth by street outreach, basic center shelter, and transitional living programs. Specifically, the bill amends P.L.1999, c.224 (C.9:12A-2 et seq.), which establishes street outreach, basic center shelter, and transitional living programs, to require such programs to:  (1) provide services that consider the age, gender, and social and developmental needs of homeless youth, and that are culturally and linguistically appropriate; (2) include services that provide assistance in completing and submitting a Free Application for Federal Student Aid or other financial aid form; and (3) offer trauma-informed services specific to youth who are survivors of human trafficking or sexual exploitation. The bill amends section 7 of P.L.1999, c.224 (C.9:12A-8) to require transitional living programs to provide residential care and treatment services to homeless youth between the ages of 16 to 25.  Current law requires these programs to provide services to youth between the ages of 16 and 21. The bill also amends section 7 of P.L.1999, c.224 (C.9:12A-8) to mandate that the programs are to decide what additional services to provide homeless youth between 22 and 25 years of age, which may include crisis intervention services, referrals to organizations and agencies for homelessness assistance services, and assistance in accessing other age appropriate support services as may be necessary to move the youth towards the goal of permanent affordable housing and self-sufficiency.  Under current law, transitional programs are only required to provide services as determined by contract and are not required to provide additional services to homeless youth between 22 and 25 years of age. The provisions of the bill require that every staff member employed by a street outreach, basic center shelter, or transitional living program complete, within the time frame established in the bill, a training program developed by the DCF, in consultation with the Commission of Human Trafficking, on the relationship between human trafficking, childhood trauma, and sexual exploitation and the prevalence of youth homelessness. The DCF is to conduct a periodic review of the training program to insure that the training includes information on relevant changes to State or federal law and other information pertinent to the delivery of services a staff member is required to provide to homeless youth.
