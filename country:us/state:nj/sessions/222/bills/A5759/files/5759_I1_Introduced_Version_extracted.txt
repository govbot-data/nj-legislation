A5759 ASSEMBLY, No. 5759 STATE OF NEW JERSEY 222nd LEGISLATURE INTRODUCED NOVEMBER 16, 2026 Sponsored by: Assemblywoman  ANNETTE QUIJANO District 20 (Union) SYNOPSIS Concerns licensure and regulation of last-mile facilities. CURRENT VERSION OF TEXT As introduced. An Act concerning licensure and regulation of last-mile facilities and supplementing Title 34 of the Revised Statutes. Be It Enacted by the Senate and General Assembly of the State of New Jersey: 1.    As used in this act: Commissioner means the Commissioner of Labor and Workforce Development. Consumer means a final retail customer in the State from whom a merchant received an offer to purchase a product. Core delivery services means the subset of core services that involves transporting and delivering packages or goods to consumers in the State in the normal course of business, subject to the exceptions in the definition of core services. Core services means core delivery services and core warehouse services.  Core services shall not include work performed by any person that facilitates, offers, or arranges for the delivery of goods solely by commercial motor vehicles over 26,001 pounds in gross vehicle weight rating required by federal law, rule, or regulation to be registered with the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration. Core warehouse services means the subset of core services that involves sorting and handling of packages within a last-mile facility in the State in the normal course of business, subject to the exceptions in the definition of core services. Department means the Department of Labor and Workforce Development. Egregious misconduct means misconduct that is so outrageous, dangerous, or illegal that a facility operator cannot reasonably expect to provide an employee notice of termination of employment prior to the employees termination.  Egregious misconduct may include, but is not limited to, depending on the circumstances: (1)   operating a vehicle recklessly or under the influence of alcohol or other intoxicating substance during work hours; (2)   violence or threats of violence; (3)   theft; (4)   sexual harassment; (5)   discrimination in violation of federal or State law; or (6)   willful destruction of property. Facility operator means any person, except a governmental entity, who owns, leases, manages or operates a last-mile facility and has control of core services. Governmental entity means the State or a county, municipality, or other political subdivision of the State, or any agency, authority, department, or other entity thereof, or the federal government. Last-mile facility means a warehouse, storage facility, or other location that receives goods as part of a delivery supply chain, and from which those goods are delivered either to the final destination to consumers, or to locations designated for the transfer of goods to sustainable modes of transport for final delivery to consumers, or both.  Last-mile facility shall not include retail businesses in which the majority of the premises are used for the purposes of the on-site sale of goods to consumers, or businesses that prepare meals for immediate consumption as their primary business. Licensee means any person holding a license under this act. Pattern or practice means three or more violations in any two-year period in the previous five years, or four or more violations in the previous five years, attributable to the licensee, or any corporate or legal parent or subsidiary thereof, where the subsidiary is dominated and controlled by the licensee. 2.    a.  (1) It shall be unlawful for any person to engage in business as a facility operator without a valid license for each place of business wherein a person operates a last-mile facility. (2)   It shall be unlawful for a person to permit any premises under a persons control to be used by any other person in violation of paragraph (1) of subsection a. of this section. b.    (1)  In order to obtain or renew a license to engage in business as a last-mile facility, a person shall file an application with the commissioner for a license for each place of business that the applicant desires to use for the operation of a last-mile facility.  The application for each license or renewal thereof shall be made upon a form as prescribed by the commissioner and shall contain such information as the commissioner shall require. (2)   An applicant for a license or renewal thereof under this section shall disclose to the commissioner any finding by a court of law, any final agency determination, or any ruling by an arbitrator that the applicant violated State or federal law relating to workplace safety, road or highway safety, environmental protection, unfair or deceptive trade practices, or workplace or worker protections, and any additional information that the commissioner deems appropriate. (3)   There shall be a fee of $500 for a licensee to engage in the business of a last-mile facility at each location where a last-mile facility operates in the State.  The term of a license shall be two years, after which period the licensee may apply for renewal. (4)   A license shall be issued or renewed to a person to conduct the business of a last-mile facility in the State only if: (a)   the applicant for a license or renewal thereof certifies that the applicant meets all the requirements of this act, and any additional criteria established by the commissioner by rule or regulation as deemed necessary to effectuate the purposes of this act; and (b)   the applicant satisfies the commissioner that the certification is true and correct, and that the applicant has not materially misrepresented or concealed any information in the license application, as determined by the commissioner. c.     A last-mile facility license shall not be assignable and shall be valid only for the person in whose name it is issued and for the transaction of business in the place designated therein and shall at all times be conspicuously displayed at the place for which it is issued. d.    An applicant may seek review of a determination by the commissioner to deny an application for a license or a renewal thereof under this section in accordance with the "Administrative Procedure Act," P.L.1968, c.410 (C.52:14B-1 et seq.), within 30 days of receiving written notice of the determination. 3.    a.  The commissioner may suspend or revoke a license to operate a last-mile facility, upon due notice and hearing, for the failure of a licensee to comply with any applicable provision of law or any rule duly promulgated by the commissioner. b.    A licensee may seek review of a determination by the commissioner that a license has been suspended or revoked in accordance with the "Administrative Procedure Act," P.L.1968, c.410 (C.52:14B-1 et seq.), within 30 days of receiving written notice of the determination. c.     The commissioner may deny any application for a license or renewal thereof, or revoke or suspend any license, if the applicant or licensee has engaged in a pattern or practice of violations of any State or federal law relating to workplace safety, road or highway safety, environmental protection, unfair or deceptive trade practices, or workplace or worker protections in connection with operating one or more last-mile facilities. d.    For the purposes of this section, violation means a finding by a court of law or a final agency determination that the applicant or licensee violated any State or federal law relating to workplace safety, road or highway safety, environmental protection, unfair or deceptive trade practices, or workplace or worker protections. e.     Upon a first instance of a finding of non-compliance with the requirements of this act, the commissioner may grant a licensee or applicant for renewal a period, not to exceed 90 days, to meet the requirements of this act prior to suspension, revocation, or denial of renewal of a license. 4.    a.  Except as otherwise provided in this section, any worker providing core warehouse services at a last-mile facility in the State shall be directly employed by the facility operator. b.    Except as otherwise provided in this section, any worker providing core delivery services for more than eight hours in any 30-day period shall be directly employed by the facility operator. c.     Except as otherwise provided in this section, contracting with any third party, including staffing agencies or other subcontractors, for the performance of core delivery services or core warehouse services, shall be prohibited. d.    In the event that any facility operator is required to terminate a contract in which a contracted worker engaged in core services is terminated by the subcontractor as a result of this act, the facility operator shall offer to rehire the terminated worker as an employee, to perform the same work the worker previously performed, before offering to hire any other worker as an employee.  An offer to rehire a terminated worker shall include a preservation of rights, privileges, and benefits under existing collective bargaining or other agreements and terms no lesser than those in effect at the time of the termination. e.     (1)  The commissioner shall grant an extension for a facility operator who contracts with a third-party agency to supply workers to provide core services otherwise covered by this act, where the contract was in effect prior to the date of enactment of this act and the contract binds the contracting parties to performance after the effective date of this section. (2)   No extension shall extend later than the earlier of: (a)   the end of the relevant contract term; or (b)   the last day of the 24th month next following enactment of this act. (3)   A facility operator seeking an extension under this subsection shall apply to the commissioner in a manner prescribed by the commissioner by rule or regulation. 5.    a. A facility operator that engages a contracted worker to perform core services shall: (1)   maintain a complete list of the identities of individuals engaged in core delivery services, along with the following information for each worker: name; contact information; proof of commercial vehicle insurance coverage; driving record, including a full list of any moving violations; and records of all routes taken while delivering packages from the last-mile facility; (2)   submit to the commissioner a certification that each driver has the name and contact information of their employer clearly and prominently displayed on their clothes and on their delivery vehicle.  The name of the employer shall be displayed more prominently than the name of any other non-employer entity; and (3)   for each delivery worker retained as a non-employee, the facility operator shall post bond in the amount of $500,000 to be held by the commissioner in a fund.  The fund shall be made available in the event any person injured or otherwise harmed in a road incident involving a covered delivery worker obtains a judgment against the delivery worker or the delivery workers employer. b.    It shall be an additional violation for any facility operator who engages a contracted worker to perform core services to fail to comply with the requirements of this section. c.     Notwithstanding any extension granted pursuant to subsection e. of section 4 of this act, this section shall take effect on the first day of the 12th month next following enactment. 6.    a.  Except where a termination is for egregious conduct, a facility operator under this act shall notify an employee of a termination of employment at least 30 days before the termination becomes effective, as well as upon the effective date of termination.  The notice shall include: (1)   a plain language description of the reasons, if any, for termination; (2)   the effective date of the termination; and (3)   all records relied upon to substantiate the termination. b.    A facility operator shall not discharge an employee or in any manner discriminate, retaliate, or take any adverse action against any employee or applicant for employment because the employee or applicant engaged in any conduct protected under this act, or protected under any other applicable labor or employment statute under State or federal law, except where prohibited by other applicable law. c.     Any employee who is discharged, threatened with discharge, demoted, suspended, retaliated against, subjected to an adverse action, or in any other manner discriminated against in the terms and conditions of their employment because the employee engaged in any protected conduct shall be entitled to reinstatement and reimbursement for lost wages and work benefits caused by those acts of the employer. d.    If a facility operator engages in any action prohibited by this section within 60 days of the protected activity specified in this section, there shall be a rebuttable presumption in favor of the employees claim. 7.    a.  The department shall establish a process to certify and designate at least one last-mile facility training organization. b.    In order to become certified as a designated last-mile facility training organization, the organization shall be a not-for-profit organization which meets requirements set forth by the department that shall include, but not be limited to, the following: (1)   experience providing job training and placement services within the State; (2)   utilization of interactive teaching strategies that engage across multiple literacy levels; and (3)   the provision of trainers and educators who are culturally competent and fluent in the language or languages that last-mile facility workers understand. c.     The last-mile facility training organization shall offer a last-mile facility training program that includes no less than six hours of training, including live and interactive instruction, on the following elements: (1)   last-mile facility worker rights and facility operator responsibilities under this act and any other provision of applicable law; (2)   how to drive a delivery vehicle safely; (3)   how to adhere to quota requirements and aggressive delivery routes while driving defensively; (4)   when and where to make delivery stops along trafficked roads; (5)   how to ensure the safety of pedestrians while making frequent stops; (6)   how to identify bicycle routes and protected bicycle lanes, and how to avoid making stops along bicycle routes and lanes in a way that might create a safety hazard for pedestrians, cyclists, or workers; (7)   best ergonomic practices for workers who handle, sort, and move packages; (8)   how to identify potential injuries, heat stress, or repetitive stress, and means to find assistance for physical therapy or other practices to mitigate injuries; and (9)   guidance on safety with regards to powered industrial trucks, automated and robotic vehicles, hazardous chemicals, and electrical hazards. d.    The last-mile facility training organization may coordinate with a last-mile facility to ensure that training content aligns, where appropriate, with the last-mile facilitys policies and procedures.  Ultimate discretion regarding training content shall remain with the last-mile facility training organization, subject to requirements set forth by the department. e.     A facility operator shall: (1)   ensure that any person providing core services receives training by a last-mile facility training organization within 92 days of the persons first day of employment, and once per year thereafter; (2)   contract, at the expense of the facility operator, with a certified last-mile facility training organization to conduct, no less than annually, a last-mile facility training program attended by each person providing core services operating out of the last-mile facility; and (3)   document compliance with the training requirement set forth in this section by completing and signing a form as required by the department to certify the dates that the training was conducted and the workers who attended. f.     Each facility operator shall retain records sufficient to demonstrate compliance with this section, including a copy of a valid form attesting to compliance with the training program requirements. 8.    Nothing in sections 4 through 7 of this act shall impair or impede any provision of a valid collective bargaining agreement with a bona fide labor organization, if the agreement contains express and specific waivers for some or all of the barred or required conduct, in exchange for valuable consideration. 9.    a.  The commissioner shall enforce the provisions of this act. b.    (1)  Any person alleging a violation of this act may file a complaint with the department within two years of the date the person knew or should have known of the alleged violation. (2)   The department shall investigate upon receipt of a complaint. (3)   The department may open an investigation on its own initiative. (4)   A person or entity under investigation shall, in accordance with applicable law, provide the department with information or evidence that the department requests pursuant to the investigation.  The department may attempt to resolve an investigation concerning a violation of this act through any action authorized by law.  Adjudicatory powers pursuant to this act may be exercised by the commissioner or in accordance with the "Administrative Procedure Act," P.L.1968, c.410 (C.52:14B-1 et seq.). (5)   The department shall keep the identity of any complainant confidential unless disclosure is necessary to resolve the investigation or is otherwise required by law.  The department shall, to the extent practicable, notify the complainant that the department will be disclosing the complainant's identity before disclosure. c.     The commissioner may promulgate rules and regulations, pursuant to the "Administrative Procedure Act," P.L.1968, c.410 (C.52:14B-1 et seq.), necessary to carry out the purposes of this act. 10.  In addition to any remedies otherwise available under applicable law, a worker whose rights under this act have been violated shall be entitled to the following relief: a.     all compensatory damages and other relief required to make the worker or former worker whole; b.    an order directing compliance with the requirements set forth in this act; c.     for each violation of subsection a. of section 6 of this act: (1) reinstatement; (2)   reimbursement of 30 days wages; and (3)   $500 for each day after the thirtieth day before termination that the facility operator fails to notify the worker of their termination; and d.    for each violation of subsections b. through d. of section 6 of this act: (1)   for each instance of unlawful retaliation not including discharge from employment, full compensation including wages and benefits lost, $500 and equitable relief as appropriate; and (2) for each instance of unlawful discharge from employment, full compensation including wages and benefits lost, $2,500 and equitable relief, including reinstatement, as appropriate. 11.  a. A facility operator shall be liable for a penalty of $1,000 per day for each violation of subsection a. of section 4 of this act. b.    A facility operator shall be liable for a penalty of $1,000 per day for each violation of each of paragraphs (1), (2), and (3) of subsection a. of section 5 of this act. 12.  a.  The commissioner may initiate in any court of competent jurisdiction any action or proceeding that may be appropriate or necessary to enforce the provisions of this act, including an action to secure a permanent injunction, enjoin any act or practice that constitutes a violation of this act, compel compliance with the provisions of this act, or obtain other relief as may be appropriate. b.    Any person alleging a violation of sections 4 through 6 of this act may bring a civil action, in accordance with applicable law, in any court of competent jurisdiction. (1)   The court may order compensatory, injunctive, and declaratory relief, including the remedies set forth in section 10 of this act, and reasonable attorney's fees and costs. (2)   A civil action under this subsection shall be commenced within two years of the date the person knew or should have known of the alleged violation. (3)   Any person filing a civil action shall simultaneously serve notice of the action and a copy of the complaint upon the department. Failure to serve a notice shall not adversely affect any person's cause of action. c.     A worker need not file a complaint with the department pursuant to subsection b. of section 9 of this act before bringing a civil action; however, no person shall file a civil action based on the same facts as a complaint filed with the department pursuant to subsection b. of section 9 of this act unless the complaint has been withdrawn or dismissed without prejudice to further action. d.    The commencement or pendency of a civil action by a worker shall not preclude the department from investigating a facility operator or commencing, prosecuting, or settling a case against a facility operator based on some or all of the same violations. 13.  This act shall take effect 120 days following enactment, except that sections 4 and 5 of this act shall take effect on the first day of the 12th month following enactment. STATEMENT This bill concerns the licensure and regulation of last-mile facilities. The bill requires operators of last-mile facilities to obtain licensure from the Department of Labor and Workforce Development.  The bill establishes requirements for the issuance, renewal, suspension, and revocation of a license.  The bill defines a last-mile facility as a warehouse, storage facility, or other location that receives goods as part of a delivery supply chain, and from which those goods are delivered either to the final destination to consumers, or to locations designated for the transfer of goods to sustainable modes of transport for final delivery to consumers, or both.  The term does not include retail businesses in which the majority of the premises are used for the purposes of the on-site sale of goods to consumers or businesses that prepare meals for immediate consumption as their primary business. The bill requires that any worker providing core warehouse services at a last-mile facility in the State be directly employed by the facility operator.  Further, the bill requires that any worker providing core delivery services for more than eight hours in any 30-day period be directly employed by the facility operator.  Contracting with any third party, including staffing agencies or other subcontractors, for the performance of core services is prohibited under the bill.  The bill provides for an extension for certain facility operators that entered into a contract with a third-party agency prior to the date of enactment of the bill if the contract binds the contracting parties to performance after the effective date of the direct employment requirements. The bill also requires facility operators that engage contracted workers to perform core services to comply with certain recordkeeping and safety requirements.  Among other requirements, a facility operator is required to maintain certain information concerning workers engaged in core delivery services, submit certain driver information to the Commissioner of Labor and Workforce Development, and post a bond in the amount of $500,000 for each delivery worker retained as a non-employee.  The bond is to be held by the commissioner in a fund and made available in the event that a person injured or otherwise harmed in a road incident involving a covered delivery worker obtains a judgment against the delivery worker or the delivery workers employer. Except where a termination is for egregious conduct, facility operators are required to notify employees of a termination of their employment at least 30 days before the termination becomes effective and upon the effective date of termination.  The bill specifies the information to be included in the notice and establishes protections against retaliation for employees and applicants for employment. The bill requires the department to establish a process to certify and designate at least one last-mile facility training organization, which will be a not-for-profit organization that meets requirements set forth by the department and under the bill.  Facility operators are required to ensure that persons providing core services receive the training required by the bill. The bill authorizes the commissioner to investigate and enforce violations of the bill and provides certain remedies for workers whose rights under the bill are violated.  The bill also establishes civil penalties for certain violations and authorizes the commissioner to initiate an action or proceeding to enforce the provisions of the bill. This bill mirrors legislation proposed by New York City requiring the licensing of last-mile facilities.
