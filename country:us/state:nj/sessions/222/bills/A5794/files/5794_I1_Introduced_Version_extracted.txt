STATEMENT This bill eliminates the statute of limitations for criminal prosecutions and civil actions for certain human trafficking and sex offenses. Currently, under the provisions of N.J.S.A.2C:1-6 there is no statute of limitations for prosecutions for the following crimes: murder; manslaughter; sexual assault; and criminal offenses arising from violations of certain environmental statutes concerning widespread injury or damage. Prosecution for other crimes, such as human trafficking, must be commenced within five years, except for certain crimes enumerated in the statute such as: (1) bribery and official misconduct offenses which must be commenced within seven years; and (2) criminal sexual contact or endangering the welfare of a minor which must be commenced within five years after the victim attains the age of 18 or two years after discovery, whichever is later. Prosecutions for disorderly persons offenses must be commenced within one year after they are committed. Pursuant to N.J.S.A.2C:1-6, a civil action based on a violation of criminal law generally must be commenced within five years after the action accrues. By eliminating the statute of limitations for human trafficking crimes, criminal sexual contact, and endangering the welfare of a child, and civil actions related to these crimes, the prosecution or a civil action related to these crimes may be commenced at any time rather than within the time limits established under current law. Federal legislation, H.R.5560, was introduced in September of 2025.  H.R.5560 authorizes the Children's Bureau's Office of Child Abuse and Neglect in the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services to award grants to states that have (1) eliminated their criminal and civil statutes of limitations for child sexual abuse, and (2) revived previously time-barred civil claims.  If the federal legislation is passed, the enactment of this bill would maximize the amount of grant funding that New Jersey would receive from the Childrens Bureaus Office of Child Abuse and Neglect.
