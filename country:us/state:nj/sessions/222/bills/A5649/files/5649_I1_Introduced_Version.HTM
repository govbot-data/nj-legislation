SYNOPSIS

     Prohibits law enforcement officers, first responders, CDL drivers, or persons who operate heavy equipment from engaging in recreational use of cannabis items.

 

CURRENT VERSION OF TEXT

     As introduced.

  


An Act concerning use of cannabis by certain employees, amending various parts of the law, and supplementing Title 24 of the Revised Statutes.

 

     Be It Enacted by the Senate and General Assembly of the State of New Jersey:

 

     1.    (New Section)  a.  For the purposes of this section:

     CDL driver means a person who has been issued a commercial driver license (CDL) as that term is defined in section 3 of P.L.1990, c.103 (C.39:3-10.11) and is employed to drive a commercial motor vehicle as that term is defined in section 3 of P.L.1990, c.103 (C.39:3-10.11).

     Employer means an entity that employs law enforcement officers, first responders, CDL drivers, or persons who operate heavy equipment.

     First responder means a law enforcement officer; paid firefighter; paid member of a duly incorporated first aid, emergency, ambulance, or rescue squad association; paid emergency medical technician; paid paramedic; or paid 9-1-1 first responder dispatcher.

     Heavy equipment means machinery that is self-propelled, self-powered, or pulleyed and weighs 5,000 pounds or more, including but not limited to, road-building machines, construction equipment, or apparatus of unusual size or weight that require special permits for transportation on public roads; or motor vehicle that requires a commercial drivers license to operate.

     Person who operates heavy equipment means any person who operates, navigates, or is in actual physical control of any mobile or stationary heavy equipment.

     Law enforcement officer means a person employed as a permanent full-time member of any State, county or municipal law enforcement agency, department, or division of those governments who is statutorily empowered to act for the detection, investigation, arrest, conviction, detention, or rehabilitation of persons violating the criminal laws of this State or of the United States and statutorily required to successfully complete a training course approved by the Police Training Commission pursuant to P.L.1961, c.56 (C.52:17B-66 et seq.), or certified by the commission as being substantially equivalent to an approved course.

     b.    No person employed as a law enforcement officer, as a first responder, as a CDL driver, or to operate heavy equipment shall engage in the use of a cannabis item, as otherwise permitted under P.L.2021, c.16 (C.24:6I-31 et al.), at any time during the course of the persons employment whether on or off duty or otherwise engaged in work responsibilities. An employer may take the following actions to ensure compliance:

     (1)   An employer may discharge from employment or take any punitive action against an employee who is a law enforcement officer, first responder, CDL driver, or a person who operates heavy equipment with respect to compensation, terms, conditions, or other privileges of employment if that person uses cannabis items, as otherwise permitted under P.L.2021, c.16 (C.24:6I-31 et seq.), while employed by the employer. An employee who is a law enforcement officer, first responder, CDL driver, or a person who operates heavy equipment may be subject to adverse action by an employer solely due to the presence of cannabinoid metabolites in the employees bodily fluid from engaging in conduct otherwise permitted under P.L.2021, c.16 (C.24:6I-31 et al.).

     (2) An employer may conduct random drug tests of employees to ensure that employees restricted from cannabis use are in compliance with the provisions of this act.  The drug test shall include scientifically reliable objective testing methods and procedures, such as testing of blood, urine, or saliva, in order to determine whether an employee who is a law enforcement officer, first responder, CDL driver, or a person who operates heavy equipment engaged in the use of cannabis items.

     (3)   An employer may refuse to hire a prospective law enforcement officer, first responder, CDL driver, or a person who operates heavy equipment due to use of cannabis items, as otherwise permitted under P.L.2021, c.16 (C.24:6I-31 et al.), and may employ the use of drug testing in accordance with subsection b. of this section as part of pre-employment screening.

     c.     Nothing in this section shall be construed to apply to employees who do not meet the definition of CDL driver, law enforcement officer, first responder, or a person who operates heavy equipment as set forth in this section, or to limit any of the provisions set forth in subsection b. of section 48 of P.L.2021, c.16 (C.24:6I-52).

STATEMENT

 

     This bill prohibits law enforcement officers, first responders, CDL drivers, or persons who operate heavy equipment from using cannabis items under the recreational use statute.

     Under current law, employers are prohibited from refusing to hire a prospective employee or taking adverse action against a current employee, including discharge from employment, because the person does or does not use cannabis items.  Employers are permitted to drug test employees based on a reasonable suspicion of an employees use of a cannabis item while engaged in the performance of the employees work responsibilities or upon finding observable signs of intoxication. However, an employer may not require an employee to abstain from recreational usage while off-duty.

     Under the bill, law enforcement officers, first responders, CDL drivers, or persons who operate heavy equipment are prohibited from using cannabis items on or off-duty.  The bill provides that employers of law enforcement officers, first responders, CDL drivers, or persons who operate heavy equipment may discharge from employment or take any adverse action against an employee who is a law enforcement officer, first responder, CDL driver, or a person who operates heavy equipment with respect to compensation, terms, conditions, or other privileges of employment if that person uses cannabis items.  Additionally, the bill provides that a law enforcement officer, first responder, CDL driver, or a person who operates heavy equipment may be subject to adverse action by an employer solely due to the presence of cannabinoid metabolites in the employees bodily fluid from engaging in conduct otherwise permitted under the recreational use statute.

     Additionally, the bill permits employers to conduct random drug tests to ensure compliance with the prohibition.  The drug test is required to include scientifically reliable objective testing methods and procedures, such as testing of blood, urine, or saliva, in order to determine whether the employee has engaged in the use of cannabis items.

     The bill also permits an employer to refuse to hire a prospective law enforcement officer, first responder, or a person who operates heavy equipment due to use of cannabis items, as otherwise permitted under the recreational use statute and employ the use of drug testing as part of pre-employment screening. 

     The bill provides for definitions of law enforcement officer, first responder, CDL driver, and person who operates heavy equipment.

     Lastly, the bill amends current law to prohibit 1) the operation of a commercial motor vehicle while using cannabis items, and 2) use of cannabis items in a school bus or other form of public transportation, while the vehicle is not in operation.
