SYNOPSIS

     Requires disclosure of all fees and charges for goods and services sold or offered for sale.

 

CURRENT VERSION OF TEXT

     As introduced.

  


An Act concerning price disclosures and supplementing Title 56 of the Revised Statutes.

 

     Be It Enacted by the Senate and General Assembly of the State of New Jersey:

 

     1.    As used in this act:

     Delivery platform means a transportation network company, as defined pursuant to section 2 of P.L.2017, c.26 (C.39:5H-2), and any other website, mobile application, or digital platform that facilitates, arranges, or enables the purchase or delivery of a good or service from a person to a consumer.

     Mandatory fee or surcharge means, but is not limited to, a fee or surcharge that is: (1) to be paid in order to purchase a good or service that is advertised; (2) not reasonably avoidable by the consumer; or (3) one a reasonable person would expect to be included in the purchase of the good or service being advertised. Mandatory fee or surcharge shall not include taxes imposed by a government entity on the sale, use, receipt, or delivery of the goods or services that are advertised.

 

     2.    a.  (1) It shall be considered unlawful and a deceptive practice for a person, in the course of business, to advertise, display, or offer for sale a good or service that does not include all mandatory fees or surcharges.

     (2)   If a contract is required to complete the purchase of goods or services, it shall include, in a clear and conspicuous manner, the mandatory fees and surcharges that are part of the total cost for the good or service.

     b.    A person who charges a postage or shipping fee to be incurred by a consumer who purchased a good from the person that is to be mailed or otherwise shipped shall disclose the fee to the consumer.

     c.     Nothing in this section prevents a person from offering goods or services at a discounted price from the advertised, displayed, or offered price.

 

     3.    a.  A delivery platform shall be deemed to be in compliance with this act if:

     (1)   at the point when a consumer views and selects a vendor or items for purchase, the delivery platform displays in a clear and conspicuous manner that an additional flat fee or percentage is charged.  The disclosure shall include the fee or percentage amount; and

     (2)   after a consumer selects items for purchase, but prior to checkout, a delivery platform shall display a subtotal page that itemizes the price of the items and the additional fee that is included in the total price.

     b.    A person offering goods or services in an auction where consumers can place bids on the goods or services and the total costs are indeterminable shall be in compliance with this act if the person discloses, in a clear and conspicuous manner, any mandatory fees associated with the transaction and gives notice that the total cost of the goods or services may vary.

     c.     A person offering services where the total cost of a service is determined by consumer selections and preferences, or where the total cost of the service relates to distance or time, shall be in compliance with this act if the person discloses in a clear and conspicuous manner:

     (1)  the factors that determine the total price;

     (2)  any mandatory fees associated with the transaction; and

     (3)  that the total costs of the services may vary.

     d.    A food or beverage service establishment, including a hotel, shall be in compliance with this act if, in every offer or advertisement for the purchase of a good or service that includes pricing information, the total price of the good or service being offered or advertised includes a clear and conspicuous disclosure of the percentage of any automatic and mandatory gratuities charged.

     e.     A person shall be in compliance with this act:

     (1)  if the person provides broadband Internet access service on its own or as part of a bundle and is compliant with the broadband consumer label requirements under 47 C.F.R. s.8.1(a); or

     (2) is compliant with the pricing requirements under 47 U.S.C. s.552.

 

     4.    A violation of this act shall be deemed a violation of the consumer fraud act, P.L.1960, c.39 (C.56:8-1 et seq.).       

 

     5.    This act shall take effect on the first day of the 13th month next following the date of enactment.

 

 

STATEMENT

 

     This bill makes it an unlawful and deceptive practice for a person, in the course of business, to advertise, display, or offer for sale a good or service that does not include all mandatory fees or surcharges.  If a contract is required to complete the purchase of goods or services, it is to include, in a clear and conspicuous manner, the mandatory fees and surcharges that are part of the total cost for the good or service.  Mandatory fee or surcharge is defined in the bill to mean, but is not limited to, a fee or surcharge that is: (1) to be paid in order to purchase a good or service that is advertised; (2) not reasonably avoidable by the consumer; or (3) one a reasonable person would expect to be included in the purchase of the goods or services being advertised.  The definition excludes taxes imposed by a government entity on the sale, use, receipt, or delivery of the goods or services that are advertised.

     Under the bill, a person who charges a postage or shipping fee to be incurred by a consumer who has purchased a good from the person that is to be mailed or otherwise shipped is to disclose the fee to the consumer.  Additionally, the bill does not prevent a person from offering goods or services at a discounted price from the advertised, displayed, or offered price.

     The bill delineates how delivery platforms; persons selling goods or services at auction; persons offering services where the total cost of a service is determined by consumer selections and preferences; and food or beverage service establishment, including a hotel, can be in compliance with the bills provisions.  The bill also stipulates that a person is in compliance with its provisions: (1) if the person provides broadband Internet access service on its own or as part of a bundle and is compliant with the broadband consumer label requirements under 47 C.F.R. s.8.1(a); or (2) is compliant with the pricing requirements under 47 U.S.C. s.552.

     A violation of the bill is an unlawful practice under the consumer fraud act.  An unlawful practice is punishable by a monetary penalty of not more than $10,000 for a first offense and not more than $20,000 for any subsequent offense.  In addition, a violation can result in cease and desist orders issued by the Attorney General, the assessment of punitive damages, and the awarding of treble damages and costs to the injured party.
