S4845 SENATE, No. 4845 STATE OF NEW JERSEY 222nd LEGISLATURE INTRODUCED OCTOBER 19, 2026 Sponsored by: Senator  BRIAN P. STACK District 33 (Hudson) Senator  M. TERESA RUIZ District 29 (Essex and Hudson) Co-Sponsored by: Senator McKnight SYNOPSIS Establishes Social Housing Program; establishes Workforce Housing Investment Program; establishes Human Infrastructure Bank Fund in NJ Infrastructure Bank. CURRENT VERSION OF TEXT As introduced. An Act concerning the financing and development of certain housing projects, and supplementing and amending various parts of the statutory law. Be It Enacted by the Senate and General Assembly of the State of New Jersey: 1.    (New section) As used in P.L.    , c.    (C.        ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill): Affordable housing means low-income housing and moderate-income housing. Agency means the New Jersey Housing and Mortgage Finance Agency established pursuant to section 4 of P.L.1983, c.530 (C.55:14K-4). Authority means the New Jersey Economic Development Authority established by section 4 of P.L.1974, c.80 (C.34:1B-4). Developer means a person, including a local government redeveloper, that proposes to complete or operate a social housing project or a workforce housing project. Director means the Director of the Division of Taxation in the Department of the Treasury. Division means the Division of Investment in the Department of the Treasury. Eligibility period means the period not to exceed 10 years, as specified in an incentive award agreement, during which a local government redeveloper may claim a tax credit under the Social Housing Program, as such period shall be determined by the agency pursuant to section 6 of P.L.   , c.    (C.       ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill), provided that a local government redeveloper may elect a period not to exceed five years for a project located in a government-restricted municipality.  A local government redeveloper of a social housing project that has a project labor agreement may elect a period not to exceed five years, provided that if the project is located in a government-restricted municipality the developer may elect a period not to exceed three years. Enhanced area means (1) a municipality that contains an urban transit hub, as defined in section 2 of P.L.2007, c.346 (C.34:1B-208); (2) the five municipalities with the highest poverty rates according to the 2017 Municipal Revitalization Index; and (3) the three municipalities with the highest percentage of SNAP recipients according to the 2017 Municipal Revitalization Index. Environmental remediation costs means any costs incurred by a developer in the completion of any actions necessary to investigate, clean up, or respond to a known, suspected, or threatened discharge of contaminants, including, as necessary, the preliminary assessment, site investigation, remedial investigation, and remedial action, pursuant to sections 23 through 43 and section 45 of P.L.1993, c.139 (C.58:10B-1 et seq.). Government-restricted municipality means a municipality in this State with a municipal revitalization index distress score of at least 75, that met the criteria for designation as an urban aid municipality in the 2019 State fiscal year, and that, on the effective date of P.L.    , c.    (C.        ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill), is subject to financial restrictions imposed pursuant to the "Municipal Stabilization and Recovery Act," P.L.2016, c.4 (C.52:27BBBB-1 et seq.), or is restricted in its ability to levy property taxes on property in that municipality as a result of the State of New Jersey owning or controlling property representing at least 25 percent of the total land area of the municipality or as a result of the federal government of the United States owning or controlling at least 50 acres of the total land area of the municipality, which is dedicated as a national natural landmark.  The term "government-restricted municipality" also includes any municipality that: has a population greater than 50,000 and less than 60,000 according to the latest federal decennial census, is designated as the county seat of a county of the second class with a population greater than 800,000 according to the latest federal decennial census, and has an MRI distress score of 62.1; has a population greater than 70,000 and less than 100,000 according to the latest federal decennial census, is designated as the county seat of a county of the second class with a population greater than 515,000 and less than 525,000 according to the latest federal decennial census, and has an MRI distress score of 100; or contains the intersection of Interstate 280 and the Garden State Parkway, and corresponding land areas occupied by such highways under the ownership or control of the federal government of the United States or of this State within its municipal boundary, and has an MRI distress score of 55.5. Incentive award means an award of tax credits to reimburse a local government redeveloper for all or a portion of the project financing gap of a social housing project pursuant to the provisions of sections 2 through 12 of P.L.    , c.     (C.         through ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill). Incentive award agreement means the contract executed between a local government redeveloper and the agency pursuant to section 6 of P.L.    , c.      (C.         ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill), which sets forth the terms and conditions under which the developer may receive the incentive awards authorized pursuant to the provisions of sections 2 through 12 of P.L.    , c.     (C. through         ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill). Local government redeveloper means a county, a county improvement authority designated pursuant to the county improvement authorities law, P.L.1960, c.183 (C.40:37A-44 et seq.), a municipality, a housing authority as defined in the Local Redevelopment and Housing Law, P.L.1992, c.79 (C.40A:12A-1 et seq.), or any entity designated by a county or municipality that enters or proposes to enter into an incentive award agreement pursuant to the provisions of section 6 of P.L.    , c. (C.         ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill) for the completion and operation of a social housing project. Low-income housing means housing affordable according to the federal Department of Housing and Urban Development, or other recognized standards for home ownership and rental costs, and occupied or reserved for occupancy by households with a gross household income equal to 50 percent or less of the median gross household income for households of the same size within the housing region in which the housing is located. Minimum environmental and sustainability standards means standards established by the agency in accordance with the green building manual prepared by the Commissioner of Community Affairs pursuant to section 1 of P.L.2007, c.132 (C.52:27D-130.6), regarding the use of renewable energy, energy-efficient technology, and non-renewable resources to reduce environmental degradation and encourage long-term cost reduction. Moderate-income housing means housing affordable according to the federal Department of Housing and Urban Development, or other recognized standards for home ownership and rental costs, and occupied or reserved for occupancy by households with a gross household income equal to more than 50 percent, but less than 80 percent, of the median gross household income for households of the same size within the housing region in which the housing is located. MRI distress score means a municipal revitalization index distress score, as documented in the 2023 Municipal Revitalization Index developed by the Department of Community Affairs. Municipal Revitalization Index means the index by the Department of Community Affairs ranking New Jersey's municipalities according to eight separate indicators that measure diverse aspects of social, economic, physical, and fiscal conditions in each locality. Program means the Social Housing Program, established pursuant to section 2 of P.L.    , c. (C.        ) ( pending before the Legislature as this bill). "Project cost" or "eligible project cost" means the costs incurred in connection with a redevelopment project by a developer until the issuance of a permanent certificate of occupancy, or until such other time specified by the agency, for a specific investment or improvement, including the costs relating to lands, except the cost of acquiring such lands, buildings, improvements, real or personal property, or any interest therein, including leases discounted to present value, including lands under water, riparian rights, space rights, and air rights acquired, owned, developed or redeveloped, constructed, reconstructed, rehabilitated, or improved, any environmental remediation costs, plus costs not directly related to construction, including capitalized interest paid to third parties, of an amount not to exceed 20 percent of the total costs and the cost of infrastructure improvements, including ancillary infrastructure projects.  In addition to the foregoing, the term "project cost" shall include, for a project located in a government-restricted municipality, land costs in an amount not to exceed 20 percent of the eligible project cost. The fees associated with the application or administration of a tax credit under sections 2 through 12 of P.L.    , c.     (C.        through C.        ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill) shall not constitute a project cost. Project financing gap means the part of the total project cost, including reasonable and appropriate return on investment, that remains to be financed after all other sources of capital have been accounted for, including, but not limited to, developer-contributed capital, which shall not be less than 20 percent of the total project cost, and investor or financial entity capital or loans for which the developer, after making all good faith efforts to raise additional capital, certifies that additional capital cannot be raised from other sources on a non-recourse basis, provided, however, that for a project located in a government-restricted municipality, the local government redeveloper contributed capital shall not be less than 10 percent of the total project cost.  Developer-contributed capital may consist of cash, deferred development fees, costs for project feasibility incurred within the 12 months prior to application, property value less any mortgages when the local government redeveloper owns the project site, and any other investment by the developer in the project deemed acceptable by the agency, as provided by regulations promulgated by the agency.  Property value shall be valued at the lesser of: (1) the purchase price, provided the property was purchased pursuant to an arm's-length transaction within 12 months of application; or (2) the value as determined by a current appraisal. Project labor agreement means a form of pre-hire collective bargaining agreement covering terms and conditions of a specific project that satisfies the requirements set forth in section 5 of P.L.2002, c.44 (C.52:38-5). Qualified incentive tract means a population census tract having a poverty rate of 20 percent or more or a census tract in which the median family income for the census tract does not exceed 80 percent of the greater of the Statewide median family income or the median family income of the metropolitan statistical area in which the census tract is situated. Reasonable and appropriate return on investment means the discount rate at which the present value of the future cash flows of an investment equals the cost of the investment.  In determining the "reasonable and appropriate return on investment," an investment shall not include any federal, State, or local tax credits.  For a project that utilizes federal low-income housing tax credits awarded by the agency, the "reasonable and appropriate return on investment" shall be based on the approval of deferred developer fees pursuant to the rules established by the agency.  In the event that a project which utilizes federal low-income housing tax credits awarded by the agency generates returns on equity other than federal or local grants or proceeds from the sale of federal or local tax credits, the reasonable and appropriate return on investment shall be based on both the discount rate at which the present value of the future cash flows of an investment equal the cost of the investment for the entire project, and when evaluating only the units financed with federal low-income housing tax credits awarded by the agency, the approval of deferred developer fees pursuant to the rules established by the agency. Registered apprenticeship program means a plan containing all the terms and conditions for the qualification, recruitment, selection, employment, and training of apprentices, as required under Part 29 and Part 30 of Title 49 of the Code of Federal Regulations. Residential project means a specific construction project or improvement undertaken by a developer and any ancillary infrastructure project, that is residential in nature, intended for multi-family residency, and may include a parking component.  A residential project may involve construction or improvement upon lands, buildings, improvements, or real and personal property, or any interest therein, including lands under water, riparian rights, space rights, and air rights, acquired, owned, developed or redeveloped, constructed, reconstructed, rehabilitated, or improved. Social housing project means a residential project that is undertaken and operated by a local government redeveloper, and which: is located on land owned by the local government redeveloper within an enhanced area; is financed, in part, by general obligation bonds issued by the local government redeveloper, or issued on behalf of the local government redeveloper by a county improvement authority established pursuant to P.L.1960, c.183 (C.40:37A-44 et seq.), for not less than 20 percent of the project costs; and contains no less than 300 residential units.  A social housing project may include, but is not limited to, a single building, multiple contiguous buildings, or multiple buildings located on contiguous parcels of land or on parcels of land no farther apart than one-quarter of a mile, which project can be completed in phases over a period not to exceed six years commencing from the date of the incentive award agreement. Total project cost means the total amount of money that is required to complete the workforce housing project, as certified and approved by the agency, which amount may be audited at any time by the New Jersey Housing and Mortgage Finance Agency. Workforce housing means housing that is affordable, according to federal Department of Housing and Urban Development or other recognized standards, for home ownership and rental costs and that is occupied or reserved for occupancy by households with a gross household income not more than 120 percent of the median gross household income for households of the same size within the housing region in which the housing is located. Workforce housing project means a specific construction project or improvement undertaken by a developer, which project is located in the State and predominantly includes the construction or improvement of residential housing units, and may include a parking component, provided that at least 20 percent of the residential units in the project are reserved for workforce housing. 2.    (New section) a.  The Social Housing Program is hereby established as a program under the jurisdiction of the New Jersey Housing and Mortgage Finance Agency.  The agency shall administer the program to encourage social housing projects through the provision of incentive awards to reimburse local government redevelopers for certain project costs.  The agency may approve the award of an incentive award to a local government redeveloper upon application to the agency pursuant to sections 4 and 5 of P.L.    , c.     (C.        and C.        ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill). b.    The agency shall adopt rules and regulations, pursuant to section 12 of P.L.    , c.     (C.        ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill), concerning the establishment and administration of the affordability controls that shall apply to the residential units constructed for occupancy as low-income housing, moderate-income housing, and workforce housing under the program, including, but not limited to, residential units within residential projects that utilize federal low-income housing tax credits awarded by the agency.  Notwithstanding any provision of law or regulation to the contrary, the affordability controls shall, at a minimum, be consistent with the affordability controls established in the rules and regulations adopted pursuant to the "Fair Housing Act," P.L.1985, c.222 (C.52:27D-301 et al.), including, but not limited to, any requirements concerning the bedroom distributions, affordability averages, affirmative marketing, and long-term deed restrictions of residential units constructed for occupancy by low- and moderate-income households. 3.    (New section) a. A local government redeveloper shall be eligible to receive an incentive award for a social housing project only if the local government redeveloper demonstrates to the agency at the time of application that: (1)  without the incentive award, the social housing project is not economically feasible; (2)  a project financing gap exists, or the agency determines that the social housing project will generate a below market rate of return; (3)  the social housing project is located in an enhanced area; (4)  except for demolition and site remediation activities, the local government redeveloper has not commenced any construction at the site of the social housing project prior to submitting an application, unless the agency determines that the social housing project would not be completed otherwise; (5)  the social housing project shall comply with minimum environmental and sustainability standards; (6)  the social housing project shall comply with any applicable State affirmative action requirements; (7)  during the eligibility period, each worker employed to perform construction work at the social housing project shall be paid not less than the prevailing wage rate for the worker's craft or trade, as determined by the Commissioner of Labor and Workforce Development pursuant to P.L.1963, c.150 (C.34:11-56.25 et seq.) and P.L.2005, c.379 (C.34:11-56.58 et seq.); and (8)  the social housing project shall be completed, and the local government redeveloper shall be issued a certificate of occupancy for the social housing project facilities by the applicable enforcing agency, within four years of executing the incentive award agreement. b.    In addition to the requirements set forth in subsection a. of this section, for a social housing project to qualify for an incentive award, the project shall: (1) have a total project cost of at least $17,500,000 if the project is located in a municipality with a population greater than 200,000 according to the latest federal decennial census; (2) have a total project cost of at least $10,000,000 if the project is located in a municipality with a population less than 200,000 according to the latest federal decennial census; or (3) have a total project cost of at least $5,000,000 if the project is in a qualified incentive tract or government-restricted municipality. c.    (1)  In addition to the requirements set forth in subsections a. and b. of this section, the local government redeveloper shall permanently reserve at least 20 percent of the residential units constructed for workforce housing, and shall permanently reserve at least 20 percent of residential units constructed for low- and moderate-income housing, subject to the affordability controls adopted by the agency pursuant to subsection b. of section 2 of P.L.    , c.    (C.        ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill). (2)  In addition to the requirements set forth in subsections a. and b. of this section, a local government redeveloper shall certify that upon completion of a social housing project if, during any year, the local government redeveloper receives rental payments from tenants of the project in excess of the amounts necessary to defray the operating costs of the project, together with any debt service on bonds issued to finance the project, not less than 75 percent of the excess payments shall be deposited into a non-lapsing fund maintained by the local government redeveloper and used exclusively for the maintenance or additional production of social housing projects. d.    Prior to considering an application submitted by a local government redeveloper, the agency shall confirm with the Department of Labor and Workforce Development, the Department of Environmental Protection, and the Department of the Treasury whether the local government redeveloper is in substantial good standing with the respective department or has entered into an agreement with the respective department that includes a practical corrective action plan for the local government redeveloper.  The local government redeveloper shall certify that any contractors or subcontractors that will perform work at the redevelopment project: (1) are registered as required by "The Public Works Contractor Registration Act," P.L.1999, c.238 (C.34:11-56.48 et seq.); (2) have not been debarred by the Department of Labor and Workforce Development from engaging in or bidding on Public Works Contracts in the State; and (3) possess a tax clearance certificate issued by the Division of Taxation in the Department of the Treasury.  The agency may also contract with an independent third party to perform a background check on the local government redeveloper. 4.    (New section) a. A local government redeveloper seeking an incentive award for social housing projects eligible pursuant to section 3 of P.L.    , c.     (C.        ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill) shall submit an application to the agency in a form and manner prescribed in regulations adopted by the agency pursuant to section 12 of P.L.    , c.    (C.        ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill).  At the time of application, a local government redeveloper shall be subject to a non-refundable application fee not to exceed $10,000. b.    If at any time during the eligibility period the agency determines that the local government redeveloper made a material misrepresentation on the local government redevelopers application, the local government redeveloper shall forfeit the incentive award. c.    If circumstances require a local government redeveloper to amend its application to the agency, then the local government redeveloper, or an authorized agent of the local government redeveloper, shall certify to the agency that the information provided in its amended application is true under the penalty of perjury. 5.    (New section) a.  For social housing projects eligible pursuant to section 3 of P.L.    , c. (C.        ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill), the agency shall award incentive awards based on the order in which complete, qualifying applications are received by the agency.  If a local government redeveloper intends to apply to both the authority and the agency for subsidies, the local government redeveloper shall notify the authority simultaneously with any application made to the agency.  The agency shall transmit its grant determination for such residential projects to the authority along with any information developed by the agency and confirmation of the agencys intent to provide an incentive award or award to the project.  The authority shall review the project cost and shall evaluate and validate the project financing gap estimated by the local government redeveloper. b.    Prior to allocating an incentive award to a social housing project, the agency shall confirm with the Department of Labor and Workforce Development, the Department of Environmental Protection, and the Department of the Treasury that the local government redeveloper is in substantial good standing with the respective department, or a local government redeveloper not in substantial good standing with each department has entered into an agreement with the respective department that includes a practical corrective action plan for the local government redeveloper, and that the local government redeveloper shall confirm that each contractor or subcontractor performing work at the social housing project: (1) is registered as required by "The Public Works Contractor Registration Act," P.L.1999, c.238 (C.34:11-56.48 et seq.); (2) has not been debarred by the Department of Labor and Workforce Development from engaging in or bidding on Public Works Contracts in the State; and (3) possesses a tax clearance certificate issued by the Division of Taxation in the Department of the Treasury.  The agency may also contract with an independent third party to perform a background check on the local government redeveloper.  Provided that the local government redeveloper, and all contractors and subcontractors, are in compliance with this subsection, the agency shall allocate incentive awards to social housing projects according to the social housing project's score and until either the available incentive awards are exhausted or all social housing projects obtaining the minimum score receive an incentive award, whichever occurs first.  If insufficient funding exists to fully fund all eligible projects, a project may be offered partial funding. 6.    (New section) a. Following approval and selection of an application pursuant to sections 4 and 5 of P.L.    , c.    (C.       and C.       ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill), the agency shall enter into an incentive award agreement with the local government redeveloper.  The executive director of the agency shall negotiate the terms and conditions of the incentive award agreement on behalf of the State. b.    An incentive award agreement shall specify the amount of the incentive award the agency shall award to the local government redeveloper and the duration of the eligibility period.  The duration of the eligibility period shall not exceed 10 years for a social housing project, except that to reduce the total value of tax credits needed to reimburse a local government redeveloper for all or part of the project financing gap of a social housing project, the agency may, in its discretion, approve a duration for the eligibility period that is shorter than the applicable maximum periods.  The incentive award agreement shall provide an estimated date of completion and include a requirement for periodic progress reports, including the submittal of executed financing commitments and documents that evidence site control, provided, however, that the local government redeveloper may sell one or more buildings during the eligibility period, subject to such rules and regulations as may be adopted by the agency. If the agency does not receive periodic progress reports, or if the progress reports demonstrate unsatisfactory progress, then the agency may rescind the incentive award.  The incentive award agreement may also provide for a verification of the financing gap at the time the local government redeveloper provides executed financing commitments to the agency and a verification of the local government redeveloper's projected cash flow at the time of certification that the project is completed, provided that the agency shall not resize or reduce the tax credit based on the executed financing commitments or the updated projected cash flow, but that the agency shall use the commitments and cash flow to calculate the return on investment required pursuant to subsection c. of this section. c.    The incentive award agreement shall include a requirement that the agency confirm with the Department of Environmental Protection, the Department of Labor and Workforce Development, and the Department of the Treasury that the local government redeveloper is in substantial good standing with the respective department, or the local government redeveloper has entered into an agreement with the respective department that includes a practical corrective action for the local government redeveloper, and the local government redeveloper shall confirm that each contractor or subcontractor performing work at the social housing project: (1) is registered as required by "The Public Works Contractor Registration Act," P.L.1999, c.238 (C.34:11-56.48 et seq.); (2) has not been debarred, suspended, or disqualified by the Department of Labor and Workforce Development from engaging in or bidding on Public Works Contracts in the State, or been debarred, suspended, or disqualified by a federal agency from engaging in federally funded construction projects or bidding on federal contracting opportunities; and (3) possesses a tax clearance certificate issued by the Division of Taxation in the Department of the Treasury.  The incentive award agreement shall also include a provision that the local government redeveloper shall forfeit the incentive award in any year in which the local government redeveloper is neither in substantial good standing with each department nor has entered into a practical corrective action.  The incentive award agreement shall also require a local government redeveloper to engage in on-site consultations with the Division of Workplace Safety and Health in the Department of Health. d.    A local government redeveloper shall submit, prior to the first disbursement of tax credits under the incentive award agreement, but no later than six months following project completion, satisfactory evidence of actual project costs, as certified by a certified public accountant, evidence of a temporary certificate of occupancy, or other event evidencing project completion that begins the eligibility period indicated in the incentive award agreement.  The local government redeveloper, or an authorized agent of the local government redeveloper, shall certify that the information provided pursuant to this subsection is true under the penalty of perjury.  Claims, records, or statements submitted by a local government redeveloper to the agency in order to receive tax credits shall not be considered claims, records, or statements made in connection with State tax laws. e.    The incentive award agreement shall include a provision allowing the agency to extend, in individual cases, the deadline for any annual reporting or certification requirement. f.     The incentive award agreement shall include one or more provisions, as determined by the agency, concerning the terms and conditions for default and the remedies for the local government redeveloper in the event of default.  The incentive award agreement shall not allow the agency to declare a cross-default when the local government redeveloper, including any business affiliate of the local government redeveloper or any other entity with common principals as the local government redeveloper, is in default with any other assistance program administered by the agency. g.    For a social housing project, the incentive award agreement shall include a requirement that upon completion of the project if, during any year, the local government redeveloper receives rental payments from tenants of the project in excess of the amounts necessary to defray the operating costs of the project, together with any debt service on bonds issued to finance the project, not less than 75 percent of the excess payments shall be deposited into a non-lapsing fund maintained by the local government redeveloper and used exclusively for the maintenance or additional production of social housing projects. 7.    (New section) a. Up to the limits established in this section and in accordance with an incentive award agreement, beginning upon the receipt of occupancy permits for any portion of the social housing project, or upon any other event evidencing project completion as set forth in the incentive award agreement, a local government redeveloper shall be allowed a total tax credit that shall not exceed: 85 percent of the eligible project cost for a social housing project constructed under a project labor agreement; or 80 percent of the eligible project cost for a social housing project not constructed under a project labor agreement. b.    The value of all tax credits approved by the agency under the program for a social housing project shall not exceed $225,000,000 per social housing project. c.    The agency shall not approve a total amount of tax credits greater than $475,000,000 for all social housing projects approved under the program. 8.    (New section)  a. A local government redeveloper approved for an incentive award pursuant to sections 4 and 5 of P.L.    , c.     (C.        and C.        ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill) and that enters an incentive award agreement pursuant to section 6 of P.L.    , c.     (C.       ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill) shall submit annually, commencing in the year in which the incentive award is issued and for the remainder of the eligibility period, a report indicating whether the local government redeveloper is aware of any condition, event, or act that would cause the local government redeveloper not to be in compliance with the incentive award agreement or the provisions of sections 2 through 12 of P.L.    , c.    (C.         through C.       ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill) and any additional reporting requirements contained in the incentive award agreement or tax credit certificate.  The local government redeveloper, or an authorized agent of the local government redeveloper, shall certify that the information provided pursuant to this subsection is true under the penalty of perjury. b. (1) Upon receipt and review of each report submitted during the eligibility period, the agency shall provide to the local government redeveloper and the director a certificate of compliance indicating the amount of tax credits that the local government redeveloper may apply against the local government redeveloper's tax liability.  Notwithstanding any provision of law or regulation to the contrary, the agency shall not require the local government redeveloper to include a permanent certificate of occupancy in the first annual report, but the local government redeveloper shall include the permanent certificate of occupancy in the next annual report after the local government redeveloper receives the permanent certificate of occupancy.  Subject to forfeiture, reduction, or other action for failure to comply with a program requirement, within 120 days after the agency preliminarily determines that an annual report is complete, the agency shall either: (a) approve the annual report and notify the director that the agency has approved the report and that the director is to issue the tax credit certificate; or (b) request more information from the local government redeveloper to finalize the approval.  If the agency fails to act within 120 days from its preliminary determination that the annual report is complete, the annual report shall be deemed approved by the agency, and the local government redeveloper shall be entitled to receive its tax credit certificate. (2)  Upon receipt by the director of the certificate of compliance, the director shall allow the local government redeveloper a credit against the tax imposed pursuant to section 5 of P.L.1945, c.162 (C.54:10A-5).  A local government redeveloper shall apply the credit awarded against the local government redeveloper's liability under section 5 of P.L.1945, c.162 (C.54:10A-5), sections 2 and 3 of P.L.1945, c.132 (C.54:18A-2 and C.54:18A-3), section 1 of P.L.1950, c.231 (C.17:32-15), or N.J.S.17B:23-5 in the tax period for which it was issued, in the tax period in which it was issued, or in any tax period during the eligibility period without the need to amend the tax return for the tax period for which the credit was issued, subject to the carry-forward provision in this section. Notwithstanding the foregoing, no more than the amount of tax credits equal to the total credit amount divided by the duration of the tax credit term, in years, may be taken in any tax period.  A local government redeveloper may carry forward an unused credit resulting from the limitations of paragraph (3) of this subsection, if necessary, for use in the seven privilege periods next following the privilege period for which the credits are applied.  Credits granted to a partnership shall be passed through to the partners, members, or owners, respectively, pro-rata, or pursuant to an executed agreement among the partners, members, or owners documenting an alternate distribution method provided to the director accompanied by any additional information as the director may prescribe. (3)  The director shall prescribe the order of priority of the application of the credit allowed under this section and any other credits allowed by law against the tax imposed under section 5 of P.L.1945, c.162 (C.54:10A-5).  The amount of the credit applied under this section against the tax imposed pursuant to section 5 of P.L.1945, c.162 (C.54:10A-5) for a privilege period, together with any other credits allowed by law, shall not reduce the tax liability to an amount less than the statutory minimum provided in subsection (e) of section 5 of P.L.1945, c.162 (C.54:10A-5). 9.    (New section) a. (1)  A local government redeveloper may apply to the director and the agency for a tax credit transfer certificate, covering one or more years, in lieu of the local government redeveloper being allowed any amount of the credit against the tax liability of the local government redeveloper.  Subject to the forfeiture, reduction, or other action for failure to comply with a program requirement, within 120 days after the agency preliminarily determines that an application is complete, the agency shall either: (a) approve the application and notify the director that the agency has approved the application and that the director is to issue the tax credit transfer certificate; or (b) request more information from the local government redeveloper to finalize the approval.  If the agency fails to act within 120 days from its preliminary determination that the application is complete, the application shall be deemed approved by the agency, and the local government redeveloper shall be entitled to receive its tax credit transfer certificate. (2) The tax credit transfer certificate, upon receipt thereof by the local government redeveloper from the director and the agency, may be sold or assigned, in full or in part in an amount not less STATEMENT This bill provides for increased support for the development of affordable housing and workforce housing through new State programs. Specifically, the bill establishes the Social Housing Program under the New Jersey Housing and Mortgage Finance Agency (HMFA), which authorizes the award of tax credits for social housing projects.  The bill also requires the New Jersey Economic Development Authority (EDA) and HMFA to prioritize applications that include the development of:  affordable housing or workforce housing situated on land owned by a unit of local government; or affordable housing in excess of the minimum affordable housing requirements for the program under which the application is submitted. The bill also establishes the Workforce Housing Investment Program (WHIP), through which a developer of a certified workforce housing project may become eligible for a loan through the New Jersey Infrastructure Bank, as well as consideration for State investment by the Division of Investment in the Department of the Treasury. Additionally, the bill establishes a temporary task force in the Department of the Treasury, designated as the Interagency Task Force on Public Land for Housing. Social Housing Program The bill establishes the Social Housing Program in the HMFA, under which a local government redeveloper may apply for tax credits to finance the construction of a social housing project.  A local government redeveloper will be eligible to receive a tax credit if the social housing project meets certain conditions.  A tax credit granted for a social housing project may not exceed 80 percent of the eligible project cost, or 85 percent if constructed under a project labor agreement. Each incentive award may not exceed $225,000,000.  The bill limits the cumulative value of all tax credits awarded for social housing projects to $475,000,000. Following the award of a tax credit, a local government redeveloper may transfer the tax credit if approved by the HMFA. Under the bill, a social housing project is defined as a residential project that is undertaken and operated by a local government redeveloper, and which: (1) is located on land owned by the local government redeveloper within an enhanced area; (2) is financed, in part, by general obligation bonds issued by the local government redeveloper, or issued on behalf of the local government redeveloper by a county improvement authority for not less than 20 percent of the project costs; and (3) contains no less than 300 residential units.  A social housing project may include, but is not limited to, a single building, multiple contiguous buildings, or multiple buildings located on contiguous parcels of land or on parcels of land no farther apart than one-quarter of a mile, which can be completed over six years.  A local government redeveloper is defined under the bill to mean a county, county improvement authority, municipality, housing authority, or any entity designated by a county or municipality that enters or proposes to enter into an incentive award agreement for the completion and operation of a social housing project under the program.  The bill also amends existing law to explicitly provide county improvement authorities and housing authorities with the ability to apply for and receive a tax credit award as a developer of a social housing project. A local government redeveloper undertaking a social housing project would be required to permanently reserve at least 20 percent of the residential units constructed for workforce housing, and at least 20 percent of residential units constructed for occupancy by low- and moderate-income households with affordability controls, subject to the affordability controls established by the HMFA. Additionally, the local government redeveloper would be required to dedicate certain excess rent payments received from the project for future housing purposes.  Specifically, the bill provides that upon completion of the project if, during any year, the local government redeveloper receives rental payments from tenants of the project in excess of the amounts necessary to defray the operating costs of the project, together with any debt service on bonds issued to finance the project, not less than 75 percent of the excess payments would be required to be deposited into a non-lapsing fund maintained by the redeveloper and used exclusively for the production or maintenance of additional housing projects. The bill also provides that a local government redeveloper would be required to submit a non-refundable application fee not to exceed $10,000. The bill provides that upon completion of a social housing project, tax credits would be issued to the local government redeveloper over a specified eligibility period, which would be set at 10 years for most projects.  However, the local government redeveloper may elect an eligibility period of five years for any social housing project that has a project labor agreement may elect an eligibility period not to exceed five years, or three years if the project is located in a government-restricted municipality.  The bill also authorizes a local government redeveloper to sell, transfer, or pledge the right to receive any tax credits issued under the program. Additionally, the HMFA is required to adopt any necessary rules and regulations to administer the program and publish on its Internet site any pledge, assignment, transfer, or sale approved as a result of the provisions of the bill. The bill also requires the HMFA to elect a State college or university to prepare a report concerning the implementation of the Social Housing Program every two years.  The report is required to include: a description of each social housing project that receives a tax credit under the program; a detailed analysis of the consideration of project factors; and recommendations to further incentivize development. Workforce Housing Investment Program (WHIP) Within one calendar year of the enactment of this bill, the HMFA is required to establish the WHIP for the purposes of facilitating the financing of certified workforce housing projects. Under the program, the HMFA, in coordination with the EDA, will issue certifications for certain projects that qualify as workforce housing projects.  Each certification will be issued upon application by the developer of the project. The bill defines a workforce housing project as a specific construction project or improvement undertaken by a developer, including a local government redeveloper, which project is located in the State and predominantly includes the construction or improvement of residential housing units, and may include a parking component, provided that at least 20 percent of the residential units in the project are reserved for workforce housing.  The bill defines workforce housing as housing that is affordable, according to federal Department of Housing and Urban Development or other recognized standards, for home ownership and rental costs and that is occupied or reserved for occupancy by households with a gross household income not more than 120 percent of the median gross household income for households of the same size within the housing region in which the housing is located. The bill also amends existing law to explicitly provide county improvement authorities and housing authorities with the ability to apply for and receive a tax credit award as a developer of a workforce housing project. The bill provides that a workforce housing project is eligible for certification if the project:  (1) is located in a transit-oriented, high-pedestrian, or walkable area or is located in a former Brownfield site; (2) reserves at least 20 percent of the residential units in the project for workforce housing; (3) is constructed under a project labor agreement; (4) is affiliated with a registered apprenticeship program; (5) contains a minimum capital investment provided by the developer of the project of at least 30 percent of total project costs; and (6) adheres to monitoring standards as determined by the HMFA. When applying for certification, the developer of a workforce housing project may submit evidence of investment from a private pension fund.  Upon receipt of such evidence, the HMFA is required to provide the evidence to the Division of Investment. Upon certification, the HMFA will notify the Division of Investment for consideration of investment in the project, including a match to any private investments for which evidence is submitted, subject to the fiduciary duties and standards for prudent investment, and the New Jersey Infrastructure Bank for determination of eligibility of the developer for a loan issued by the bank through the Human Infrastructure Bank Fund, also established by the bill. Specifically, the bill establishes a Human Infrastructure Bank Fund within the New Jersey Infrastructure Bank.  The fund will be used to provide loans to the developer of a workforce housing project, as certified by the HMFA, to finance all or part of the costs incurred for the planning, acquisition, engineering, construction, reconstruction, repair, and rehabilitation of a workforce housing project.  Under the bill, all moneys placed into the Human Infrastructure Bank Fund are required to be held separate from other funds of the trust. Interagency Task Force on Public Land for Housing The bill also establishes the Interagency Task Force on Public Land for Housing.  The task force will consist of eight members as follows: the Chief Executive Officer of the EDA; the Director of the Division of Investment in the Department of the Treasury; the Executive Director of the HMFA; the Chairperson of the New Jersey Infrastructure Bank; and four public members, one to be appointed by the Senate President, one by the Senate Minority Leader, one by the Speaker of the General Assembly, and one by the Assembly Minority Leader. The task force is required to publish three reports following initial organization as follows: (1) within three months, a report recommending rules and regulations to increase coordination among State entities, counties, and municipalities to reduce administrative delays and improve the efficiency of the review process for WHIP applications; (2) within six months, a report recommending ways in which the State, counties, and municipalities can utilize existing financial structures and practices to invest funds, increase revenues, and develop projects under WHIP; and (3) within one calendar year, a report recommending sites of public land that are suitable for the development of projects under WHIP, beginning with those that are able to support at least 100 housing units. Following the publication of the reports, the EDA and HMFA are required to consider and implement, to the extent feasible, the recommendations provided by the task force regarding the review process for WHIP applications. The task force is set to expire 30 days following the submission of its final report. Local Unit Participation as a Developer This bill expressly provides that a county, municipality, or entity designated by a county or municipality may participate as the developer in the Social Housing Program or WHIP.  The bill specifically authorizes a housing authority designated by a municipality, and a county improvement authority designated by a county, to participate as the developer of such projects.
