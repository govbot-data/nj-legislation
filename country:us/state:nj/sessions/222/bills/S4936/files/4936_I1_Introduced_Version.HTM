STATEMENT

 

     This bill revises various provisions of law related to the veterans property tax deduction, including increasing the amount of the deduction, and expanding eligibility for veterans who served active duty for training, active duty for field training, and initial active duty for training.  This bill would also requires certain forms of discharge documentation to be accepted in determining a veterans eligibility for the veterans property tax deduct may provide evidence of their service.

 

Increased Amount of Veterans Property Tax Deduction

     Under current law, veterans who have been honorably discharged or released under honorable circumstances from active service in the United States Armed Forces are annually entitled to a $250 deduction from the amount of property taxes due on real property.  The deduction was last increased by voter approval of a constitutional amendment in 1999 to gradually raise the deduction over a period of four years.  The final increase occurred in 2003 to set the amount of the deduction at $250, where it has remained ever since.

     This bill would increase the amount of the veterans property tax deduction from $250 to $500 beginning in tax year 2027.  Thereafter, beginning in tax year 2030, the amount of the deduction would be adjusted every 10 years based on the percentage increase in the CPI during the preceding 10 years.   This adjusted amount would be rounded to the next highest multiple of $10.  Consumer Price Index is defined to mean the Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers, New York-Northern New Jersey-Long Island Metropolitan Area, NY-NJ-CT-PA, All Items, as published by the Bureau of Labor Statistics in the United States Department of Labor.

     Under the bill, if the amount of any tax bill is less than $500 or, after tax year 2030, less than the adjusted deduction in any tax year, there would be a cancellation of such tax bill.

     The provisions of the bill to increase the amount of the veterans property tax deduction would remain inoperative until the approval by the voters of the State, at a general election, of an amendment to the New Jersey Constitution authorizing such increases.

 

Eligibility for Veterans Property Tax Deduction

     This bill expands eligibility for the veterans property tax deduction to include veterans who served active duty, active duty for training, active duty for field training, and initial active duty for training in the Armed Forces of the United States or any reserve component thereof, including but not limited to the National Guard.

     Specifically, this bill defines the term active service in any branch of the United States Armed Forces to include active duty, active duty for training, active duty for field training, and initial active duty for training in the Armed Forces of the United States or any reserve component thereof, including but not limited to the National Guard, and to require that such service was for at least 90 days, unless the service is ended prior to 90 days due to service-connected injury.  The bill also defines the Armed Forces of the United States to mean means the land, air, sea, and space forces established by State or federal laws.

     Since 1948, regulations adopted by the Division of Taxation in the Department of the Treasury have provided that the National Guard and reserve components of the United States Armed Forces are not to be considered branches of the Armed Forces of the Unites States.  As a result, members of the National Guard and reserve components of the United States Armed Forces who served in active duty for training generally do not qualify for the $250 veterans property tax deduction.  However, members of the National Guard and members of reserve components of the United States Armed Forces may qualify for the benefits if they are called into active service or deployed by Presidential or Congressional Order.

     P.L.2001, c.351 amended the definition of active duty in N.J.S.A.38A:1-1 to remove the phrase other than active duty for training.  The removal of that language reflected the Legislatures intent that active duty include active duty for training, and should have allowed veterans whose only service was active duty for training to be eligible for the $250 property tax deduction.  However, the Director of the Division of Taxation in the Department of the Treasury did not amend the existing regulations governing these property tax benefits after that enactment, which regulations defined active duty as excluding active duty training or field training, and continued to deny eligibility to veterans whose only service was active duty for training based on that regulation, even though the Legislature had amended the law to specifically ensure that active duty includes active duty for training.

     In 2020, the voters of the State also approved an amendment to the New Jersey Constitution to remove the requirement for veterans to have rendered active service in time of war or other emergency, in order to be eligible for $250 property tax deduction.  Despite this constitutional amendment, veterans whose only active service was active duty for training, active duty for field training, and initial active duty for training are still considered to be ineligible for this property tax benefits.

     This bill clarifies that veterans who serve in the United States Armed Forces, the National Guard, or a reserve component of the United States Armed Forces, during active duty for training, active duty for field training, or initial active duty for training are eligible for the veterans property tax deduction.

 

Certifying Eligibility for the Veterans Property Tax Deduction

     Current law provides that a veteran is required to submit a written application to the Division of Taxation in order to certify their eligibility as a claimant of the veterans property tax deduction.  A veteran is required to submit a copy of the claimant's certificate of honorable discharge or of the claimants certificate of release under honorable circumstances from active service in a branch of the Armed Forces of the United States.

     This bill clarifies that a veteran is permitted to certify their eligibility for the veterans property tax deduction by submitting either a DD Form 214, which is issued to a veteran as a Report of Separation, or a DD Form 256, which is issued to a veteran as an Honorable Discharge Certificate.
