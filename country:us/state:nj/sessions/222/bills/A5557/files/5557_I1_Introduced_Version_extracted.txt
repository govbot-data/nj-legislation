A5557 ASSEMBLY, No. 5557 STATE OF NEW JERSEY 222nd LEGISLATURE INTRODUCED SEPTEMBER 24, 2026 Sponsored by: Assemblyman  GERRY SCHARFENBERGER District 13 (Monmouth) SYNOPSIS Requires health claims utilization data to be shared with public entity plan sponsors. CURRENT VERSION OF TEXT As introduced. An Act concerning public entity health claims utilization data and supplementing various parts of the statutory law. Be It Enacted by the Senate and General Assembly of the State of New Jersey: 1.    This act shall be known and may be cited as the Data Ownership Now Enforced Act or the DONE Act. 2.    The Legislature finds and declares that: a.     Public entities in New Jersey, including municipalities, school districts, counties, and their joint insurance funds and health insurance funds, face double-digit percentage increases in health benefits costs.  The Legislature finds that an emergency exists in the public health benefits market requiring immediate legislative action. b.    The fundamental cause of uncontested, above-market renewals is data asymmetry: incumbent carriers, third-party administrators, fund administrators, and the State Division of Pensions and Benefits hold claims utilization data that is necessary for competing entities to develop credible alternative proposals, and they routinely fail to provide that data proactively, timely, or at no cost.  Public entities have a compelling interest in accessing this data, generated by public expenditures on their behalf, and this act establishes and enforces that right of access. c.     The State Health Benefits Program and School Employees' Health Benefits Program, administered by the Division of Pensions and Benefits within the Department of the Treasury, collectively cover hundreds of thousands of New Jersey public employees and represent the single largest segment of the public health benefits market.  The division currently provides claims data to participating public entities occasionally, only upon specific request, and charges a fee of up to $2,000 for detailed claims data reports.  This practice insulates the State programs from competitive market forces and imposes a cost barrier on the public entities the programs are intended to serve.  The Legislature finds this practice to be contrary to the public interest. d.    The proactive, automatic, cost-free dissemination of claims data to every public entity plan sponsor, without requiring a formal request, is the only mechanism that can realistically allow the January 1, 2027 renewal cycle to be competitive.  Requiring a request-based system imposes administrative friction that advantages incumbents and disadvantages public entities, particularly smaller ones without dedicated benefits staff. e.     In September 2025, the New Jersey Office of the State Comptroller issued a report entitled Local Government and School Board Health Insurance Funds: A Report on Conflicts of Interest and Procurement Violations, documenting systemic conflicts of interest, procurement violations, and governance failures within the health insurance fund structure serving hundreds of New Jersey local governments and school boards.  The comptroller found that the absence of independent oversight and transparent data had created conditions in which public contracts were awarded without genuine competitive process, and called upon the Legislature to strengthen oversight of joint insurance funds.  The Legislature finds that mandatory proactive claims data disclosure is a necessary and foundational component of the reforms the comptroller has recommended. f.     New Jersey's public health benefits market operates through multiple distinct structures, including commercial carriers, third party administrators, municipal health insurance funds, school district health insurance funds, the State Health Benefits Program, and the School Employees Health Benefits Program, all of which must be subject to uniform proactive disclosure obligations for this act to be effective. g.    This act is intended to operate immediately upon enactment, with a 30-day universal dissemination deadline applying to all data custodians for existing claims data already in their possession, so that public entities have the data they need as soon as possible. 3.    As used in this act: "Authorized representative" means a licensed insurance producer, licensed third-party administrator, enrolled actuary, or other agent holding a professional license in New Jersey or executing a data use agreement with enforceable professional liability exposure, designated in writing by a plan sponsor to receive claims data on its behalf for the purpose of evaluating, soliciting, or obtaining competing health benefits proposals. "Benchmark comparison report" means a de-identified actuarial report produced by a data custodian comparing a plan sponsor's utilization patterns, cost per member per month, and claim distribution against an anonymized cohort of similarly-sized and similarly-situated groups in the same geographic rating area, furnished in lieu of individual claim-line data when group size falls below the threshold in section 6 of this act. "Carrier" means any insurance company, hospital service corporation, medical service corporation, health service corporation, or health maintenance organization authorized to issue health benefits plans in New Jersey. "Claims data package" means the structured data set required to be disclosed under sections 4 and 5 of this act. "CPT code" means a Current Procedural Terminology code published by the American Medical Association, or any successor coding system, used to identify medical, surgical, diagnostic, and other health care services. "Data custodian" means any carrier, third-party administrator, health insurance fund administrator, joint insurance fund administrator, stop-loss carrier, or the Division of Pensions and Benefits, that holds, processes, or maintains claims data on behalf of a plan sponsor or fund member.  For purposes of the State Health Benefits Program and School Employees Health Benefits Program, the Division of Pensions and Benefits shall be the data custodian. "De-identified data" means health information from which all direct and indirect identifiers have been removed consistent with 45 C.F.R. s.164.514(b). "Diagnosis code" means an ICD-10-CM code, or successor standard, used to classify diagnoses and conditions for claims processing purposes. "Division" means the Division of Pensions and Benefits within the New Jersey Department of the Treasury. "Fund administrator" means the executive director, administrator, or third-party management firm engaged to administer operations of a health insurance fund or joint insurance fund on behalf of its fund commissioners. "Health Benefits Data Compliance Designee" or "designee" means the individual designated by a public entity pursuant to Section 8 of this Act. "Health insurance fund" means a joint insurance fund established to provide group health and related employee benefits, formed pursuant to P.L.1983, c.372 (C.40A:10-36 et seq.) for municipal members, or P.L.1983, c.108 (C.18A:18B-1 et seq.) for school district members, and regulated by DOBI under N.J.A.C.11:15-3 or 11:15-5 respectively, including any regional employee benefits fund or municipal employee benefit fund operating under those statutes. "Joint insurance fund" means a joint insurance fund established under P.L.1983, c.372 (C.40A:10-36 et seq.) providing property, liability, workers' compensation, or health benefits, or any combination thereof, to local government units. Where a joint insurance fund provides health benefits, it is subject to this act with respect to those health benefits. "Municipal Reinsurance Health Insurance Fund" means a second-tier joint insurance fund whose members are other health insurance funds, formed under P.L.1983, c.372 (C.40A:10-36 et seq.) for the purpose of providing aggregate stop-loss reinsurance to member health insurance funds. "NDC code" means a National Drug Code assigned by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration to identify pharmaceutical products. "Plan sponsor" means a public entity that sponsors, participates in, or maintains a group health benefits plan through any mechanism, including a commercial carrier, third-party administrator, health insurance fund, joint insurance fund, the State Health Benefits Program, or the School Employees Health Benefits Program. "Public entity" means any municipality, county, school district, regional school district, vocational school district, special district, authority, board, commission, or other unit of local government in this State. "Standard annual claims data package" means the claims data package required under section 5 of this act, including all medical claims data, pharmacy claims data, summary utilization statistics, and plan design and financial summary elements.  A standard annual claims data package does not include custom actuarial modeling, predictive analytics, population health projections, or other analytical products beyond the data elements specified in section 5 of this act. The Division of Pensions and Benefits may not reclassify any element required under section 5 as a special analytical report for purposes of imposing a fee. "Stop-loss carrier" means an insurance company providing specific or aggregate excess loss coverage to a self-funded plan, health insurance fund, or joint insurance fund, which in doing so receives and holds individual or aggregate claims data. "Third-party administrator" means any entity licensed under N.J.S.A. 17B:27B-1 et seq. or otherwise engaged to process health benefits claims on behalf of a self-funded plan, health insurance fund, or joint insurance fund. 4.    a.  Within 30 days of the effective date of this act, every data custodian shall proactively transmit, without any request, application, or fee, a complete claims data STATEMENT This bill enacts the Data Ownership Now Enforced Act, or DONE Act.  The bill requires health insurance carriers, third-party administrators, health insurance fund and joint insurance fund administrators, stop-loss carriers, and the Division of Pensions and Benefits to proactively transmit structured, HIPAA-compliant claims utilization data to every public entity plan sponsor within 30 days of enactment of the bill, without request and without charge. Public entities (municipalities, school districts, counties, health insurance funds and joint insurance funds) are among the largest purchasers of health benefits in New Jersey and among the least equipped to evaluate the pricing they receive.  The claims utilization data necessary for a competing carrier or administrator to develop a credible alternative proposal is held exclusively by the incumbent data custodian.  Without that data, competitive procurement is not possible. The result is that renewal increases go unchallenged, not because they are justified, but because the public entity lacks the information to evaluate them.  This bill corrects that condition by establishing an affirmative, immediate, and cost-free data disclosure obligation applicable to every data custodian in the public health benefits market. The absence of data transparency also creates the governance conditions documented by the New Jersey Office of the State Comptroller in its September 2025 report, Local Government and School Board Health Insurance Funds: A Report on Conflicts of Interest and Procurement Violations.  The comptroller found that the absence of independent oversight and transparent data had created conditions in which public contracts were awarded without genuine competitive process, and called upon the Legislature to strengthen oversight of joint insurance funds.  This bill implements that recommendation at the foundational level, as no meaningful oversight is possible without the underlying data that allows for evaluation. The current system denies elected officials and administrative teams responsible for public funds the information necessary for them to fulfill their fiduciary responsibilities to taxpayers.  Smaller public entities (small school districts, boroughs, and townships without dedicated benefits staff) bear the heaviest burden of data asymmetry, as they lack the leverage to extract data informally.  This bill creates a uniform disclosure floor so that every public entity, regardless of size, receives the same proactive data package automatically, enabling officials at every level to make informed decisions about one of their largest budget expenditures. The State Health Benefits Program and School Employees' Health Benefits Program are the largest segment of the public health benefits market in New Jersey.  The Division of Pensions and Benefits currently charges participating public entities up to $2,000 for claims data reports and provides data only upon specific request. This bill requires the division to transmit standard annual claims data packages to every participating entity within 30 days of enactment and annually thereafter, at no charge.  Any fee schedule charging for standard data disclosure is superseded on the date of enactment.  The State is subject to the same disclosure standard this bill imposes on private data custodians. New Jersey's public health benefits market operates through multiple distinct legal structures.  Health insurance funds and joint insurance funds are not insurance companies, but statutory entities, meaning standard carrier disclosure obligations do not automatically apply to their administrators.  This bill defines fund administrators and third-party administrators as data custodians with the same disclosure obligations as commercial carriers.  The Municipal Reinsurance Health Insurance Fund and stop-loss carriers, which hold individual large-claim data not otherwise accessible to plan sponsors, are separately addressed. For public entities with fewer than 50 covered lives, individual claim-line disclosure raises HIPAA re-identification concerns.  In lieu of claim-line data, this bill requires data custodians to furnish a benchmark comparison report: an actuarially certified comparison of the group's utilization and cost against an anonymized cohort of similarly-sized groups in the same geographic rating area.  A multi-year pooling option allows small groups with three years of coverage history to request Tier 2 claim-level data.  A benchmark fallback provision addresses data custodians that lack a sufficient same-geography cohort. The bill requires each public entity to designate an existing employee as its health benefits data compliance designee.  This provision requires no new hire, no certification, and no fee. The designee is a named accountability contact who serves as the point of contact for data custodians, coordinates authorized representative access, and files an annual attestation. The bill provides for penalties for failure to meet the initial 30-day dissemination obligation accrue without a cure period at $1,000 per data custodian per day, capped at $100,000 per month.  Ongoing violations carry penalties up to $5,000 per day, or $25,000 per day for willful non-compliance.  Plan sponsors may seek expedited judicial relief on a five-business-day order to show cause with priority calendar treatment.  Non-compliance by the Division of Pensions and Benefits is reported directly to the Governor and Legislature, and public entities may seek a writ of mandamus compelling division compliance.
