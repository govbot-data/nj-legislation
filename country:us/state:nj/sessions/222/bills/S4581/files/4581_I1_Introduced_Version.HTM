SYNOPSIS

     Revises statutes concerning assessment of real property.

 

CURRENT VERSION OF TEXT

     As introduced.

  


An Act concerning the assessment of real property, supplementing Title 54 of the Revised Statutes, and amending and repealing various parts of the statutory law.

 

     Be It Enacted by the Senate and General Assembly of the State of New Jersey:

 

     1.  (New section)  Sections 1 through 3 of P.L.     , c.       (C.          ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill) shall be known and may be cited as the Modernization of Real Property Assessment Act.

 

     2.  (New section)  The Legislature finds and declares:

     a.  In New Jersey, the property assessment function is the mechanism for distributing the cost of county, municipal and school district services amongst the taxpayers of each  district.  The property tax levy for county, municipal and school district budgets exceeded $36 billion Statewide in 2025, much larger than income tax collections or sales tax collections in that year.

     b.  Under State law, there are two objective measures of the quality of property assessments:  the general coefficient of deviation, and the directors assessed-value to market-value ratio.  Currently, in only two of 21 counties, namely Monmouth and Somerset counties, do the vast majority of municipalities, on average, satisfy the permissible threshold for both of those assessment measures.  The property taxpayers in the remaining 19 counties generally do not have access to the same assessment transparency and accuracy.  If a property is over-assessed, its owners will generally pay higher property taxes than they would if their property was assessed at its correct market value.  If a property is under-assessed, its owners will generally pay less property taxes than they would if their property was assessed at its correct market value.  Neither scenario is fair to the individual property taxpayers, or the broader group of municipal residents.

     c.  Based on the States published metrics, a substantial percentage of the property assessments throughout the State are in violation of the law and should undergo immediate reassessment.  To address the roughly 1.5 million individual properties that would require reassessment, if those assessments were corrected using a traditional revaluation of property in the municipalities in which those properties are located, which would cost on average $130 per parcel of real property revalued, it would cost the municipalities in the State approximately $195,650,000 to perform those revaluations and correct the real property assessments.  This multi-million dollar cost would be borne by property taxpayers and would add to the already high property tax burden in this State.

     d.  Using the current per-parcel cost for a traditional revaluation, it would cost property taxpayers roughly $455,000,000 to reassess the States approximately 3.5 million parcels of real property to bring them up to true value, which is the statutory standard that the State has set for uniformity of real property assessments as required in the State Constitution.  Without periodic maintenance, the accuracy of the assessments and the corresponding distribution of the property tax levy begins to erode over time.  However, if the State required every municipal assessor to perform recurring reassessments of real property, supported by ongoing periodic internal inspections, to maintain the true value of assessments, municipalities would have a diminished need to contract with revaluation companies, the property tax burden would be fairly distributed among all property taxpayers, and local residents would be taxed based on the true value of their property.

     e.  Therefore, it is in the public interest for the State to require, in every municipality, a transition to regular reassessments of real property supported by ongoing periodic internal inspections, and related revisions to the timing of assessment-related functions, as well as to also revise and streamline  the property assessment appeal process.  These changes would benefit all property taxpayers in the State, who would be assured that they are paying only their fair share of the property tax burden through the accurate assessment of their propertys true value.

 

     3.  (New section)  a.  On or before November 1 of the ninth full calendar year next following the enactment of P.L.     , c.       (C.        ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill), every municipality in the State shall implement the performance of recurring five-year real property reassessment cycles.  Each five-year real property reassessment cycle shall consist of either of the following processes:

     (1)   five consecutive years in which all real property assessments in the municipality shall be reassessed to 100 percent of the true value of the property, as of September 1 of the pre-tax year; or

     (2)   four consecutive years in which all real property assessments in the municipality shall be assessed at the directors ratio, followed by the reassessment of all real property in the municipality at 100 percent of the true value of the property, as of September 1 of the pre-tax year.

     b.    The governing body of each municipality shall, by resolution, determine whether the five-year real property reassessment cycles shall be implemented in accordance with the process set forth in paragraph (1) or paragraph (2) of subsection a. of this section.  A municipalitys resolution shall remain in effect for not less than two full five-year cycles.  Regardless of the process implemented in the municipality for the five-year real property reassessment cycle, the municipality shall conduct an internal inspection of all properties in the municipality at least once every five years.  If a governing body of a municipality fails to adopt a resolution pursuant to this subsection by July 1 of the tax year immediately preceding the initial implementation of the five-year real property reassessment cycle, the reassessment cycle shall be implemented in accordance with the process set forth in paragraph (1) of subsection a. of this section.

     c.  Each county board of taxation shall assist all municipalities in the county in meeting the requirements of this section through the promulgation of a county-wide phase-in plan for the orderly completion and implementation of the transition to the performance of the reassessments required in this section, or by any other means that the board deems appropriate.

     As used in this section, directors ratio means the average ratio of assessed to true value for each taxing district in the State, calculated annually by the Director of the Division of Taxation in the Department of the Treasury.

STATEMENT

 

     This bill revises current law concerning the assessment of real property, in order to make the assessment process more uniform among all 21 counties in the State.  The bill creates a system of perpetual, technology-based reassessments using current market data that would maintain property assessments at their estimated market value, supported by ongoing internal inspections.  By periodically maintaining assessments at or closer to the estimated current market value, the bill seeks to ensure the fair distribution of the property tax levy within each town as local markets change over time.  The bill also makes changes to the timing of annual assessment-related functions, and streamlines the property assessment appeal process.

 

Changes to the Timing of Annual Assessment-Related Functions:

     The bill amends the provisions of statutory law concerning the functions related to the assessment of real property.  P.L.2012, c.13 (C.54:1-101 et al) established the Real Property Assessment Demonstration Program, in part, to create an alternative property assessment calendar.  That calendar accelerated the tax appeals process for property owners and allowed for tax appeals to be heard by county boards of taxation prior to a municipalitys adoption of its budget, permitting a municipality to more effectively budget for tax appeal refunds.  Monmouth County has participated in the Real Property Assessment Demonstration Program for over a decade.  Gloucester County and Burlington County have also adopted that alternative calendar, as authorized by subsequent legislation.

     This bill repeals the current Real Property Assessment Demonstration Program and revises current law to change the statutory dates for assessment functions so that all counties operate under these alternative calendar dates for tax appeals.  This allows tax appeals to county boards of taxation throughout the State to be heard and decided prior to a municipalitys adoption of its budget, allowing all municipalities in the State to budget for tax appeal refunds effectively.

Requirement for Ongoing Periodic Reassessment of Real Property:

     The bill would require every municipality, over a nine-year period, to implement the performance of perpetual five-year reassessment cycles, either through: five consecutive years of the revision of all assessments to the estimated current market value; or four consecutive years of the annual revision of all assessments to the current  ratio of assessed value to true value as calculated by the Director of the Division of Taxation in the Department of the Treasury, and in the fifth year, a revision of the assessments to the current market value as of September 1 of the pretax year.  In order to ensure the ability of municipal assessors to perform these valuations, the bill would establish a new certification program, the mass appraisal certification, to be developed by the Director of the Center for Government Services at Rutgers, the State University.  With the establishment of a mass appraisal certification for assessors, this bill seeks to significantly and permanently reduce the administrative costs of the property assessment function Statewide.

 

Requirements for Property Inspections:

     The bill would also require municipal assessors to physically inspect the interior of each property in their municipalities.  The bill requires that inspections of 100 percent of the parcels of real property be attempted every five years, pursuant to a schedule set forth in the bill.  The bill also allows assessment-related inspections, at the taxpayers discretion, to be performed virtually, using smartphone technology and protocols established by the county board of taxation.

 

Assessment Appeals:

     The bill makes several changes to statutes concerning appeals of property assessments.  Currently, the owner of any parcel of real property assessed at $1,000,000 or more may appeal an assessment directly to the Tax Court.  This bill changes that threshold from $1,000,000 to $2,000,000.  Under the bill, the owner of a property that is a one- to four-family family residential dwelling, regardless of its assessed or estimated market value, is required to file an assessment appeal with the county board of taxation.  The bill amends the appeal filing fee calculation to be performed utilizing the estimated market value of the property as found on the annual notification of assessment required to be provided to each taxpayer annually.  The bill also requires that at the written request of a property owner, an assessment appeal hearing is to be conducted virtually, using conference call technology and protocols adopted by the county board of taxation, or as an evidence-only hearing.

     The bill also amends current law that requires the assessment of a  property owner who is successful in the appeal of an assessment, before either the Tax Court or the county board of taxation, to be binding for the tax year of the successful appeal and the next two succeeding tax years.  This current law is informally known as the freeze act.  This bill would amend current law to require that when a municipality has implemented five-year periodic reassessments, as required by the bill, the change in the assessment would be limited to the municipalitys year-over-year aggregate value percentage change, which is to be calculated using the total taxable values from each year for the two years following the judgment.

     Under the bill, the tools used to determine the need for assessment revision under appeal do not apply in assessment appeals before a county board of taxation or the Tax Court when the assessments have been revised to current market value as part of a five-year recurring reassessment program.

Public Notice:

     The bill requires the use of the countys and the municipalitys public notification website to inform taxpayers of the ability to review new property assessments.  The bill also amends the information provided on the notification of assessment that is annually mailed to every property taxpayer.  The purpose of the additional information is to provide transparency as to the estimated market value of current assessments.
