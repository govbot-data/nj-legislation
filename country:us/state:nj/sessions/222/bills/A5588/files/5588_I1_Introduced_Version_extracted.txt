CURRENT VERSION OF TEXT As introduced. An Act concerning opioid settlement funds, amending P.L.2023, c.25, and supplementing Title 26 of the Revised Statutes. Be It Enacted by the Senate and General Assembly of the State of New Jersey: 1.    (New Section) The Legislature finds and declares: a.     The opioid epidemic in the United States has resulted in a complex array of lawsuits across the country against pharmaceutical companies and other entities implicated in the crisis. b.    New Jersey and its local governments will be paid more than $1.1 billion through 2038 pursuant to settlement agreements with opioid manufacturers, distributors, and retailers. The State will receive half of the funds and the other half will be allocated directly to 262 local government entities, including all 21 counties in the State. c.     The national opioid settlement agreements, a State Attorney Generals memorandum of agreement, and State law govern the approved uses of the settlement funds in New Jersey. d.    The majority of the funds are intended for opioid abatement programs focused on treatment, prevention, and other strategies to combat the opioid epidemic and the harms it inflicts on New Jerseyans.  A relatively small amount of the funds - no more than five percent - may be used for administrative purposes. e.     Under State law, the Department of Human Services administers the non-lapsing Opioid Recovery and Remediation Fund, where the State-portion of settlement-agreement payments are deposited, and functions as lead agency for allocating and disbursing State funds and for the reporting, public disclosure, and other compliance obligations of all funds under the settlements. f.     In a July 2025 report on an investigation of Irvingtons mismanagement of opioid settlement funds, the New Jersey Office of the State Comptroller asserted that the Department of Human Services should provide more guidance and oversight regarding the use of opioid settlement funds by local entities. g.    It is in the publics interest for the Legislature to strengthen the law which governs the use of opioid settlement funds to hold recipients accountable, thereby ensuring that these important resources support the individuals, families, and communities impacted by the opioid crisis through expanding access to care and mitigating the ongoing harms of the epidemic. 2.    Section 1 of P.L.2023, c.25 (C.26:2G-39) is amended to read as follows: 1.    a. STATEMENT This bill expands the oversight and transparency of funds received by the State and local entities due to national opioid settlements.  Through 2038, New Jersey and its local governments will be paid more than $1.1 billion pursuant to settlement agreements with opioid manufacturers, distributors, and retailers for their responsibility in the nationwide opioid epidemic.  The State will receive half of the funds and the other half will be allocated directly to 262 local government entities, including all 21 counties in the State. Specifically, under the bill, any moneys allocated to or otherwise received by the State or any of its counties or municipalities as a result of a national opioid litigation resolution, and subsequently distributed in accordance with current law, are required to be expended within 18 months of receipt by the recipient unless the intended use of such funds is for a multi-year project, under which circumstance the funds are to be expended according to a pre-determined timeline approved by the Department of Human Services or the applicable oversight entity of the county or municipality.  If, after 18 months or the conclusion of the pre-determined timeline for multi-year projects, the moneys are not expended, the recipient is required to either: 1) seek approval from the Department of Human Services or the applicable oversight entity of the county or municipality for an extension; or 2) return the moneys to the "Opioid Recovery and Remediation Fund," the repository of the States share of opioid settlement funds, or the applicable county or municipality fund for redistribution. The bill also requires that any opioid settlement moneys not expended in accordance with current law, as determined by the Department of Human Services, the State Comptroller, the Attorney General, or the applicable oversight entity of the county or municipality, are to be reimbursed by the recipient and deposited into the "Opioid Recovery and Remediation Fund" or the applicable county or municipality fund within 60 days of the determination.  Broadly, funds must support treatment of Opioid Use Disorder and any co-occurring Substance Use Disorder or Mental Health conditions through evidence-based or evidence-informed programs or strategies.  The bill permits affected entities to request an extension on this 60-day deadline.  The bill also permits entities in receipt of opioid settlement moneys, who determine that all or a portion of the funds cannot be spent in accordance with current law, to return such funds to the applicable account for redistribution. The bill directs the existing Opioid Recovery and Remediation Advisory Council to establish a training program designed to educate counties and municipalities on the approved uses of opioid settlement funds, and to implement a formal process by which a county or municipality may request guidance from the council regarding the appropriate expenditure of funds.  The bill also requires that no member of the council is to take any official action or make a recommendation on any matter in which the member has a direct or indirect financial interest.  Additionally, the bill affirms that any staff or administrative support provided to the council by the Department of Human Services, as required under existing law, is to constitute an administrative cost of the Opioid Recovery and Remediation Fund. Finally, the bill requires the Department of Human Services and a  county or municipality that receives proceeds from national opioid litigation resolutions to post a link on the applicable websites to the New Jersey Office of the State Comptrollers online form which allows members of the public to file a report regarding alleged fraud, waste, or abuse of State funds, along with instructions that this link may be used to report suspected fraud, waste, or abuse relating to the expenditure of national opioid settlement funds.  Counties and municipalities must also post a link to any reports required to be compiled by the county or municipality regarding the expenditure of moneys received as a result of a national opioid litigation resolution.
