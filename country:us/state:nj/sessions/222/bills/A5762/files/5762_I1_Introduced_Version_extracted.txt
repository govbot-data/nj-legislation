A5762 ASSEMBLY, No. 5762 STATE OF NEW JERSEY 222nd LEGISLATURE INTRODUCED NOVEMBER 16, 2026 Sponsored by: Assemblywoman  SHAMA A. HAIDER District 37 (Bergen) SYNOPSIS Requires certain State, local, and regulated private entities to use plain language in public communications. CURRENT VERSION OF TEXT As introduced. An Act requiring certain State, local, and regulated private entities to communicate with the public using plain language and supplementing Title 52 of the Revised Statutes and Title 40A of the New Jersey Statutes. Be It Enacted by the Senate and General Assembly of the State of New Jersey: 1.  a.  Every form, notice, and digital interface communicated to the public by a State agency or a State-regulated private entity shall be written in a simple, clear, understandable, and easily readable way that offers the average reader fair notice of its provisions and shall follow the requirements set forth in this section, as appropriate, including: (1)  shall have a readability score of no higher than a seventh-grade reading level using validated readability formulas; (2)  shall use plain-language field labels on digital interfaces, explain why each piece of information is needed, and include an accessible glossary; and (3)  shall use plain language, which shall include, but not be limited to, active voice, short sentences with an average of 20 words, plain definitions of technical terms, numbered steps for instructions, explicit deadlines and consequences, a summary at the top of long documents, and shall not hide material information or discourage residents from exercising their rights. b.  (1)  A person may file a complaint with the Division of Consumer Affairs alleging a violation of the provisions of this section by submitting the complaint on a form prescribed by the division.  No complaint shall be accepted by the division unless it has been signed. (2)  Within 30 calendar days of receiving a complaint, the division shall provide the State agency or State-regulated private entity, as appropriate, with written notice of the alleged violation. The State agency or State-regulated private entity, as appropriate, shall be permitted 30 calendar days to cure the alleged violation or demonstrate that no violation occurred.  The division shall have 30 calendar days from the date of receipt of the response to the complaint by the agency or entity, as appropriate, to determine whether the violation has been cured or there is satisfactory proof that no violation existed. (3)  In making its determination, the division may consider the following factors: (a)  the nature, circumstances, and significance of the violations; (b)  the history of prior violations during the prior three-year period; (c)  any good faith efforts to cure the violation within a reasonable time period following the notice; and (d)  any other considerations that the division may deem appropriate. (4)  A person who files a complaint with the division alleging a violation of this section may, within 45 calendar days of receipt of the final determination of the division, file an appeal with the Appellate Division of the Superior Court. (5)  The failure of a State agency or State-regulated private entity, as appropriate, to respond to a notice of complaint filed with the division pursuant to paragraph (2) of this subsection shall result in a fine of $500 for each day in which the State agency or State-regulated private entity, as appropriate, fails to respond to the complaint, beginning on the 31st calendar day after the submission of the written notice of complaint to the State agency or State-regulated private entity, as appropriate, by the division. Moneys received from fines collected shall be deposited with the State Treasurer, and shall be disbursed to the division to defray the expenses of administering the provisions of this act. c.  As used in this section: Digital interface means online applications, portals, and forms. State agency means all executive branch departments, agencies, offices, boards, and commissions. State-regulated private entity means banks, insurers, hospitals, utilities, and landlords regulated by the State. d.  The provisions of this section shall apply to every form, notice, and digital interface created after the effective date of P.L.   c.   (C.   ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill). 2.  a.  Every form, notice, and digital interface communicated to the public by a political subdivision or an agency of a political subdivision having the authority to adopt rules or regulations shall be written in a simple, clear, understandable, and easily readable way that offers the average reader fair notice of its provisions and shall follow the requirements set forth in this section, as appropriate, including: (1)  shall have a readability score of no higher than a seventh-grade reading level using validated readability formulas; (2)  shall use plain-language field labels on digital interfaces, explain why each piece of information is needed, and include an accessible glossary; and (3)  shall use plain language, which shall include, but not be limited to, active voice, short sentences with an average of 20 words, plain definitions of technical terms, numbered steps for instructions, explicit deadlines and consequences, a summary at the top of long documents, and shall not hide material information or discourage residents from exercising their rights. b. (1)  A person may file a complaint with the Division of Consumer Affairs alleging a violation of the provisions of this section by submitting a complaint on a form prescribed by the division.  No complaint shall be accepted by the division unless it has been signed. (2)  Within 30 calendar days of receiving a complaint, the Director of the Division of Consumer Affairs, or a designee, shall provide the political subdivision or an agency of the political subdivision, as appropriate, with written notice of the alleged violation.  The political subdivision or an agency of the political subdivision, as appropriate, shall be permitted 30 calendar days to cure the alleged violation or demonstrate that no violation occurred.  The division shall have 30 calendar days from the date of receipt of the response to the complaint by the political subdivision or an agency of the political subdivision, as appropriate, to determine whether the violation has been cured or there is satisfactory proof that no violation existed. (3)  In making its determination, the division may consider the following factors: (a)  the nature, circumstances, and significance of the violations; (b)  the history of prior violations during the prior three-year period; (c)  any good faith efforts to cure the violation within a reasonable time period following the notice; and (d)  any other considerations that the division may deem appropriate. (4)  A person who files a complaint with the division alleging a violation of this section may, within 45 calendar days of receipt of the final determination of the division, file an appeal with the Appellate Division of the Superior Court. (5)  The failure of a political subdivision or an agency of the political subdivision, as appropriate, to respond to a notice of complaint filed with the division pursuant to paragraph (2) of this subsection shall result in a fine of $500 for each day in which the political subdivision or an agency of the political subdivision, as appropriate, fails to respond to the complaint, beginning on the 31st calendar day after the submission of the written notice of complaint to the political subdivision or an agency of the political subdivision, as appropriate by the division. Moneys received from fines collected shall be deposited with the State Treasurer, and shall be disbursed to the division to defray the expenses of administering the provisions of this act. c.  As used in this section: Digital interface means online applications, portals, and forms. Political subdivision means, but is not limited to, a municipality, a county, a township, a district, or a special district. d.  The provisions of this section shall apply to every form, notice, and digital interface created after the effective date of P.L.   c.   (C.   ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill). 3.  This act shall take effect immediately. STATEMENT This bill requires State agencies, State-regulated private entities, political subdivisions of the State, and agencies of a political subdivision of this State to use plain language when communicating with the public. Millions of residents read below a sixth-grade level, yet many State and local entities and regulated private entities routinely use forms, notices, and digital interfaces with complex jargon and bureaucratic language.  Inaccessible language causes missed benefits, avoidable appeals, administrative errors, and unequal access, disproportionately burdening low-income, elderly, disabled, and limited-English-proficient residents. Under this bill, every form, notice, and digital interface communicated to the public by a State agency, State-regulated private entity, political subdivision, and an agency of a political subdivision will be written in a simple, clear, understandable, and easily readable way that offers the average reader fair notice of its provisions and will follow the following requirements, as appropriate: (1)  will have a readability score of no higher than a seventh-grade reading level using validated readability formulas; (2)  will use plain-language field labels on digital interfaces, explain why each piece of information is needed, and include an accessible glossary; and (3)  will use plain language, which shall include, but not be limited to, active voice, short sentences with an average of 20 words, plain definitions of technical terms, numbered steps for instructions, explicit deadlines and consequences, a summary at the top of long documents, and shall not hide material information or discourage residents from exercising their rights. A person may file a complaint with the Division of Consumer Affairs alleging a violation of the provisions of this bill by submitting a signed complaint on a form prescribed by the division. The appropriate entity will be permitted to cure the alleged violation or demonstrate that no violation occurred within 30 days of receiving the notice of complaint.  The division will have 30 calendar days from the date of receipt of the appropriate entitys response to the complaint to determine whether the violation has been cured or there is satisfactory proof that no violation existed.  If the appropriate entity fails to respond to the notice of complaint, a fine of $500 will be imposed for each day in which the entity fails to respond.  Moneys received from fines will be deposited with the State Treasurer, and disbursed to the division to defray the expenses of administering the provisions of the bill. A person who files a complaint with the division alleging a violation of this bill, may, within 45 calendar days of the determination of the division, file an appeal with the Appellate Division of the Superior Court As defined in the bill, digital interface means online applications, portals, and forms and State-regulated private entity means banks, insurers, hospitals, utilities, and landlords with respect to communications with the public. The provisions of the bill are prospective in nature and will apply to every form, notice, and digital interface created after the effective date of the bill.
