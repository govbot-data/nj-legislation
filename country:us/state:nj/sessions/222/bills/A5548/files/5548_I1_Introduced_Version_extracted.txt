A5548 ASSEMBLY, No. 5548 STATE OF NEW JERSEY 222nd LEGISLATURE INTRODUCED SEPTEMBER 24, 2026 Sponsored by: Assemblyman  KENYATTA STEWART District 35 (Bergen and Passaic) SYNOPSIS Automatic Utility and Communications Service Credit Act; requires certain telecommunications utilities to provide credits for service outages of longer than three hours. CURRENT VERSION OF TEXT As introduced. An Act concerning bill credits for telecommunications service outages, designated as the Automatic Utility and Communications Service Credit Act, amending P.L.1987, c.422 and P.L.1991, c.428, and supplementing Title 48 of the Revised Statutes. STATEMENT This bill, which is designated as the Automatic Utility and Communications Service Credit Act, establishes and amends certain telecommunications bill credits for service outages.  Under the bill, every:  (1) cable television company; (2) direct broadcast satellite service provider; (3) internet service provider; (4) local exchange telecommunications company; (5) wireless telephone provider; and (6) interexchange telecommunications carrier (collectively, telecommunications companies), is required to provide a bill credit to a customer or subscriber that experienced a complete outages of service for three or more hours in duration, which bill credit is to be prorated at one thirtieth of the customers or subscribers monthly bill for every three hours of complete outage of service and is to be automatically applied to the customers or subscribers next bill statement. The bill further specifies that a failure to apply a bill credit by a telecommunications company is to be a violation of the New Jersey Consumer Fraud Act.  Additionally, the bill provides that a telecommunications company is to be liable, in addition to any legal or equitable remedy under the New Jersey Consumer Fraud Act, to a civil penalty of $1,000 per affected customer or subscriber and up to $25,000 per each instance of complete outage, which is to be collected by the Board of Public Utilities in a summary proceeding before a court of competent jurisdiction. Under the bill, a complete outage means a total interruption of service that prevents the customer or subscriber from using the service for the services intended purpose but does not include outages due to:  non-emergency inclement weather; customer or subscriber equipment failure; customer or subscriber nonpayment; scheduled maintenance with advance notice to the customer or subscriber; and acts of war or terrorism.
