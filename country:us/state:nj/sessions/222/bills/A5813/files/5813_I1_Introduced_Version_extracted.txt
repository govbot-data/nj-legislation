A5813 ASSEMBLY, No. 5813 STATE OF NEW JERSEY 222nd LEGISLATURE INTRODUCED NOVEMBER 16, 2026 Sponsored by: Assemblyman  ALEX SAUICKIE District 12 (Burlington, Middlesex, Monmouth and Ocean) Co-Sponsored by: Assemblywoman Fantasia SYNOPSIS Requires electric public utilities to perform vegetation management on certain vegetation at certain intervals. CURRENT VERSION OF TEXT As introduced. An Act concerning electric public utility vegetation management and supplementing Title 48 of the Revised Statutes. Be It Enacted by the Senate and General Assembly of the State of New Jersey: 1.    a.  As used in this section: Electric public utility or utility means a public utility, as that term is defined pursuant to R.S.48:2-13, that transmits and distributes electricity to end users in the State. Vegetation manager means an arborist selected by a utility to administer all aspects of that utilitys vegetation management program. b.    Notwithstanding any law, rule, regulation, order, or agreement to the contrary, each electric public utility shall perform vegetation management at least once every three years on any vegetation deemed by the utilitys vegetation manager to pose a threat to the utilitys energized conductors. 2.    This act shall take effect immediately. STATEMENT This bill requires each electric public utility (utility) to perform vegetation management at least once every three years on all vegetation deemed by the utilitys vegetation manager to pose a threat to the utilitys energized conductors.  Currently, each utility is required to perform vegetation management at least once every four years on all vegetation deemed by the utilitys vegetation manager to pose a threat to the utilitys energized conductors. It is the sponsors belief that most rural and suburban outages in the State are caused by the lack of vegetation management.  Outages in rural areas present an additional hazard for residents due to their dependency on electricity for certain services, such as water and septic, potentially impacting both human and animal health.
