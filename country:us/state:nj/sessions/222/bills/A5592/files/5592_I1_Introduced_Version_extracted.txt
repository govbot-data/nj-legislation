A5592 ASSEMBLY, No. 5592 STATE OF NEW JERSEY 222nd LEGISLATURE INTRODUCED OCTOBER 5, 2026 Sponsored by: Assemblyman  GERRY SCHARFENBERGER District 13 (Monmouth) Co-Sponsored by: Assemblyman Clifton and Assemblywoman Flynn SYNOPSIS Provides corporation business and gross income tax credits to certain businesses for qualified purchases from NJ small businesses. CURRENT VERSION OF TEXT As introduced. An Act providing tax credits to certain business for certain purchases from small business and supplementing P.L.1945, c.162 (C.54:10A-1 et seq.) and Title 54A of the New Jersey Statutes. Be It Enacted by the Senate and General Assembly of the State of New Jersey: 1.    a. As used in this section: Eligible business means a business with no more than 100 full-time employees and taxable income of no more than $20 million that is independently owned and operated, has its principal place of business in this State, and is not delinquent or deficient with respect to the payment of any State tax. Qualified purchase means a purchase of goods or services from a small business, including, but not limited to:  agricultural or horticultural projects produced in this State; tools or manufacturing equipment; furniture or fixtures; supplies; products manufactured or substantially transformed in this State; professional services; technology services; construction, maintenance, repair, and other services related to the property used for the eligible business; communications or advertising services; and transportation or logistics services.  Qualified purchase does not include payments to a small business for utilities, rent, or a mortgage. Small business means a business with no more than 50 full-time employees, at least one of whom is a resident of this State, that is registered to do business in this State, maintains at least one physical business location in this State, is independently owned and operated, has its principal place of business in this State, and is not delinquent or deficient with respect to the payment of any State tax. Small business does not include any subsidiary or affiliate of the eligible business applying for a tax credit pursuant to this section. b.    A taxpayer that is an eligible business shall be allowed a credit against the tax imposed pursuant to section 5 of P.L.1945, c.162 (C.54:10A-5) in an amount equal to five percent of the qualified purchases incurred during the privilege period by the taxpayer, provided that the total value of credits allowed to a taxpayer during any privilege period shall not exceed $100,000. c.     When filing a return that includes a claim for a tax credit allowed pursuant to this section, a taxpayer shall include such business records as may be required by the director documenting the qualified purchases incurred by the taxpayer during the privilege period. d.    No tax credit shall be allowed pursuant to this section for any costs or expenses included in the calculation of any other tax credit or exemption granted pursuant to a claim made on a tax return filed with the director, or included in the calculation of an award of business assistance or incentive, for a period of time that coincides with the privilege period for which a tax credit authorized pursuant to this section is allowed. e.     The order of priority in which the tax credit allowed pursuant to this section and any other tax credits allowed by law may be taken shall be as prescribed by the director.  The amount of the tax credit applied under this section against the tax imposed pursuant to section 5 of P.L.1945, c.162 (C.54:10A-5) for a privilege period, when taken together with any other payments, credits, deductions, and adjustments allowed by law, shall not reduce the tax liability of the taxpayer to an amount less than the statutory minimum provided in subsection (e) of section 5 of P.L.1945, c.162 (C.54:10A-5).  The amount of the tax credit otherwise allowable under this section which cannot be applied for the privilege period due to the limitations of this subsection or under other provisions of P.L.1945, c.162 (C.54:10A-1 et seq.) may be carried forward, if necessary, to the seven privilege periods following the privilege period for which the tax credit was allowed, after which the credit shall expire. 2.    a. STATEMENT This bill creates a corporation business tax and gross income tax credit for eligible businesses that make qualified purchases from small businesses. Under the bill, the value of the credit is to equal five percent of the qualified purchases incurred by the eligible business in the tax period, up to a total value of $100,000 for an eligible business during any tax period.  When filing a tax return that includes a claim for the tax credit allowed under the bill, a taxpayer is required to include such business records as may be required by the Director of the Division of Taxation in the Department of the Treasury documenting the qualified purchases incurred by the taxpayer. Under the bill, an eligible business is a business with no more than 100 full-time employees and taxable income of no more than $20 million that is independently owned and operated, has its principal place of business in this State, and is not delinquent or deficient with respect to the payment of any State tax. Qualified purchase means a purchase of goods or services from a small business, including, but not limited to:  agricultural or horticultural projects produced in this State; tools or manufacturing equipment; furniture or fixtures; supplies; products manufactured or substantially transformed in this State; professional services; technology services; construction, maintenance, repair, and other services related to the property used for the eligible business; communications or advertising services; and transportation or logistics services.  Qualified purchase does not include payments to a small business for utilities, rent, or a mortgage. Small business means a business with no more than 50 full-time employees, at least one of whom is a resident of this State, that is registered to do business in this State, maintains at least one physical business location in this State, is independently owned and operated, has its principal place of business in this State, and is not delinquent or deficient with respect to the payment of any State tax. Small business does not include any subsidiary or affiliate of the eligible business applying for a tax credit under the bills provisions.
