A5770 ASSEMBLY, No. 5770 STATE OF NEW JERSEY 222nd LEGISLATURE INTRODUCED NOVEMBER 16, 2026 Sponsored by: Assemblywoman  TENNILLE R. MCCOY District 14 (Mercer and Middlesex) Assemblyman  BALVIR SINGH District 7 (Burlington) Assemblywoman  MARGIE DONLON, M.D. District 11 (Monmouth) SYNOPSIS Establishes requirements concerning celiac disease identification on patient intake forms; provides for voluntary type 1 diabetes and celiac disease screening guidance and public education. CURRENT VERSION OF TEXT As introduced. An Act concerning celiac disease and type 1 diabetes screening, identification, and education, and supplementing Title 26 of the Revised Statutes. Be It Enacted by the Senate and General Assembly of the State of New Jersey: 1.  The Legislature finds and declares that: a. Type 1 diabetes and celiac disease are distinct chronic autoimmune conditions that have overlapping genetic susceptibility associated with human leukocyte antigen markers, including HLA-DQ2 and HLA-DQ8.  Celiac disease is an immune-mediated condition in which ingestion of gluten may cause injury to the small intestine and impair nutrient absorption. b. Delayed or missed diagnosis of type 1 diabetes and celiac disease can result in significant adverse health consequences.  Celiac disease may present with gastrointestinal and non-gastrointestinal manifestations and may be mistaken for other conditions, contributing to diagnostic delay. c. Untreated celiac disease may be associated with malnutrition, iron-deficiency anemia, osteoporosis, neurologic complications, reproductive complications, autoimmune thyroid disease, and certain malignancies. d. Barriers to health care access, differences in clinical presentation, and socioeconomic disparities may contribute to delayed diagnosis and unequal access to screening, monitoring, and treatment for autoimmune conditions. e. Patient intake documentation that identifies celiac disease only under a general category for food allergies or food intolerances may fail to reflect that celiac disease is an autoimmune condition and may impede appropriate clinical recognition and accommodation. f. It is therefore in the public interest to improve the identification of celiac disease in health care settings, promote appropriate clinical evaluation, make voluntary screening guidance available to schools and community health settings, and increase public awareness of celiac disease and type 1 diabetes. 2.  As used in this act: "Celiac disease" means a chronic immune-mediated disorder in which ingestion of gluten in a genetically susceptible individual may result in injury to the small intestine and other clinical manifestations. "Department" means the Department of Health. "Health care provider" means a person or entity licensed, certified, or otherwise authorized pursuant to Title 26 or Title 45 of the Revised Statutes to provide health care services in this State. "Patient intake form" means a paper or electronic registration, medical history, or intake document completed by or on behalf of a patient in connection with the provision of health care services. 3.  a. The Commissioner of Health shall develop standards for patient intake forms used by health care providers to ensure that celiac disease may be identified as an autoimmune condition separately from food allergies and food intolerances. b. The standards developed pursuant to subsection a. of this section shall require a patient intake form that solicits information concerning a patient's diagnosed medical conditions to include a distinct means by which the patient may identify "Celiac Disease Autoimmune Condition."  Nothing in this subsection shall require a health care provider to include the designation on a form that does not otherwise solicit information concerning diagnosed medical conditions. c. A health care provider shall not classify a patient's diagnosis of celiac disease solely as a food allergy or food intolerance in a manner that obscures the diagnosis in the patient's intake documentation. d. When clinically appropriate, a health care provider treating a patient who has type 1 diabetes, celiac disease, or signs, symptoms, family history, or other risk factors associated with either condition may provide the patient, or the patient's parent or guardian, with information concerning appropriate diagnostic evaluation or screening.  The information may include discussion of serologic testing for celiac disease, including tissue transglutaminase immunoglobulin A and total serum immunoglobulin A testing, and any additional evaluation that may be clinically indicated. e. The Commissioner of Health shall make model language for compliance with this section available on the department's Internet website. 4. a. The Department of Health, in consultation with the Department of Education, shall develop guidelines concerning voluntary pediatric screening and early identification of type 1 diabetes and celiac disease.  The guidelines shall be made available to public school districts, school nurses, community health centers, and pediatric health care providers. b. The guidelines developed pursuant to subsection a. of this section shall include, at a minimum: (1) information concerning signs and symptoms that may warrant evaluation for type 1 diabetes or celiac disease; (2) information concerning family history and other recognized risk factors; (3) information concerning available screening and diagnostic tests; (4) recommendations concerning when a parent or guardian should be advised to consult the child's health care provider; and (5) information explaining that participation in any screening program is voluntary and that screening does not substitute for diagnosis or treatment by a licensed health care provider. c. A school district may participate in or facilitate a voluntary pediatric screening program consistent with guidelines developed pursuant to this section and applicable State and federal requirements concerning parental consent, student privacy, and the provision of health care services.  Nothing in this act shall be construed to require a student to undergo screening as a condition of school enrollment or attendance. d. Nothing in this section shall authorize a school nurse or other school employee to diagnose type 1 diabetes or celiac disease. 5. a. The Department of Health shall develop and publish educational materials concerning celiac disease and type 1 diabetes. The materials shall include information concerning: (1) common and less common clinical manifestations of celiac disease; (2) the autoimmune nature of celiac disease and its association with type 1 diabetes and other autoimmune conditions; (3) the potential consequences of delayed diagnosis; (4) appropriate consultation with a health care provider regarding screening and diagnostic evaluation; and (5) the distinction between celiac disease, a food allergy, and a food intolerance. b. The Department of Health, in consultation with the Department of Agriculture, may develop voluntary best-practice guidance for grocery retailers and food service establishments concerning the communication of information regarding gluten-containing foods and the risk of cross-contact.  The guidance shall be consistent with applicable federal and State food labeling and food safety requirements. 6.  The Commissioner of Health, pursuant to the "Administrative Procedure Act," P.L.1968, c.410 (C.52:14B-1 et seq.), and in consultation with the Commissioner of Education, the Director of the Division of Consumer Affairs, and the Secretary of Agriculture, as appropriate, shall adopt rules and regulations necessary to effectuate the purposes of this act. 7.  This act shall take effect on the first day of the seventh month next following the date of enactment, except that the Commissioner of Health, the Commissioner of Education, the Director of the Division of Consumer Affairs, and the Secretary of Agriculture may take such anticipatory administrative action in advance thereof as shall be necessary for the implementation of this act. STATEMENT This bill establishes requirements and guidelines concerning the identification, screening, and public understanding of celiac disease and type 1 diabetes. The bill requires the Commissioner of Health to develop standards for patient intake forms used by health care providers.  When an intake form solicits information concerning diagnosed medical conditions, the form would be required to provide a distinct means by which a patient may identify "Celiac Disease Autoimmune Condition."  The bill also provides that a diagnosis of celiac disease is not to be classified solely as a food allergy or food intolerance in a manner that obscures the diagnosis in the patient's intake documentation. The bill further directs the Department of Health, in consultation with the Department of Education, to develop guidelines concerning voluntary pediatric screening and early identification of type 1 diabetes and celiac disease.  The guidelines would address signs and symptoms, family history and other risk factors, available screening and diagnostic tests, consultation with a child's health care provider, and the voluntary nature of screening.  A school district would be permitted to participate in or facilitate a voluntary screening program consistent with applicable consent and privacy requirements. In addition, the Department of Health would develop educational materials concerning celiac disease and type 1 diabetes. In consultation with the Department of Agriculture, the department may develop voluntary best-practice guidance for grocery retailers and food service establishments concerning gluten information and cross-contact risks. The bill takes effect on the first day of the seventh month next following enactment and permits anticipatory administrative action before the effective date.
