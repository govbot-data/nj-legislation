STATEMENT This bill makes permanent a program in the Department of Education, originally established as a five-year pilot program, that authorizes the issuance of limited certificates of eligibility with advanced standing (CEAS) and limited certificates of eligibility (CE) and revises certain requirements under the program. A limited CEAS and a limited CE is available to individuals who may not meet one of the general requirements for a CEAS or CE and who are seeking employment in a school district, charter school, or renaissance school.  Individuals are eligible for a limited CEAS if they do not meet one of the following requirements: (1) the minimum grade point average requirement, and all other alternative grade point average requirements and exceptions; or (2) the requirement to achieve a minimum passing score on an appropriate State test of subject matter knowledge and any related alternative requirements or exceptions.  Individuals are eligible for a limited CE if they do not meet one of the following requirements: (1) any requirement to complete a minimum number of subject area course credits; (2) the minimum grade point average requirement; or (3) the requirement to achieve a minimum passing score on an appropriate State test of subject matter knowledge. Under the bill, a candidate for a limited CE or a limited CEAS is not required to complete a Commissioner of Education-approved test of basic reading, writing, and mathematical skills, in alignment with current law in which a candidate for an instructional certificate is not required to complete a commissioner-approved basic skills test. An individual who holds a limited CEAS or limited CE is only eligible to be hired at certain school districts, charter schools, and renaissance schools approved by the Commissioner of Education to be permitted to hire such individuals.  A district or school seeking to be approved to hire individuals holding the limited certificates is required to apply to the commissioner. An applicant is required to demonstrate a sufficient capability to provide support to new teachers and: a demographic disparity between the districts or schools student population and teaching staff; a shortage of bilingual education teachers; or a critical need to fill teacher vacancies or hardship caused by teacher vacancies. Currently, a limited CE and limited CEAS is provided to an individual under a five-year pilot program that began in the 2022-2023 school year.  This bill would make the program permanent.
