SYNOPSIS

     Aligns criteria for designating endangered and threatened wildlife and plant species with federal standards.

 

CURRENT VERSION OF TEXT

     As introduced.

  


An Act concerning regulations of endangered and threatened species, amending P.L.1973, c.309 and P.L.1989, c.56, and supplementing Title 4 of the Revised Statutes.

     "Take" means to harass, hunt, capture, kill, or attempt to harass, hunt, capture, or kill, wildlife;

     "Wildlife" means any wild mammal, bird, reptile, amphibian, fish, mollusk, crustacean or other wild animal or any part, product, egg or offspring or the dead body or parts thereof.

STATEMENT

    

     This bill requires the Commissioner of Environmental Protection to review, and if necessary, update, the lists of endangered wildlife and plant species, within one year after the bills enactment, and at least every 10 years thereafter.  This aligns State standards with existing federal regulations.

     The commissioner would be directed to delist a wildlife or plant species if: (1) the species becomes extinct; (2) the species recovers to the point that it no longer meets the definition of an endangered or threatened species; (3) information becomes available showing that the listed entity no longer meets the definition of an endangered or threatened species; or (4) information becomes available showing that the listed entity no longer meets the definition of a species.  The commissioner would be required to make this determination without consideration to the economic or other impacts of adding or delisting a wildlife or plant species.

     The commissioner would be required to establish programs for the conservation of endangered wildlife and plant species, including the acquisition of land or aquatic habitats, the designation of certain areas as essential habitats, and the adoption of habitat management protocols.

     Finally, the bill would require the Department of Environmental Protection (DEP) to promulgate rules and regulations authorizing the taking of black vultures, if the black vulture is delisted as a protected species under the federal Migratory Bird Treaty Act (16 U.S.C. s.703 et seq.), or if the federal Migratory Bird Treaty Act is repealed.
