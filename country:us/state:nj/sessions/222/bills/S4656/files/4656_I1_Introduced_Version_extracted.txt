S4656 SENATE, No. 4656 STATE OF NEW JERSEY 222nd LEGISLATURE INTRODUCED OCTOBER 1, 2026 Sponsored by: Senator  BOB SMITH District 17 (Middlesex and Somerset) SYNOPSIS Protect our Health from Plastics Act. CURRENT VERSION OF TEXT As introduced. An Act concerning plastic packaging and supplementing Title 13 of the Revised Statutes. Be It Enacted by the Senate and General Assembly of the State of New Jersey: 1.  This act shall be known and may be cited as the Protect our Health from Plastics Act. 2.  As used in this act: Chemical conversion means any method by which pre-consumer or post-consumer plastic waste is converted or altered through any non-mechanical process.  Chemical conversion shall include: (1) any process that results in the creation of hazardous chemicals by any means including, but not limited to, incineration, waste-to-energy, or waste-to-fuel; (2) any process used to transform plastic or plastic-derived materials into any of, but not limited to, the following: (a) plastic polymers; (b) plastic monomers; (c) chemicals; (d) waxes; (e) lubricants; (f) chemical feedstocks; (g) crude oil; (h) diesel; (i) gasoline; or (j) home heating oil; and (3) any process that subjects plastics to high heat, combustion, solvents, solutions, catalysts, or other chemical processes including, but not limited to: (a) gasification; (b) pyrolysis; (c) solvolysis; (d) hydropyrolysis; (e) methanolysis; (f) glycolosis (g) enzymatic breakdown; (h) combustion; (i) flash graphene conversion; (j) radical anion attack or electron-driven depolymerization; (k) thermal desorption; (l) continuous microwave assisted pyrolysis; (m) flash joule heating; and (n) dissolution Consumer product means an item manufactured, assembled, packaged or otherwise prepared for sale, including its product components, which is sold for personal use or consumption by individuals.  Consumer product shall not include used products offered for sale or resale. Covered plastic packaging means any plastic, in whole or in part, that is used for the containment, protection, handling, delivery, transport, distribution, or presentation of any consumer product or other product that is sold, offered for sale, imported, or distributed in the State.  Covered plastic packaging includes, but is not limited to:  (1) sales packaging, or primary packaging, intended to provide the user or consumer with the individual serving or unit of the product and most closely containing the product, food, or beverage; (2) grouped packaging, or secondary packaging, intended to bundle, sell in bulk, brand, or display the product; and (3) transport packaging, or tertiary packaging, intended to protect the product during transport.  Covered plastic packaging shall not include:  medical devices and packaging which are included with products regulated as a drug, medical device, or dietary supplement by the United States Food and Drug Administration under the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act, 21 U.S.C. s.301 et seq., or the federal Dietary Supplement Health and Education Act, Pub.L.103-417; packaging included with a combination product as defined at 21 C.F.R. s.3.2(e); animal biologics packaging, including vaccines, bacterins, antisera, diagnostic kits, and other products or biological origin, and other covered materials regulated by the United States Department of Agriculture under 21 U.S.C. ss.151-159, known as the Virus-Serum-Toxin Act; or packaging regulated by the Federal Insecticide, Fungicide, and Rodenticide Act, 7 U.S.C. s.136 et seq., or other applicable federal law, rule, or regulation. Department means the Department of Environmental Protection. Disposal means the landfilling or incineration of packaging material.  Disposal shall also include:  (1) the chemical conversion of discarded materials or discarded product component materials; (2) landfill disposal of discarded material or discarded product component materials, including the use of materials as landfill cover; and (3) composting, aerobic digestion, anaerobic digestion, or biodegradation in industrial or non-industrial facilities. Food contact plastic packaging means covered plastic packaging, or a packaging component, that is designed or intended to contain or come into direct contact with food, foodstuffs, beverages, or ingredients intended for human consumption. Food contact plastic packaging includes, but is not limited to, containers, bottles, cups, trays, wrappers, films, liners, closures, seals, coatings, and other packaging components that are designed or intended to directly contact food or beverages.  Food contact plastic packaging shall not include secondary or tertiary packaging that does not directly contact food or beverages. Licensee means a person holding the exclusive right to use a trademark or brand in the United States or the State in connection  In the instance where a private label product is being sold by a retailer, the producer is the person or entity who is the owner or licensee of the brand or trademark under which the product is used in a commercial enterprise, sold, offered for sale, or distributed in or into New Jersey, whether or not the trademark is registered in New Jersey.  Producer includes a franchisor of a STATEMENT This bill, to be known as the Protect our Health from Plastics Act, would require producers of plastic packaging to gradually reduce the amount of plastic packaging that they produce each year, and would prohibit the presence of certain toxic substances in plastic packaging. The bill would define a producer of plastic packaging according to the following hierarchy.  First, the producer would be the manufacturer of the product that is packaged in the plastic packaging.  Second, if the manufacturer is outside the State, the producer would be owner of the brand under which the product is sold. Finally, if the brand owner is outside the State, the producer would be the person who imports the product into the State (e.g., the distributor).  The bill would exempt certain small producers, as described in section 7 of the bill.  The bill would define plastic packaging to include primary packaging, which immediately surrounds the product, secondary packaging, which is used to bundle multiple products together, and tertiary packaging, which is used to protect products during transportation. The bill would require each producer of plastic packaging to register with the Department of Environmental Protection (DEP), pay a registration fee, and provide information about the amount of plastic packaging that they produce, which would serve as the baseline.  Each producer would be required to reduce the total amount of plastic packaging they produce according to the schedule described in section 4 of the bill, culminating in a 50 percent reduction 10 years after the producer registers with the DEP. Section 5 of the bill would prohibit the use of plastic packaging that contains the listed toxic substances, including antimony, arsenic, and cadmium.  The bill would also direct the DEP to prohibit the use of 10 additional toxic substances in plastic packaging five years after the bills enactment and every three years thereafter. The bill would direct the Attorney General to establish an independent enforcement office, to be known as the Office of Inspector General.  The bill would direct the Office of Inspector General to enforce the bills provisions and investigate potential violations. Finally, the bill would authorize the DEP and the Office of Inspector General to levy civil and civil administrative penalties of up to $50,000 per violation for persons that violate the bills provisions.  Each day of a continuing violation would be considered a separate violation.
