(New section) If any condemnor, as defined in section 2 of P.L.1971, c.361 (C.20:3-2), acquires property by condemnation for a stated public use, determines the property has become unsuited or inconvenient for that use, and attempts to convert the property, in whole or in part, to a different public use, the condemnor's governing body shall adopt a new resolution, ordinance, or other formal action identifying the new public use prior to conversion.  Any resolution, ordinance, or other formal action that states only that the property is being converted to a public use or purpose without specifying the exact use or purpose shall not satisfy this section, and the resolution, ordinance, or other formal action shall have no force or effect related to acquiring the real property.  This section shall apply to every condemnor, as defined in section 2 of P.L.1971, c.361 (C.20:3-2), regardless of whether that condemnor is also subject to section 5 of P.L.1971, c.199 (C.40A:12-5). 5.  This act shall take effect on the first day of the sixth month following enactment and shall apply to any resolution, ordinance, or other formal action authorizing an eminent domain acquisition of real property on or after that date. STATEMENT This bill requires any resolution, ordinance, or other formal action authorizing the acquisition of real property through eminent domain to specify the exact public use or uses for which the property is being acquired, at the time of acquisition.  The bill applies this requirement to every condemnor in the State, including: counties, municipalities, independent authorities, redevelopment entities, school districts, the State, and public utilities which possess eminent domain powers. The bill requires that the written offer required to be provided to a property owner by a condemnor during bona fide negotiation under the "Eminent Domain Act of 1971," P.L.1971, c.361 (C.20:3-1 et seq.) (EDA), shall disclose the exact public use for which the property is being acquisitioned.  The bill further requires a condemnor that has already acquired property for a State public use and later determines the property is to be converted to a different public use is required to first adopt a new resolution, ordinance, or other formal action identifying the new public use before the conversion occurs. The bill is in response to the August 10, 2026 decision by the New Jersey Supreme Court in Borough of Seaside Park v. Shree Jyoti, LLC, Docket No. A-2 25, in which the Court held neither the "Local Lands and Buildings Law," P.L.1971, c.199 (C.40A:12-1 et seq.) (LLBL), nor the EDA requires that the condemning municipality specify the intended public use for the property it is taking in the ordinance authorizing the acquisition of the property.  The Court asserted that municipalities should, as a matter of best practice, identify the intended public use for properties they seek to condemn as early as practicable, as doing so will ensure transparency, facilitate meaningful participation by affected property owners, and conserve municipal resources and uphold public trust in the government's exercise of eminent domain.  In Justice Hoffmans dissent, while he agreed with the majority's holding that the LLBL and EDA do not expressly require a municipality to state the public purpose for the taking in the authorizing ordinance, he noted that through the Borough's intentional lack of candid engagement, it failed to "turn square corners."  The "square corners" doctrine is a mandate that municipalities comport themselves with transparency, candor, and integrity.  Justice Hoffman recommended "that the Legislature consider requiring that municipalities disclose the intended public purpose for the property in question at the time of adopting an ordinance authorizing the taking of that property."  This bill codifies that requirement by amending the LLBL and the EDA and supplements the EDA applying the requirement to every condemnor in the State. The bill is to take effect on the first day of the sixth month following enactment and is to apply to any resolution, ordinance, or other formal action authorizing an eminent domain acquisition of real property on or after that date.  The bill also makes technical corrections.
