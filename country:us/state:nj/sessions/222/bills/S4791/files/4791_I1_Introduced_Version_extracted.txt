S4791 SENATE, No. 4791 STATE OF NEW JERSEY 222nd LEGISLATURE INTRODUCED OCTOBER 5, 2026 Sponsored by: Senator  VIN GOPAL District 11 (Monmouth) SYNOPSIS Makes any business owned by both women and minorities eligible for certain business certifications and set-aside programs. CURRENT VERSION OF TEXT As introduced. An Act concerning eligibility for certain business certifications and set-aside programs for women and minorities and amending various parts of the statutory law. Be It Enacted by the Senate and General Assembly of the State of New Jersey: 1.  Section 3 of P.L.1987, c.318 (C.5:10-21.1a) is amended to read as follows: 3.  The authority is authorized and directed to establish, prior to initiating any project on or after the effective date of this 1987 amendatory and supplementary act, small business, minority business, minority and womens business, and womens business set-aside contract goals and procedures in accordance with the provisions of the Set-Aside Act for Small Businesses, Female Businesses, and Minority Businesses, P.L. 1983, c. 482 (C. 52:32-17 et seq.). STATEMENT This bill makes any business that is owned by a certain combination of women and minorities eligible for certain business certifications and set-aside programs. Under current law, the Division of Revenue and Enterprise Services in the Department of the Treasury is permitted to offer certain certifications to businesses, including a minority business certification and a womens business certification.  Additionally, current law permits certain entities, and requires other certain entities, to designate a contract, or a portion thereof, as certain set-aside contracts, including womens business set aside contracts or minority business set-aside contracts, under certain circumstances. This bill expands the available types of business certifications and types of set-aside programs to include minority and womens businesses or minority and female businesses, where applicable.  Under the bill, a business with at least 51 percent of the ownership interest being held by a combination of minorities and women may qualify for a business certification or set-aside program, or both.
