(cf: P.L.1992, c.157, s.5) 2.    (New section) Notwithstanding any provision of any other law, rule, or regulation to the contrary, whenever any condemnor, as defined in section 2 of P.L.1971, c.361 (C.20:3-2) that is a municipality seeks to acquire property by condemnation, the ordinance authorizing the action shall specify the exact public use or uses for which the property is being acquired.  Any ordinance stating only that the acquisition is for a public use or purpose, without a specified use or purpose, shall not satisfy the requirements of P.L.    , c.    (C.        ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill). 3.    This act shall take effect immediately. STATEMENT This bill requires municipalities to specify, in any ordinance implementing the use of eminent domain, the explicit particular use intended for that property.  The requirement, under the "Local Lands and Buildings Law," P.L.1971, c.199 (C.40A:12-1 et seq.), is to apply to acquisition of any real property, capital improvement, or personal property within its jurisdiction to be acquired under the "Eminent Domain Act of 1971," P.L.1971, c.361 (C.20:3-1 et seq.). The bill also supplements the "Eminent Domain Act of 1971" to specify that when any condemnor that is a municipality seeks to acquire property by condemnation, the ordinance authorizing the action is to specify the exact public use or uses for which the property is being acquired. In Borough of Seaside Park v. Shree Jyoti, LLC, Docket No. A-2-25, decided on August 10, 2026, the New Jersey Supreme Court ruled 6-1 that municipalities do not have to state a specific public use or purpose in an ordinance when starting a condemnation action because such a requirement is not specified under current law.  This bill amends current law to require municipalities to specify in any ordinance implementing eminent domain the particular use intended for that property. The bill also makes technical corrections.
