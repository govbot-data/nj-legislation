STATEMENT This bill amends P.L.2023, c.240 (C.26:1A-131.1 et seq.) to require the public awareness campaign established pursuant to the law to include information on uterine fibroids and premenstrual dysphoric disorder.  Currently, the public awareness campaign provides information to the public on endometriosis, PCOS, and menstrual toxic shock syndrome but is not required to provide information on any other disorders and conditions. The bill also amends the law to require the Department of Health (DOH) to make available to:  (1) public libraries, institutions of higher education, local health departments, community health centers, public health clinics, shelters and organizations serving individuals experiencing homelessness, and other public or nonprofit entities that provide menstrual products to the public, information about common menstrual disorders and menstruation-related disorders and conditions; and (2) school districts that ensure students have access to menstrual products pursuant to P.L.2023, c.147 (C.18A:33-29 et seq.) educational resources about common menstrual disorders and menstruation-related disorders and conditions. Current law requires the DOH to make the information provided to the public available electronically, but does not:  (1) mandate that the DOH make the information available in print; (2) specify the entities or organizations that are to receive the printed information; or (3) require the DOH to provide certain information about menstrual and menstruation-related disorders and conditions to school districts that ensure students access to menstrual products. The bill amends P.L.2023, c.147 (C.18A:33-29 et seq.) to require school districts prominently display, in the school bathrooms where students have access to menstrual products, and disseminate, in writing, to the appropriate students, a notice in English, Spanish, and any other language spoken by the majority of the students in the district, with the information contained in the educational resources made available by the public awareness campaign. The bill appropriates $500,000 to the DOH for preparation and dissemination of the information and educational resources made available by the public awareness campaign.
