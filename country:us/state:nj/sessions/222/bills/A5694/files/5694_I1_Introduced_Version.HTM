SYNOPSIS

     Extends eligibility for sales and use tax exemption certificate program administered by EDA to include certain projects undertaken by clean energy product manufacturers.

STATEMENT

 

     This bill extends eligibility for the sales and use tax exemption certificate program administered by the New Jersey Economic Development Authority (EDA) to purchases made by clean energy product manufacturers related to the construction or renovation of specified business facilities. 

     A clean energy product manufacturer is defined under the bill as a business engaged in the production or assembly of goods by transforming raw materials or sub-components into components for renewable energy, such as offshore wind, solar, geothermal, green hydrogen, nuclear energy, fuel cells, battery storage, or other clean energy manufacturing. The bill specifies that a clean energy manufacturer would not include businesses engaged in retail, wholesale, packaging, software development, resource extraction, or waste incineration.

     Under current law, qualifying businesses may apply to the EDA for a sales and use tax exemption certificate to be used for the purchase of eligible property located or placed at the site of an approved project.  The exemption certificate allows businesses to purchase machinery, equipment, furniture, fixtures, and building materials without the imposition of tax on the sale of those items.  In order to be eligible for the program, a business is required to have operated continuously in the State for at least 10 years, while also satisfying requirements related to the number of full-time employees employed by the business in the State and the location and characteristics of the proposed business facility to be constructed or renovated under the project.  Generally, a project is required to be situated in Planning Area 1 or 2, as defined in the State Development and Redevelopment Plan adopted by the State Planning Commission.  Planning Areas are regions of the State with shared characteristics that inform where growth or conservation should occur, with a preference for concentrating development primarily in Metropolitan and Suburban Areas, also known as Planning Areas 1 and 2 respectively.  In certain instances, a business project is not required to be located in either of these planning areas provided that the project involves renovation or expansion of an existing facility if all other applicable program criteria are satisfied.

     The bill would extend eligibility for the EDAs sales and use tax exemption program to a clean energy product manufacturer that undertakes an eligible project.  Specifically, the bill requires the project to involve the construction of a new facility, or a substantial rehabilitation of an existing facility, that will predominantly serve as a new research and development facility or new manufacturing facility, which facility will house a minimum of 200 new, full-time employees for no less than five years, and which project has an expected cost of $500 million or more.  The bill exempts clean energy product manufactures from certain existing requirements of the program, such as the requirement to: (1) demonstrate that receiving the exemption certificate is a material factor in the decision of the business to locate the project in the State; (2) have operated continuously in the State for at least 10 years prior to applying for the program; and (3) undertake a business project involving the construction of specified new facilities by a clean energy product manufacturer within Planning Areas 1 or 2.
