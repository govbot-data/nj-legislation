S4858 SENATE, No. 4858 STATE OF NEW JERSEY 222nd LEGISLATURE INTRODUCED OCTOBER 19, 2026 Sponsored by: Senator  NICHOLAS P. SCUTARI District 22 (Somerset and Union) SYNOPSIS Establishes Minor Protection Order Act of 2026; makes appropriation. CURRENT VERSION OF TEXT As introduced. An Act establishing the Minor Protection Order Act of 2026, supplementing Title 2A of the New Jersey Statutes, amending various parts of the statutory law, and making an appropriation. Be It Enacted by the Senate and General Assembly of the State of New Jersey: 1.    (New section)  This act shall be known and may be cited as the Minor Protection Order Act of 2026. 2.    (New section) a.  A parent or guardian of a minor who is at least 14 years of age and alleges to be the victim of conduct set forth in paragraph (1) or (2) of this subsection may file an application with the Superior Court pursuant to the Rules of Court alleging the commission of this conduct and seeking a minor protection order against a minor respondent who is at least 14 years of age. (1)  In cases where there is a dating or domestic relationship, including a current or former dating relationship, the minor respondent committed or attempted to commit a delinquent act which if committed by an adult would constitute homicide pursuant to N.J.S.2C:11-1 et seq., assault pursuant to N.J.S.2C:12-1, terroristic threats pursuant to N.J.S.2C:12-3, kidnapping pursuant to N.J.S.2C:13-1, criminal restraint pursuant to N.J.S.2C:13-2, false imprisonment pursuant to N.J.S.2C:13-3, sexual assault pursuant to N.J.S.2C:14-2, criminal sexual contact pursuant to N.J.S.2C:14-3, lewdness pursuant to N.J.S.2C:14-4, criminal mischief pursuant to N.J.S.2C:17-3, burglary pursuant to N.J.S.2C:18-2, criminal trespass pursuant to N.J.S.2C:18-3, harassment pursuant to N.J.S.2C:33-4, stalking pursuant to section 1 of P.L.1992, c.209 (C.2C:12-10), criminal coercion pursuant to N.J.S.2C:13-5, robbery pursuant to N.J.S.2C:15-1, cyber-harassment pursuant to section 1 of P.L.2013, c.272 (C.2C:33-4.1), or any other crime involving risk of death or serious bodily injury to a person who qualifies for relief. When it is alleged that the conduct set forth in this paragraph has been committed against a minor by a parent, guardian, or other person having care, custody and control of that child as defined in R.S.9:6-2 or by a sibling, an applicant seeking a minor protection order shall not proceed under the provisions of P.L.    , c. (C.        ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill), but shall report the incident to the Department of Children and Families for appropriate action. (2) In cases where there is a non-domestic relationship and there is no dating or former dating relationship, nonconsensual sexual contact, sexual penetration, or lewdness, or any attempt at this conduct, or stalking or cyber-harassment as those terms are defined in subsection h. of this section. b.  The application shall be filed with the Superior Court during regular court hours.  The application shall be filed by a parent or legal guardian on behalf of the minor alleged victim.  The application shall set forth the following information to the extent the information is known to the alleged victim: (1)  the name, address, and date of birth of the respondent; (2)  the name and address of the respondents parent or guardian and if the respondent is in the custody of some other person, the name and address of the custodian; (3)  the date, time, manner, and place of the acts alleged as the basis of the application; (4)  a citation of the law allegedly violated by the respondent; and (5)  the signature of the alleged victim. c.     An application for a temporary minor protection order may be filed in the county where the minor alleged victim who is seeking the minor protection order resides; the county where the alleged conduct occurred; or the county where the respondent resides. d.    Venue shall remain in the county where the temporary minor protection order is issued, unless there is good cause to transfer the case to the county where the minor respondent resides. e.     Every application made pursuant to P.L.    , c.    (C.        ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill) shall be docketed in the Superior Court, Chancery Division, Family Part and designated under a distinct case type. f.     An applicant may seek a minor protection order pursuant to  P.L.    , c.    (C.        ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill) and the court may issue a minor protection order regardless of whether delinquency charges based on the incident were filed and regardless of the disposition of the charges.  In cases where delinquency charges arising from the incident have been filed, the prosecutor: (1) shall advise the victim of the right to apply for a temporary minor protection order pursuant to P.L.    , c.    (C.        ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill); or (2) may make an appropriate application to the court for a no contact order. g.    The filing of an application pursuant to P.L.    , c.    (C.        ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill) shall not prevent the filing of a delinquency complaint or the institution or maintenance of a juvenile delinquency proceeding based on the same conduct. h.    As used in this section: "Cause a reasonable person to fear" means to cause fear which a reasonable victim, similarly situated, would have under the circumstances. "Cyber-harassment" means conduct that occurs while making one or more communications in an online capacity via any electronic device or through a social networking site and with the purpose to harass another, that involves: threatening to inflict injury or physical harm to any person or the property of any person; knowingly sending, posting, commenting, requesting, suggesting, or proposing any lewd, indecent, or obscene material to or about a person with the intent to emotionally harm a reasonable person or place a reasonable person in fear of physical or emotional harm to the reasonable person; or threatening to commit any crime against a person or the person's property. "Emotional distress" means significant mental suffering or distress. "Intimate parts" means the following body parts: sexual organs, genital area, anal area, inner thigh, groin, buttock, or breast of a person. "Lewdness" means the exposing of the genitals for the purpose of arousing or gratifying the sexual desire of the actor or of any other person. "Repeatedly" means on two or more occasions. "Sexual contact" means an intentional touching by the victim or actor, either directly or through clothing, of the victim's or actor's intimate parts for the purpose of degrading or humiliating the victim or sexually arousing or sexually gratifying the actor. "Sexual penetration" means vaginal intercourse, cunnilingus, fellatio, or anal intercourse between persons or insertion of the hand, finger, or object into the anus or vagina either by the actor or upon the actor's instruction. "Stalking" means purposefully or knowingly engaging in a course of conduct directed at or toward a person that would cause a reasonable person to fear for the reasonable person's own safety or the safety of a third person, or suffer other emotional distress, because the conduct involves: repeatedly maintaining a visual or physical proximity to a person; directly, indirectly, or through third parties, by any action, method, device, or means, following, monitoring, observing, surveilling, threatening, or communicating to or about a person, or interfering with a person's property; repeatedly committing harassment against a person; or repeatedly conveying, or causing to be conveyed, verbal or written threats or threats conveyed by any other means of communication or threats implied by conduct or a combination thereof directed at or towards a person. 3.    (New section) a.  (1) An applicant may seek emergency, ex parte relief in the form of a temporary minor protection order.  A judge of the Superior Court may enter an emergency ex parte order when necessary to protect the safety and well-being of an alleged victim on whose behalf the relief is sought.  The court shall, upon consideration of the application, order emergency ex parte relief in the form of a temporary minor protection order if the court determines that the applicant is a victim of conduct set forth in paragraph (1) or (2) of subsection a. of section 2 of P.L.    , c.    (C.        ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill) and qualifies for relief pursuant to section 2 of P.L.    , c.    (C.        ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill). (2) The court may issue a temporary minor protection order upon sworn testimony of the applicant or an application of an alleged victim who is not physically present, pursuant to the Rules of Court, or by a person who represents an alleged victim who is physically or mentally incapable of personally filing. (3)  The court shall render a decision on the application and issue a temporary minor protection order, where appropriate, in an expedited manner.  An order for emergency, ex parte relief shall be granted upon good cause shown and shall remain in effect until a judge of the Superior Court issues a further order. b.    An order granting emergency relief, together with the complaint, shall immediately be forwarded to the appropriate law enforcement agency for service on the respondent and the parent or legal guardian of the respondent, and to the police of the municipality in which the victim resides or is sheltered, and shall immediately be served upon the respondent and the parent or guardian of the respondent along with a copy of the translated order, if applicable, by the police, except that an order issued during regular court hours may be forwarded to the sheriff for immediate service upon the respondent and the parent or guardian of the respondent in accordance with the Rules of Court.  If personal service cannot be affected upon the respondent and the parent or legal guardian of the respondent, the court may order other appropriate substituted service. The alleged victim shall not be asked or required to serve any order on the respondent or the parent or legal guardian of the respondent. The court shall also provide a copy of the temporary minor protection order to the school attended by the victim and the school attended by the respondent. 4.    (New section) a. Following the entry of a temporary minor protection order, a hearing for a final minor protection order shall be held in the Superior Court within 10 business days of the filing of the application for the temporary minor protection order pursuant to section 3 of P.L.    , c.    (C.        ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill) in the county where the temporary minor protection order was issued, unless good cause is shown for the hearing to be held elsewhere.  A copy of the application shall be served on the respondent and the parent or legal guardian of the respondent in conformity with the Rules of Court.  A parent or legal guardian of both the minor victim and minor respondent shall be present at the hearing.  The court may, in its discretion, appoint a guardian ad litem for either party when the court deems it necessary or appropriate. b.    At the hearing, the standard for proving the allegations made in the application for a final minor protection order shall be a preponderance of the evidence.  The court shall consider, at a minimum, the following factors: (1) the occurrence of one or more acts set forth in paragraph (1) or (2) of subsection a. of section 2 of P.L.    c.    (C.        ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill) against the alleged victim; (2) any prior history of or threats to commit one or more acts set forth in in paragraph (1) or (2) of subsection a. of section 2 of P.L.    c.    (C.        ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill) against the alleged victim; and (3) the future risk to the safety or well-being of the alleged victim. c.     The court shall issue a final minor protection order only after a finding or an admission is made that the respondent committed an act set forth in paragraph (1) or (2) of subsection a. of section 2 of P.L.    , c.    (C.        ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill) against the alleged victim. d.    A final minor protection order may include any relief the court deems necessary to protect the victim including, but not limited to: (1)  restraining the respondent from committing any further acts of violence against the victim or any prohibited conduct; (2)  prohibiting all forms of contact with the victim, whether direct or indirect, including in-person, telephonic, electronic, or third-party communications; (3)  restricting the respondent from entering specified locations, including the victims residence, school, workplace, or other locations regularly frequented by the victim; (4)  prohibiting the respondent from stalking, harassing, or threatening the victim; (5)  ordering the respondent to undergo counseling, treatment, or an evaluation, including psychological, psychiatric, or substance abuse assessments; and (6)  prohibiting or limiting the respondents use of social media. e.     A final minor protection order issued pursuant to this section shall: (1) prohibit the respondent from purchasing, owning, possessing, accessing, or controlling firearms or weapons; (2) require the immediate surrender of any firearms or weapons in the respondents possession; and (3)  include any other safeguards, as determined appropriate by the court. A law enforcement officer shall accompany the respondent and the parent or legal guardian of the respondent, or may proceed without the respondent and the parent or legal guardian of the respondent if necessary, to a location where the judge has reasonable cause to believe any firearm or other weapon belonging to or in the possession of the respondent is located, to ensure that the respondent does not gain access to any firearm or other weapon, and that the firearm or other weapon is appropriately surrendered in accordance with the order. f.     A copy of the final minor protection order shall immediately be forwarded to the appropriate law enforcement agency for service on the respondent and the parent or guardian of the respondent, and to the police of the municipality in which the victim resides or is sheltered, and shall immediately be served upon the respondent and the parent or guardian of the respondent along with a copy of the translated order, if applicable, by the police, except that an order issued during regular court hours may be forwarded to the sheriff for immediate service upon the respondent and the parent or guardian of the respondent in accordance with the Rules of Court.  If personal service cannot be affected upon the respondent and the parent or guardian of the respondent, the court may order other appropriate substituted service. The alleged victim shall not be asked or required to serve any order on the respondent or the parent of guardian of the respondent.  The court shall also provide a copy of the final minor protection order to the school attended by the victim and the school attended by the respondent. g.    (1)  A final minor protection order issued pursuant to this section shall remain in effect for a period of six months to one year.     (2)  In cases where a final minor protection order is entered against a respondent who is at least 17.5 years old when the order was issued, the final minor protection order may remain in effect until the respondent is 18.5 years of age. h.    Upon application to the court by the victim, the court may extend the order in six-month increments until the respondents 18th birthday if the court finds good cause for the continuation of the order, including the continued risk to the safety or well-being of the victim. i.     Either party may file a petition with the court to dissolve or modify a final minor protection order. Upon application for modification or dissolution of the final minor protection order by the victim or the respondent, the court shall conduct a hearing to consider whether a material change in circumstances has occurred since the issuance of the final minor protection order which would make its continued enforcement inequitable, oppressive, or unjust taking into account the current status of the parties, including the desire of the victim for the continuation of the final minor protection order, the potential for contact between the parties, the history of the respondents violations of the minor protection order or adjudications of delinquency, and any other factors that the court may find relevant to protecting the safety and well-being of the victim. 5.    (New section) a. Any temporary or final minor protection order issued pursuant to P.L.    , c. (C.        ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill) shall be in effect throughout the State and shall be enforced by all law enforcement officers. b.    A respondents violation of a temporary or final minor protection order issued pursuant to P.L.    , c.    (C.        ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill) shall constitute a crime or offense under subsection f. of N.J.S.2C:29-9.  Each order shall state that a violation constitutes this crime or offense. c.     All temporary and final minor protection orders shall be entered into the juvenile central registry. d.    A temporary minor protection order shall be removed from the juvenile central registry upon dismissal of the order.  A final minor protection order shall be removed from the juvenile central registry upon the respondent reaching the age of 18, provided that the final minor protection order has been dissolved.  In any case where a final minor protection order remains in effect beyond the respondents 18th birthday, including under circumstances set forth in paragraph (2) of subsection g. of section 4 of P.L.    , c.    (C.        ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill), the final minor protection order shall be removed from the juvenile central registry upon dissolution of the order. 6.    (New section) If a criminal or juvenile delinquency complaint arising out of the same incident which is the subject matter of an application for a minor protection order pursuant to P.L.    , c.    (C.        ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill) has been filed, testimony given by the victim or respondent in the minor protection order matter shall not be used in a simultaneous or subsequent criminal proceeding or juvenile delinquency proceeding against the respondent, other than proceedings for contempt of a minor protection order and where it would otherwise be admissible hearsay under the rules of evidence that govern where a party is unavailable. 7.    (New section) a.  The Division of Criminal Justice shall develop and approve a training course and curriculum regarding the provisions of P.L.    , c.    (C.        ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill).  The Division of Criminal Justice shall distribute the curriculum to all local police agencies and county prosecutors.  The Attorney General shall be responsible for ensuring that all law enforcement officers and assistant county prosecutors have completed the training, in accordance with standards and procedures established by the Attorney General. b.    The Administrative Director of the Courts shall develop and approve a training course and curriculum regarding the provisions of P.L.    , c.    (C.        ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill) for all Superior Court judges responsible for issuing or denying minor protection orders and any appropriate judicial personnel who are involved with the intake and processing of applications for minor protection orders.  The Administrative Director of the Courts shall be responsible for ensuring that these Superior Court judges and judicial personnel have completed the training in accordance with standards and procedures established by the Administrative Director of the Courts. 8.    N.J.S.2C:29-9 is amended to read as follows: 2C:29-9.     Contempt. a. (1) Except as provided in paragraph (2) of this subsection, a  person is guilty of a crime of the fourth degree if the person purposely or knowingly disobeys a judicial order or protective order, pursuant to section 1 of P.L.1985, c.250 (C.2C:28-5.1), or hinders, obstructs, or impedes the effectuation of a judicial order or the exercise of jurisdiction over any person, thing, or controversy by a court, administrative body, or investigative entity, or purposely or knowingly violates a condition to avoid all contact with an alleged victim or a condition of home detention with or without the use of an approved electronic monitoring device, ordered pursuant to subparagraph (b) of paragraph (1) or subparagraph (k) of paragraph (2) of subsection b. of section 3 of P.L.2014, c.31 (C.2A:162-17), when the conduct which constitutes the violation could also constitute a crime or a disorderly persons offense. (2) In all other cases a person is guilty of a disorderly persons offense if that person purposely or knowingly violates a condition to avoid contact with an alleged victim or a condition of home detention with or without the use of an approved electronic monitoring device. b.    (1) Except as provided in paragraph (2) of this subsection, a person is guilty of a crime of the fourth degree if that person purposely or knowingly violates any provision in an order entered under the provisions of the "Prevention of Domestic Violence Act of 1991," P.L.1991, c.261 (C.2C:25-17 et al.) or an order entered under the provisions of a substantially similar statute under the laws of another state or the United States when the conduct which constitutes the violation could also constitute a crime or a disorderly persons offense. Orders entered pursuant to paragraphs (3), (4), (5), (8), and (9) of subsection b. of section 13 of P.L.1991, c.261 (C.2C:25-29) or substantially similar orders entered under the laws of another state or the United States shall be excluded from the provisions of this paragraph. (2) In all other cases a person is guilty of a disorderly persons offense if that person purposely or knowingly violates an order entered under the provisions of the "Prevention of Domestic Violence Act of 1991," P.L.1991, c.261 (C.2C:25-17 et al.) or an order entered under the provisions of a substantially similar statute under the laws of another state or the United States. Orders entered pursuant to paragraphs (3), (4), (5), (8), and (9) of subsection b. of section 13 of P.L.1991, c.261 (C.2C:25-29) or substantially similar orders entered under the laws of another state or the United States shall be excluded from the provisions of this paragraph. c.     A person is guilty of a crime of the third degree if that person purposely or knowingly violates any provision in an order entered under the provisions of section 3 of P.L.1996, c.39 (C.2C:12-10.1) or section 2 of P.L.1999, c.47 (C.2C:12-10.2) or an order entered under the provisions of a substantially similar statute under the laws of another state or the United States when the conduct which constitutes the violation could also constitute a crime or a disorderly persons offense. d.    (1) Except as provided in paragraph (2) of this subsection, a person is guilty of a crime of the fourth degree if that person purposely or knowingly violates any provision in an order entered under the provisions of P.L.2015, c.147 (C.2C:14-13 et al.) or an order entered under the provisions of a substantially similar statute under the laws of another state or the United States when the conduct which constitutes the violation could also constitute a crime or a disorderly persons offense. (2) In all other cases a person is guilty of a disorderly persons offense if that person purposely or knowingly violates an order entered under the provisions of P.L.2015, c.147 (C.2C:14-13 et al.) or an order entered under the provisions of a substantially similar statute under the laws of another state or the United States. e.     A person is guilty of a crime of the fourth degree if the person purposely or knowingly violates any provision of an order entered under the provisions of the "Extreme Risk Protective Order Act of 2018," P.L.2018, c.35 (C.2C:58-20 et al.) or an order entered under the provisions of a substantially similar statute under the laws of another state or the United States. f.     A person is guilty of a crime of the fourth degree if the person purposely or knowingly violates any provision of a temporary or final minor protection order entered pursuant to the provisions of P.L.    , c.    (C.        ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill) or an order entered pursuant to the provisions of a substantially similar statute under the laws of another state or the United States when the conduct which constitutes the violation could also constitute a crime or disorderly persons offense. In all other cases, a person is guilty of a disorderly persons offense if the person purposely or knowingly violates any provision of a temporary or final minor protection order entered pursuant to the provisions of P.L.    , c.    (C.        ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill) or an order entered under the provisions of a substantially similar statute under the laws of another state or the United States. As used in this section, "state" means a state of the United States, the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, the United States Virgin Islands, or any territory or insular possession subject to the jurisdiction of the United States. The term includes an Indian tribe or band, or Alaskan native village, which is recognized by a federal law or formally acknowledged by a state. (cf:  P.L.2023, c.46, s.1) 9.    Section 2 of P.L.2015, c.147 (C.2C:14-14) is amended to read as follows: 2.    Application for Temporary Protective Order. a. (1) Any person alleging to be a victim of nonconsensual sexual contact, sexual penetration, or lewdness, or any attempt at such conduct, or stalking or cyber-harassment, and who is not eligible for a restraining order as a "victim of domestic violence" as defined by the provisions of subsection d. of section 3 of P.L.1991, c.261 (C.2C:25-19), may, except as provided in subsection b. of this section, file an application with the Superior Court pursuant to the Rules of Court alleging the commission of such conduct or attempted conduct and seeking a temporary protective order. As used in this section and in sections 3, 4, and 8 of P.L.2015, c.147 (C.2C:14-15, C.2C:14-16, and C.2C:14-20): "Sexual contact" means an intentional touching by the victim or actor, either directly or through clothing, of the victim's or actor's intimate parts for the purpose of degrading or humiliating the victim or sexually arousing or sexually gratifying the actor. (cf: P.L.2025, c.35, s.18) 10.  Section 3 of P.L.1991, c.261 (C.2C:25-19) is amended to read as follows: 3.    As used in this act: a.     "Domestic violence" means the occurrence of one or more of the following acts inflicted upon a person protected under this act by an adult or an emancipated minor: (1)   Homicide N.J.S.2C:11-1 et seq. STATEMENT This bill establishes the Minor Protection Order Act of 2026.  Under the provisions of this bill, a parent or guardian of a minor who is at least 14 years of age and alleges to be the victim of certain conduct may file an application with the Superior Court alleging the commission of the conduct and seeking a minor protection order against a respondent who is at least 14 years of age. Under the bill, conduct means the following: 1) in cases where there is a dating or domestic relationship, including a current or former dating relationship, the respondent committed or attempted to commit a delinquent act which if committed by an adult would constitute homicide, assault, terroristic threats, kidnapping, criminal restraint, false imprisonment, sexual assault, criminal sexual contact, lewdness, criminal mischief, burglary, criminal trespass, harassment, stalking, criminal coercion, robbery, cyber-harassment, or any other crime involving risk of death or serious bodily injury to a person who qualifies for relief under the bill; or (2) in cases where there is a non-domestic relationship and there is no dating or former dating relationship, nonconsensual sexual contact, sexual penetration, or lewdness, or any attempt at such conduct, or stalking or cyber-harassment as these terms are defined under the bill. The application is to be filed during regular court hours and is to be filed by a parent or legal guardian on behalf of the victim. The bill authorizes an applicant to seek a minor protection order and the court to issue a minor protection order regardless of whether delinquency charges based on the incident were filed and regardless of the disposition of the charges.  In cases where delinquency charges arising from the incident have been filed, the prosecutor is required to advise the victim of the right to apply for a temporary minor protection order or may make an appropriate application to the court for a no contact order.  Further, the filing of an application does not prevent the filing of a delinquency complaint or the institution or maintenance of a juvenile delinquency proceeding based on the same conduct. Under the bill, an applicant may seek emergency, ex parte relief in the nature of a temporary minor protection order.  A judge may enter an emergency ex parte order when necessary to protect the safety and well-being of the alleged victim on whose behalf the relief is sought.  The court is required to, upon consideration of the application, issue a temporary minor protection order if the court determines the applicant is a victim of conduct set forth in the bill and qualifies for relief under the bills provisions.  The court may issue a temporary minor protection order upon sworn testimony of the applicant or an application of an alleged victim who is not physically present, pursuant to the Rules of Court, or by a person who represents an alleged victim who is physically or mentally incapable of personally filing.  The order would be granted upon good cause shown and is to remain in effect until a judge of the Superior Court issues a further order. Following the issuance of the temporary minor protection order, a hearing for a final minor protection order is required to be held in the Superior Court within 10 business days of the filing of the application for the temporary minor protection order.  A copy of the application is to be served on the respondent and his or her parent or legal guardian in conformity with the Rules of Court.  A parent or legal guardian of both the minor victim and the minor respondent are required to be present at the hearing.  The court may, in its discretion, appoint a guardian ad litem for either party when the court deems it necessary or appropriate. Further, the bill provides that at the hearing, the standard for proving the allegations made in the application for a final minor protection order is preponderance of the evidence.  The court is to consider the following factors: 1) the occurrence of one or more acts set forth under the bill against the alleged victim; 2) any prior history of or threats to commit one or more acts set forth under the bill against the alleged victim; and 3) the future risk to the safety or well-being of the alleged victim. Under the bill, a court is to issue a final minor protection order only after a finding or an admission is made that the respondent committed conduct set forth under the bill against the alleged victim. A final minor protection order may include any relief the court deems necessary to protect the victim including: 1) restraining the respondent from committing any further acts of violence against the victim or any prohibited conduct; 2) prohibiting all forms of contact with the victim, whether direct or indirect, including in-person, telephonic, electronic, or third-party communications; 3) restricting the respondent from entering specified locations, including the victims residence, school, workplace, or other locations regularly frequented by the victim; 4) prohibiting the respondent from stalking, harassing, or threatening the victim; 5) ordering the respondent to undergo counseling, treatment, or an evaluation, including psychological, psychiatric, or substance abuse assessments; and 6) prohibiting or limiting the respondents use of social media. The bill further provides that the order is required to: 1) prohibit the respondent from purchasing, owning, possessing, accessing, or controlling firearms; 2) require the immediate surrender of any firearms or weapons in the respondents possession; and 3) include any other safeguards, as determined appropriate by the court. Under the bill, a final minor protection order is to remain in effect for a period of six months to one year.  In cases where a final minor protection order is entered against a respondent who is at least 17.5 years old when the order was issued, the final minor protection order may remain in effect up until the respondent is 18.5 years of age.  Upon application to the court by the victim, the court may extend the order in six-month increments until the respondents 18th birthday if the court finds good cause for the continuation of the order, including the continued risk to the safety or well-being of the victim. The bill authorizes either party to file a petition with the court to dissolve or modify a final minor protection order.  Upon application for modification or dissolution of the order by the victim or respondent, the court is required to conduct a hearing to consider whether a material change in circumstances has occurred since the issuance of the minor protection order which would make its continued enforcement inequitable, oppressive, or unjust taking into account the current status of the parties, including the desire of the victim for the continuation of the minor protection order, the potential for contact between the parties, the history of the respondents violations of the minor protection order or adjudications of delinquency, and any other factors that the court may find relevant to protecting the safety and well-being of the victim. Under the bill, a respondents violation of a temporary or final minor protection order constitutes contempt, which is a crime of the fourth degree if the conduct which constitutes the violation could also constitute a crime or disorderly persons offense or, in all other cases, a disorderly persons offense. Further, the bill requires all temporary and final minor protection orders to be entered into the juvenile central registry.  A temporary minor protection order is to be removed from the juvenile central registry upon dismissal of the order.  A final minor protection order is to be removed from the juvenile central registry upon the respondent reaching the age of 18, provided that the final minor protection order has been dissolved.  In any case where a final minor protection order remains in effect beyond the respondents 18th birthday, the final minor protection order is to be removed from the juvenile central registry upon dissolution of the order. This bill also sets forth requirements for training for law enforcement, assistant prosecutors, and certain Superior Court judges and judicial personnel regarding the bills provisions. Finally, under the bill, there is appropriated from the General Fund to the Administrative Office of the Courts such funds as are necessary for the implementation of the bill, as certified by the Administrative Director of the Courts and subject to the approval of the Director of the Division of Budget and Accounting in the Department of the Treasury.
