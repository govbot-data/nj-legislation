STATEMENT This bill prohibits a covered online service provider, including social media companies, from targeting advertisements for cosmetic products containing dangerous chemicals to minors. The bill defines cosmetic products containing dangerous chemicals as any item or component of an item, other than soap, that is intended to be rubbed on, poured on, sprinkled on, sprayed on, introduced into, or otherwise applied to the human body for cleansing, beautifying, promoting attractiveness, or altering appearance that includes certain chemical substances, such as quaternium-15 and certain per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) and their salts.  A cosmetic product is not considered to be dangerous if the product includes an unavoidable trace quantity of a chemical substance due to the impurities of natural or synthetic ingredients or the manufacturing process, storage, or migration from packaging of the product. A violation of the bills provisions is an unlawful practice under the consumer fraud act.  An unlawful practice under the consumer fraud act is punishable by a monetary penalty of not more than $10,000 for a first offense and not more than $20,000 for any subsequent offense.  In addition, violations may result in cease and desist orders issued by the Attorney General, the assessment of punitive damages, and the awarding of treble damages and costs to the injured party.  Additionally, a minor injured by a violation of this bill, or the minors parent or the Attorney General on the minors behalf, may bring a civil action against a covered online service provider who commits a violation.
