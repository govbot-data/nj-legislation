AR152 ASSEMBLY RESOLUTION No. 152 STATE OF NEW JERSEY 222nd LEGISLATURE INTRODUCED SEPTEMBER 14, 2026 Sponsored by: Assemblywoman  KATIE BRENNAN District 32 (Hudson) SYNOPSIS Encourages use of alternative disposal methods to restrict discharge of treatment wastewater containing high concentrations of perfluoroalkyl and polyfluoroalkyl substances into surface waters. CURRENT VERSION OF TEXT As introduced. An Assembly Resolution encouraging the use of alternative disposal methods to restrict the discharge of treatment wastewater containing high concentrations of perfluoroalkyl and polyfluoroalkyl substances into New Jersey surface waters. Whereas, Per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) are a class of persistent, man-made chemicals that resist degradation and have been detected in drinking water sources throughout New Jersey; and Whereas, PFAS exposure is correlated with adverse health effects, and the State has taken steps to establish some of the nations most stringent drinking water standards for these compounds; and Whereas, The treatment of PFAS-contaminated water generates concentrated residual waste streams that require careful management to avoid further environmental contamination; and Whereas, Certain wastewater discharges are permitted under the New Jersey Pollutant Discharge Elimination System (NJPDES) and may be released into rivers, streams, and other surface waters; and Whereas, Even when in compliance with applicable permit limits, such discharges may contribute to the continued presence and accumulation of PFAS in surface waters that serve as drinking water sources; and Whereas, Alternative disposal methods exist for certain non-hazardous PFAS-containing residuals, including transport to properly permitted facilities such as underground injection wells regulated under the federal Safe Drinking Water Acts Underground Injection Control (UIC) program, which are designed to isolate waste deep below drinking water aquifers; and Whereas, These permitted facilities operate under stringent federal and State regulatory oversight intended to protect underground sources of drinking water; and Whereas, In its 2024 and 2026 guidance document, Interim Guidance on the Destruction and Disposal of Perfluoroalkyl and Polyfluoroalkyl Substances and Materials Containing Perfluoroalkyl and Polyfluoroalkyl Substances, the United States Environmental Protection Agency identified underground injection control as among the more effective disposal technologies for certain PFAS-containing materials; and Whereas, Reducing discharges of PFAS into surface waters and evaluating alternative disposal methods align with the States commitment to protecting public health, safeguarding water resources, and minimizing long-term environmental risk; now, therefore, Be It Resolved by the General Assembly of the State of New Jersey: 1.    The General Assembly expresses its position that the discharge of PFAS treatment wastewater into New Jersey surface waters should be limited to the greatest extent practicable. 2.    The General Assembly supports the evaluation and use of alternative disposal methods for non-hazardous PFAS-containing residuals, including transport to properly permitted underground injection wells or other approved facilities. 3.    The General Assembly encourages continued review of PFAS management practices to ensure the protection of public health and the States water resources. 4.    Copies of this resolution, as filed with the Secretary of State, shall be transmitted by the Clerk of the General Assembly to the Governor and the Commissioner of Environmental Protection. STATEMENT Per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) are a class of persistent, man-made chemicals that resist degradation and have been detected in drinking water sources throughout New Jersey.  PFAS exposure is correlated with adverse health effects, and the State has taken steps to establish some of the nations most stringent drinking water standards for these compounds.  The treatment of PFAS-contaminated water generates concentrated residual waste streams that require careful management to avoid further environmental contamination. Currently, certain wastewater discharges are permitted under the New Jersey Pollutant Discharge Elimination System (NJPDES) and may be released into rivers, streams, and other surface waters.  Even when in compliance with applicable permit limits, such discharges may contribute to the continued presence and accumulation of PFAS in surface waters that serve as drinking water sources. Alternative disposal methods exist for certain non-hazardous PFAS-containing residuals, including transport to properly permitted facilities such as underground injection wells regulated under the federal Safe Drinking Water Acts Underground Injection Control (UIC) program, which are designed to isolate waste deep below drinking water aquifers. These permitted facilities operate under stringent federal and State regulatory oversight intended to protect underground sources of drinking water. In its 2024 and 2026 guidance document on PFAS-containing material management, the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency identified underground injection control as among the more protective disposal technologies for certain PFAS-containing materials. Reducing discharges of PFAS into surface waters and evaluating alternative disposal methods aligns with the States commitment to protecting public health, safeguarding water resources, and minimizing long-term environmental risk.
