A5707 ASSEMBLY, No. 5707 STATE OF NEW JERSEY 222nd LEGISLATURE INTRODUCED OCTOBER 22, 2026 Sponsored by: Assemblyman  JAMES J. KENNEDY District 22 (Somerset and Union) Co-Sponsored by: Assemblywomen Speight, Peterpaul, Donlon and Assemblyman Sampson SYNOPSIS Clean Water and Safe Communities Bond Act, authorizes bonds for $5 billion; appropriates $5,000. CURRENT VERSION OF TEXT As introduced. An Act authorizing the creation of a debt of the State of New Jersey by the issuance of bonds of the State in the aggregate principal amount of $5 billion for the purpose of providing financing for clean water and resilient infrastructure projects; providing the ways and means to pay and discharge the principal of and interest on the bonds; providing for the submission of this act to the people at a general election; and making an appropriation therefor. Be It Enacted by the Senate and General Assembly of the State of New Jersey: 1.  This act shall be known and may be cited as the Clean Water and Safe Communities Bond Act. 2.  The Legislature finds and declares that: a.  Over the past decade, communities across New Jersey have faced the impacts of increasingly extreme weather events with 14 federal disaster declarations for severe weather at the State-level and with each county in the State experiencing at least five federal disaster declarations for severe weather since 2011; b.  Considering the scale of the need for investment in resilient infrastructure and the limitations of current federal funding, it is critical that New Jersey explore new sources of funding for mitigation and resilience projects; c.  Investment in resilient infrastructure can result in cumulative benefits to residents and businesses by averting the threat of damages and introducing co-benefits, including job opportunities and healthier ecosystems in communities; d.  The co-benefits associated with investing in resilient infrastructure may also help to advance workforce development in the State by providing access to training programs, apprenticeships, and targeted hiring practices; and e.  Infrastructure projects like flood risk and restoration projects, water infrastructure projects, and resilience projects are necessary to improve the States prevention of, mitigation of, and response to severe weather disasters impacting the State. 3.  As used in this act: Bank means the New Jersey Infrastructure Bank, created pursuant to section 4 of P.L.1985, c.334 (C.58:11B-4). Bonds mean the bonds authorized to be issued, or issued, under this act. Commission means the New Jersey Commission on Capital Budgeting and Planning. Commissioner means the Commissioner of Environmental Protection. Cost means the expenses incurred in connection with: a resilient infrastructure project; the execution of any agreements and franchises deemed by the State Office of Emergency Management to be necessary or useful and convenient in connection with any resilient infrastructure project authorized by this act; the procurement or provision of engineering, inspection, planning, legal, financial, or other professional services, estimates, studies, reports, or advice, including the services of a bond registrar or an authenticating agent; feasibility studies; the issuance of bonds, or any interest or discount thereon; the administrative, organizational, operating, or other expenses incident to the financing and completing of any project authorized by this act; the establishment of a reserve fund or funds for working capital, operating, maintenance, or replacement expenses and for the payment or security of principal or interest on bonds, as the Director of the Division of Budget and Accounting in the Department of the Treasury may determine; and reimbursement to any fund of the State of moneys which may have been transferred or advanced therefrom to any fund created by this act, or of any moneys which may have been expended therefrom for, or in connection with, any project authorized by this act. Flood prevention and protection project means any project determined by the State Office of Emergency Management, in consultation with the commissioner, to protect State residents, property, or infrastructure from flood incidents, or to reduce or prevent the impacts of a flood incident within the State, including projects undertaken by or in partnership with municipalities, nonprofit organizations, or other entities, and including, but not limited to, wetland, marsh, and floodplain restoration, dam removal, green infrastructure installation, stormwater management improvements, structure elevation, floodproofing of buildings, and drainage system upgrades. Government securities means any bonds or other obligations which as to principal and interest constitute direct obligations of, or are unconditionally guaranteed by, the United States of America, including obligations of any federal agency, to the extent those obligations are unconditionally guaranteed by the United States of America, and any certificates or any other evidences of an ownership interest in those obligations of, or unconditionally guaranteed by, the United States of America or in specified portions which may consist of the principal of, or the interest on, those obligations. Green buildings project means any project determined by the State Office of Emergency Management, in consultation with the commissioner, to improve the efficiency and sustainability of construction and development in the State, including, but not limited to, building decarbonization, energy efficiency, and indoor air quality improvements. Green communities project means any project that the State Office of Emergency Management, in consultation with the commissioner, determines to improve the sustainability of, and reduce the adverse effects of severe weather on, communities in the State, including, but not limited to, improving heat resiliency in communities. Grid modernization project means any project determined by the State Office of Emergency Management, in consultation with the commissioner, to improve the States electric grid, including, but not limited to, increasing distributed resource capacity, improving system resilience and reliability, and improving the hosting capability of the electric distribution system. Local government unit means a county, a municipality, or any board, commission, committee, authority, or agency thereof that is subject to the provisions of the " Local Public Contracts Law," P.L.1971, c.198 (C.40A:11-1 et seq.), including a housing authority or redevelopment agency created or continued under the "Local Redevelopment and Housing Law," P.L.1992, c.79 (C.40A:12A-1 et seq.). Municipal resilience project means any project undertaken by a municipality, or in partnership with other public or private entities, that is designed to enhance the ability of communities, infrastructure, or natural systems to anticipate, withstand, respond to, or recover from flood incidents or other severe weather-related hazards, and that is consistent with, or supported by, an adopted or approved resilience, adaptation, or hazard mitigation plan, including, but not limited to, nature-based solutions, infrastructure improvements, emergency preparedness measures, and land use or floodplain management activities. Nature-based solution means a strategy or approach that uses natural processes, ecosystems, or engineered systems that mimic natural processes to address environmental challenges, such as water quality, flood risk, erosion, and habitat loss, while providing co-benefits for nature and habitats.  Nature-based solution includes, but is not limited to, the creation of living shorelines, marsh restoration and enhancement, habitat creation, carbon sequestration, and bank stabilization. Post-flood recovery project means any project determined by the State Office of Emergency Management, in consultation with the commissioner, to restore, rehabilitate, or enhance State-owned property, or other lands, communities, or natural resources impacted by a flood incident, including, but not limited to, projects undertaken by, or in partnership with, municipalities, nonprofit organizations, or other entities, and including, but not limited to, natural resources, coastal restoration, and marsh restoration. Resilience project means any project that enhances the States ability to adapt to future severe weather impacts, including, but not limited to: (1) grid modernization projects; (2) green building projects; (3) green communities projects; (4) municipal resilience projects; and (5) wildfire mitigation projects. Resilient infrastructure project means any resilience project, flood prevention and protection project, post-flood recovery project, restoration project, or water infrastructure project. Restoration project means any project determined by the State Office of Emergency Management, in consultation with the commissioner, to restore State property, lands, or natural resources after a flood incident, including, but not limited to, coastal rehabilitation and shoreline restoration. State Office of Emergency Management means the Office of Emergency Management in the Division of State Police in the Department of Law and Public Safety. Water infrastructure project means any project related to the improvement of drinking water, wastewater, and stormwater infrastructure in the State, which may include the use of nature-based solutions or green infrastructure. 4.  The Department of Law and Public Safety, in consultation with the commissioner, shall adopt, pursuant to the Administrative Procedure Act, P.L.1968 c.410 (C.52:14B‑1 et seq.), rules and regulations necessary to implement the provisions of this act.  The Attorney General shall review and consider the findings and recommendations of the commission in the administration of the provisions of this act. 5.  a.  Bonds of the State of New Jersey are authorized to be issued in the aggregate principal amount of $5 billion for the purpose of financing the cost of resilient infrastructure projects, as set forth in subsection b. of this section. b.  Of the aggregate principal amount of bonds authorized pursuant to subsection a. of this section: (1) $1.7 billion shall be used to finance the costs of flood prevention and protection projects and post-flood recovery projects undertaken by the State Office of Emergency Management, in consultation with the commissioner, and to provide grants to local government units and nonprofit organizations to finance the costs of flood prevention and protection projects and post-flood recovery projects; (a) Procedures for the review of, approval of, and eligibility criteria for, a grant for a flood prevention and protection projects and post-flood recovery projects shall be established by the State Office of Emergency Management and the commissioner, working in collaboration with one another. (b) Of the monies authorized pursuant to this paragraph, not less than $300 million shall be available for post-flood recovery projects with not less than 20 percent going towards nature-based solutions, not less than $300 million shall be available for restoration projects with not less than 20 percent going towards nature-based solutions, and not less than $400 million shall be available for voluntary property buyouts in flood-prone areas of the State.  The remainder of the monies authorized pursuant to this paragraph shall be available for municipal resilience projects that are supported by an adopted, approved, or otherwise finalized resilience, adaptation, or hazard mitigation plan. (2) $1 billion shall be used to finance the costs of water infrastructure projects undertaken by the Department of Environmental Protection and to provide grants to local government units and nonprofit organizations to finance the costs of water infrastructure projects; and (a) Procedures for the review of, approval of, and eligibility criteria for, a grant for a water infrastructure project shall be established by the commissioner.  The procedures shall be substantively similar to the procedures utilized under the New Jersey Environmental Infrastructure Trust Financing Program. (b) Of the monies authorized pursuant to this paragraph, not less than $300 million shall be available for drinking water infrastructure projects, not less than $400 million shall be available for stormwater infrastructure projects with not less than 20 percent going towards nature-based solutions, and not less than $300 million shall be available for wastewater infrastructure projects.  The remainder of the monies authorized pursuant to this paragraph shall be available for water infrastructure projects. (3) $2.3 billion shall be used to finance the costs of resilience projects undertaken by the State Office of Emergency Management, in consultation with the commissioner, and to provide grants to local government units and nonprofit organizations to finance the costs of resilience projects. (a) Procedures for the review of, approval of, and eligibility criteria for, a grant for a resilience project shall be established by the State Office of Emergency Management, in consultation with the commissioner. (b) Of the monies authorized pursuant to this paragraph, not less than $500 million shall be available for grid modernization projects, not less than $600 million shall be available for green buildings projects, and not less than $450 million shall be available for green communities projects.  The remainder of the monies authorized pursuant to this paragraph shall be available for resilience projects. (c) The State Office of Emergency Management may collaborate with the Board of Public Utilities or delegate grant selection processes to the Board of Public Utilities concerning energy-related resilience projects. c.  The State Office of Emergency Management, in consultation with the commissioner and the bank, shall for each fiscal year develop a priority system for the ranking of resilient infrastructure projects to be financed pursuant to this act and a Resilient Infrastructure Project Eligibility List, which lists the projects to be recommended for funding in the upcoming fiscal year.  Any resilient infrastructure project, or sponsor thereof, not identified in the project priority list pursuant to this subsection, shall not be eligible for a grant from the Resilient Infrastructure Fund. The State Office of Emergency Management, in consultation with the commissioner, may revise or supplement the project priority list no more than once during the fiscal year and shall submit the revised list to the Legislature when the revisions are made. d.  On or before May 15 of each year, the bank shall submit the Resilient Infrastructure Project Eligibility List to the Legislature pursuant to section 2 of P.L.1991, c.164 (C.52:14-19.1) to be introduced in each House in the form of legislative appropriations bills, which shall be referred to the Senate Environment and Energy Committee and the Assembly Environment and Solid Waste Committee, or their successors, for their respective consideration.  The Senate Environment and Energy Committee and the Assembly Environment and Solid Waste Committee shall, either individually or jointly, consider the legislation containing the Resilient Infrastructure Project Eligibility List, and shall report the legislation, together with any modifications, out of committee for consideration by each House of the Legislature.  On or before July 1 of each year, the Legislature shall approve an appropriations act containing the Resilient Infrastructure Project Eligibility List, which act shall include the authorization of an aggregate amount of funds of the bank to be expended for grants for the resilient infrastructure projects, including the individual amounts therefor, on the list. e.  The requirements of subsections c. and d. of this section shall expire when all funds collected through the issuance of Resilient Infrastructure Bonds pursuant to this act have been expended. f.  Each project constructed pursuant to the provisions of this act shall be constructed utilizing a project labor agreement and shall pay prevailing wages, in accordance with the New Jersey Prevailing Wage Act, P.L.1963, c.150 (C.34:11-56.25 et seq.), for any construction services in State. g.  Up to 10 percent of a grant awarded pursuant to this section may be used for planning and design activities. 6.  The bonds authorized under this act shall be serial bonds, term bonds, or a combination thereof, and shall be known as Resilient Infrastructure Bonds.  They shall be issued from time to time as the issuing officials herein named shall determine and may be issued in coupon form, fully‑registered form or book‑entry form.  The bonds may be subject to redemption prior to maturity and shall mature and be paid not later than 35 years from the respective dates of their issuance. 7.  The Governor, the State Treasurer and the Director of the Division of Budget and Accounting in the Department of the Treasury, or any two of these officials, herein referred to as the issuing officials, are authorized to carry out the provisions of this act relating to the issuance of bonds, and shall determine all matters in connection therewith, subject to the provisions of this act.  If an issuing official is absent from the State or incapable of acting for any reason, the powers and duties of that issuing official shall be exercised and performed by the person authorized by law to act in an official capacity in the place signature of the Secretary of State, or an Assistant Secretary of State, and shall be countersigned by the facsimile signature of the Director of the Division of Budget and Accounting in the CLEAN WATER AND SAFE COMMUNITIES BOND ACT YES Do you approve the Clean Water and Safe Communities Bond Act?  This bond act authorizes the State to issue bonds in the amount of $5 billion.  The money from the sale of the bonds would be used to finance, or provide grants to local governments and nonprofit organizations to finance, the costs of resilient infrastructure projects.  These projects would include flood prevention and protection projects, post-flood recovery projects, water infrastructure projects, and resilience projects. INTERPRETIVE STATEMENT NO This act would allow the State to borrow a total principal amount of $5 billion.  This money would be used by the State, local governments, and nonprofit organizations to finance the costs of resilient infrastructure projects.  $1.7 billion would be used for flood prevention and protection projects and post-flood recovery projects.  $1 billion would be used for water infrastructure projects.  $2.3 billion would be used for resilience projects. STATEMENT This bill, entitled the Clean Water and Safe Communities Bond Act, authorizes the issuance of $5 billion in State general obligation bonds to be used for the purpose of financing the cost of resilient infrastructure projects in the State. The $5 billion in bonds will be allocated in the following manner: (1) $1.7 billion is required to be used to finance the costs of flood prevention and protection projects and post-flood recovery projects undertaken by the State Office of Emergency Management, in consultation with the Commissioner of Environmental Protection (commissioner) and to provide grants to local government units and nonprofit organizations to finance the costs of flood prevention and protection projects and post-flood recovery projects; (2) $1 billion is required to be used to finance the costs of water infrastructure projects undertaken by the Department of Environmental Protection and to provide grants to local government units and nonprofit organizations to finance the costs of water infrastructure projects; and (3) $2.3 billion is required to be used to finance the costs of resilience projects undertaken by the State Office of Emergency Management, in consultation with the commissioner, and to provide grants to local government units and nonprofit organizations to finance the costs of resilience projects. Procedures for the review, approval of, and eligibility criteria for, grants are to be established by the State Office of Emergency Management, in consultation with the commissioner. Of the monies authorized to finance the costs of flood prevention and protection projects and post-flood recovery projects undertaken by the State Office of Emergency Management, in consultation with the commissioner, and to provide grants to local government units and nonprofit organizations to finance the costs of flood prevention and protection projects and post-flood recovery projects, not less than $300 million is to be made available for post-flood recovery projects with not less than 20 percent going towards nature-based solutions, not less than $300 million is to be made available for restoration projects with not less than 20 percent going towards nature-based solutions, and not less than $400 million is to be made available for voluntary property buyouts in flood-prone areas of the State.  Of the monies authorized to finance the costs of water infrastructure projects undertaken by the Department of Environmental Protection and to provide grants to local government units and nonprofit organizations to finance the costs of water infrastructure projects, not less than $300 million is to be made available for drinking water infrastructure projects, not less than $400 million is to be made available for stormwater infrastructure projects, and not less than $300 million is to be made available for wastewater infrastructure projects.  Of the monies authorized to finance the costs of resilience projects undertaken by the State Office of Emergency Management, in consultation with the commissioner, and to provide grants to local government units and nonprofit organizations to finance the resilience projects, not less than $500 million is to be made available for grid modernization projects, not less than $600 million is to be made available for green buildings projects, and not less than $450 million is to be made available for green communities projects.  The State Office of Emergency Management may collaborate with the Board of Public Utilities (board) or delegate grant selection processes to the board concerning energy-related resilience projects. The bill provides that the bond act is to be submitted to the people for approval at the general election to be held at least 70 days after enactment and appropriates $5,000 to the Department of State for expenses in connection with the publication of the public question.
