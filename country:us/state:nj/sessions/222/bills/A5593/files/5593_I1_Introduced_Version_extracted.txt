A5593 ASSEMBLY, No. 5593 STATE OF NEW JERSEY 222nd LEGISLATURE INTRODUCED OCTOBER 5, 2026 Sponsored by: Assemblyman  GERRY SCHARFENBERGER District 13 (Monmouth) Co-Sponsored by: Assemblywoman Flynn and Assemblyman Clifton SYNOPSIS Main Street Storefront Investment Credit Act; provides corporation business and gross income tax credits to small businesses for certain property improvements. CURRENT VERSION OF TEXT As introduced. An Act providing tax credits for certain improvements to small business properties, designated as the Main Street Storefront Investment Credit Act, and supplementing P.L.1945, c.162 (C.54:10A-1 et seq.) and Title 54A of the New Jersey Statutes. Be It Enacted by the Senate and General Assembly of the State of New Jersey: 1.    a. As used in this section: Eligible investment means an expense incurred by a qualified small business for improvements to a property owned or leased by the business for use in its principal business operations, which improvements are intended to increase the profitability of the business, including, but not limited to:  storefront improvements; accessibility improvements; installation of a security system; improvements to the exterior lighting, signage, awnings, and façade; installation of energy-efficient windows; commercial kitchen upgrades; and heating, ventilation, and air conditioning improvements. Qualified small business means a business with no more than 50 full-time employees, that is independently owned and operated, has its principal place of business in the State, and is not delinquent or deficient with respect to the payment of any State tax. b.    A taxpayer that is a qualified small business shall be allowed a credit against the tax imposed pursuant to section 5 of P.L.1945, c.162 (C.54:10A-5) in an amount equal to 30 percent of the eligible investments incurred during the privilege period by the taxpayer, provided that the total value of credits allowed to a taxpayer during any privilege period shall not exceed $30,000. c.     When filing a return that includes a claim for a tax credit allowed pursuant to this section, a taxpayer shall include such business records as may be required by the director documenting the eligible investments incurred by the taxpayer during the privilege period. d.    No tax credit shall be allowed pursuant to this section for any costs or expenses included in the calculation of any other tax credit or exemption granted pursuant to a claim made on a tax return filed with the director, or included in the calculation of an award of business assistance or incentive, for a period of time that coincides with the privilege period for which a tax credit authorized pursuant to this section is allowed. e.     The order of priority in which the tax credit allowed pursuant to this section and any other tax credits allowed by law may be taken shall be as prescribed by the director. The amount of the tax credit applied under this section against the tax imposed pursuant to section 5 of P.L.1945, c.162 (C.54:10A-5), for a privilege period, when taken together with any other payments, credits, deductions, and adjustments allowed by law shall not reduce the tax liability of the taxpayer to an amount less than the statutory minimum provided in subsection (e) of section 5 of P.L.1945, c.162 (C.54:10A-5). STATEMENT This bill, to be known as the Main Street Storefront Investment Credit Act, creates a corporation business tax and gross income tax credit for qualified small businesses that make certain improvements to their business properties. Under the bill, a qualified small business may claim a credit equal to 30 percent of the eligible investments incurred during the tax period, up to $30,000 per tax period.  When filing a tax return that includes a claim for the tax credit allowed under the bill, a taxpayer is required to include such business records as may be required by the Director of the Division of Taxation in the Department of the Treasury documenting the eligible investments incurred by the taxpayer. Under the bill, qualified small business means a business with no more than 50 full-time employees that is independently owned and operated, has its principal place of business in this State, and is not delinquent or deficient with respect to the payment of any State tax. The bill defines eligible investment as an expense incurred by a qualified small business for improvements to property owned or leased by the business for use in its principal business operations, which improvements are intended to increase the profitability of the business, including, but not limited to:  storefront improvements; accessibility improvements; installation of a security system; improvements to the exterior lighting, signage, awnings, and façade; installation of energy-efficient windows; commercial kitchen upgrades; and heating, ventilation, and air conditioning improvements.
