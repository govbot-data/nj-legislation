SYNOPSIS

     Enhanced Law Enforcement Protections Act; upgrades penalties and eliminates presumption of pretrial release for assault against law enforcement and first responders during civil disturbance.

STATEMENT

 

     This bill upgrades the penalties for aggravated assault on a law enforcement officer, firefighter, emergency first-aid or medical services provider, or correctional or sheriffs personnel that occur during a civil disturbance.

     Under current law, simple assault on a law enforcement officer, firefighter, emergency first-aid or medical services provider, or correctional or sheriffs personnel is automatically upgraded to aggravated assault.  For these categories of victims, aggravated assault is a crime of the fourth degree if the victim is uninjured, a crime of the third degree if the victim suffers bodily injury, and a crime of the second degree if the victim is a law enforcement officer and suffers serious bodily injury.  A person charged with aggravated assault on a law enforcement officer as a second degree crime is subject to the No Early Release Act and is ineligible to apply for certain pretrial diversionary programs.

     Under the bill, aggravated assault on a law enforcement officer, firefighter, emergency first-aid or medical services provider, or correctional or sheriffs personnel is further upgraded to a crime of the first degree if it occurs during and at the scene of a civil disturbance and the victim suffers serious bodily injury. 

     The bill defines civil disturbance as an incidence of riot or public brawl under N.J.S.2C:33-1, disturbance of a public gathering under subsection d. of N.J.S.2C:33-2, obstructing highways or public passages under N.J.S.2C:33-7, disrupting meetings or processions under N.J.S.2C:33-8, disrupting a funeral under section 2 of P.L.2006, c.93 (C.2C:33-8.1), interference with transportation under N.J.S.2C:33-14, gang criminality under section 1 of P.L.2007, c.341 (C.2C:33-29), or dog fighting under section 1 of P.L.2015, c.85 (C.2C:33-31).  In addition, the bill eliminates the presumption of pretrial release for a person charged with aggravated assault in the first degree.

     A crime of the first degree is punishable by ten to 20 years of imprisonment, a fine of up to $200,000, or both.  A crime of the second degree is punishable by five to ten years of imprisonment, a fine of up to $150,000, or both.  A crime of the third degree is punishable by three to five years of imprisonment, a fine of up to $15,000 or both.  A crime of the fourth degree is punishable by imprisonment for up to 18 months, a fine of up to $10,000, or both.
