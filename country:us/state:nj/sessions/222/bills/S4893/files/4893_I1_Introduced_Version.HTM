SYNOPSIS

     Provides gross income tax credit for taxpayers who donate blood.

 

CURRENT VERSION OF TEXT

     As introduced.

  


An Act providing a gross income tax credit for certain taxpayers who donate blood and supplementing chapter 4 of Title 54A of the New Jersey Statutes.

 

     Be It Enacted by the Senate and General Assembly of the State of New Jersey:

 

     1.    a.  A taxpayer shall be allowed a credit against the tax otherwise due under the New Jersey Gross Income Tax Act, N.J.S.54A:1-1 et seq., in an amount equal to $50 for each eligible blood donation made by the taxpayer through a qualified blood donation facility during the taxable year.  The total amount of the credit allowed under this section during a taxable year shall not exceed $200 for a taxpayer filing individually and $400 for a taxpayer who is married and filing jointly.

     b.    When filing a return that includes a claim for the credit, the taxpayer shall attach to their return a copy of the written statement received by the taxpayer from a qualified blood donation facility pursuant to subsection c. of this section.

     c.     Upon receipt of an eligible blood donation, a qualified blood donation facility shall issue to the taxpayer a written statement, signed and dated by an individual authorized to accept the donation on behalf of the qualified blood donation facility, that contains the name and address of the taxpayer, the date on which a blood donation was made, and the blood donation type.

     d.    The order of priority of the application of the credit allowed pursuant to this section, and any other credits allowed pursuant to the "New Jersey Gross Income Tax Act," N.J.S.54A:1-1 et seq. for a taxable year, shall be as prescribed by the director. The amount of the credit applied under this section against the tax imposed for a taxable year, together with any other credits allowed by law, shall not reduce the tax liability to an amount less than zero. 

     e.     As used in this section:

     Eligible blood donation means a donation of blood, platelets, or plasma made by the taxpayer at a qualified blood donation facility.

     Qualified blood donation facility means a facility that provides blood donation services to the public and is operated by a blood bank operator licensed under P.L.1963, c.33 (C.26:2A-2 et seq.) or a hospital licensed under P.L.1971, c.136 (C.26:2H-1 et seq.).

 

     2.    This act shall take effect immediately and shall apply to taxable years beginning on or after January 1 of the year next following the date of enactment.

 

 

STATEMENT

 

     This bill would provide a gross income tax credit to taxpayers who donate blood.  Specifically, a taxpayer would receive a $50 credit for each eligible blood donation made by the taxpayer through a qualified blood donation facility during the taxable year.  The total amount of the credit allowed under the bill would not exceed $200 per taxable year for taxpayers filing individually and $400 per taxable year for taxpayers who are married and filing jointly.

     The bill defines an eligible blood donation as a donation of blood, platelets, or plasma made by the taxpayer at a qualified blood donation facility.  The bill defines a qualified blood donation facility as a facility that provides blood donation services to the public and is operated by either a licensed blood bank or hospital.

     In order to claim the credit, a taxpayer would be required to obtain a written statement from a qualified blood donation facility following each eligible blood donation.  The written statement would be signed and dated by an individual authorized to accept the donation on behalf of the qualified blood donation facility, and contain the name and address of the taxpayer, the date on which a blood donation was made, and the blood donation type.  A copy of the written statement would be included as part of the taxpayers annual return.
