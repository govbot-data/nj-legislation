SYNOPSIS

     Requires transparency, standards, and discontinuance of certain Statewide assessments; eliminates high school graduation proficiency test.

 

CURRENT VERSION OF TEXT

     As introduced.

  


An Act concerning Statewide assessments, supplementing P.L.1979, c.241 (C.18A:7C-1 et seq.), and amending and repealing various parts of the statutory law.

 

     Be It Enacted by the Senate and General Assembly of the State of New Jersey:

 

     1.    (New section) As used in sections 1 through 3 of P.L.    , c.     (C.        ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill):

     "Commercially-developed standardized assessment" means a State-required assessment that: is administered by a school district or charter school; requires all students in a grade to answer the same questions, or a selection of questions from a common bank of questions, in the same manner; and is developed and scored by an entity under contract with a board of education.  A commercially-developed standardized assessment shall not include district-mandated assessments or diagnostic and formative assessments used by teaching staff members to identify particular student learning needs or the need for special services, or to modify instructional strategies to improve an individual student's learning.

     "State assessment" means an assessment required pursuant to State or federal law and administered to all students in a specific grade level or subject area and whose results are aggregated for analysis at the district, school, or student subgroup level.

 

     2. (New section)    a.  The Department of Education shall provide each school district with the results of any State assessment or commercially-developed standardized assessment administered to the districts students within 30 days of the administration of the test.

     b.    A school district shall provide the parents or guardians of a student enrolled in the district the results of any State assessment or commercially-developed standardized assessment administered to the student within 14 days of receiving the results from the department pursuant to subsection a. of this section.

 

     3.    (New section)  a. The Commissioner of Education shall review, and determine the continued applicability of, any State assessment or commercially-developed standardized assessment that is administered to students in grades three through 12 as of the effective date of P.L.    , c.     (C.        ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill).  The commissioner shall discontinue the use of any assessment that is not necessary for complying with the requirements of State or federal law.

     b.    Within 90 days of the effective date of P.L.    , c.     (C.   ) (pending before the Legislature as this bill), the commissioner shall develop guidelines that provide best practices for school districts in preparing students to take any State assessment or commercially-developed standardized assessment, including guidelines on limiting the amount of instructional time to be spent on test preparation for these assessments.

     c.     Nothing in this section shall be construed to limit the ability of a classroom teacher or board of education to develop, administer, and score an assessment for an individual classroom, grade level, or group of grade levels in any subject area in grades three through 12.

STATEMENT

 

     This bill requires transparency, standards, and discontinuance of certain Statewide assessments.  It also eliminates the high school graduation proficiency test.

     State Board of Education regulations require the Department of Education to provide certain information to school districts after each administration of the Statewide assessments.  This information includes individualized test results for each student in the districts.  There is no statute or regulation, however, specifying a timeframe in which the department is required to release these test results to the school district.  Additionally, State board regulations require chief school administrators to provide educators, parents, and students with the results of Statewide assessments within 30 days of receipt of the information from the department. 

     This bill specifies that the department is to provide these test results to each school district within 30 days of the administration of the test.  Under the bill, a school district would then have 14 days to provide those results to the parents or guardians of students enrolled in the district.

     The bill also requires the Commissioner of Education to review, and determine the continued applicability of, State assessments or commercially-developed standardized assessments that are administered to students in grades three through 12.  The bill requires the commissioner to discontinue the use of any assessment that is not necessary for complying with the requirements of State or federal law. The bill clarifies that it is not to be construed to limit the ability of a classroom teacher or board of education to develop, administer, and score an assessment for an individual classroom, grade level, or group of grade levels in any subject area in grades three through 12.

     Under the bill, the commissioner would develop guidelines that provide best practices for school districts in preparing students for taking any State assessment or commercially-developed standardized assessment.  The best practices will include guidelines on limiting the amount of instructional time to be spent on test preparation for these assessments.

     The bill further prohibits the State Board of Education from including in the standards for graduation from high school a requirement that students achieve satisfactory performance on the Statewide graduation proficiency test.

     The bill amends current law to remove various references to the graduation proficiency test, including: the requirement that a Statewide proficiency test be included in the State or district standards for graduation from high school; the requirement that an out-of-school youth or adult age 18 or older pass the graduation proficiency test before being granted a State endorsed diploma; the requirement that school district report cards include information regarding the results of the graduation proficiency test; and the requirement that renaissance school projects be evaluated based on students performance on the graduation proficiency test.

     Finally, the bill repeals the section of law that requires the State to administer a graduation proficiency test to all 11th grade students and certain 12th grade students, and repeals the section of law that requires the Commissioner of Education to consult with educators, parents, students, business and community representatives, and members of minority groups while developing the graduation proficiency test.  The bill also repeals sections of law requiring the commissioner and the Joint Committee on the Public Schools to review and report on student performance on the graduation proficiency test.
