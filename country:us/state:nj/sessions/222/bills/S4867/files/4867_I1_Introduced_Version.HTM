SYNOPSIS

     Requires sellers to include certain fees and surcharges in advertised price of goods and services.

 

CURRENT VERSION OF TEXT

     As introduced.

  


An Act concerning the advertised price of goods and services and supplementing P.L.1960, c.39 (C.56:8-1 et seq.).

 

     Be It Enacted by the Senate and General Assembly of the State of New Jersey:

 

     1.    a.  It shall be an unlawful practice and a violation of P.L.1960, c.39 (C.56:8-1 et seq.) for a seller to advertise, display, or offer a price for goods or services that does not include all mandatory fees or surcharges.

     b.    To comply with the provisions of this act:

     (1)   a food delivery platform shall clearly and conspicuously:

     (a)   disclose the specific cost to a customer, or, if calculated on a percentage basis, the percentage  of any mandatory fee or surcharge to be paid  to place a delivery order at a certain restaurant, including the amount of any additional fee but excluding optional gratuity;

     (b)   disclose, when an item selected by a customer is displayed, the total cost of the item, including any mandatory fee or surcharge associated with the item and the purpose and amount of the fee or surcharge; and

     (c)   display, prior to checkout, an itemized subtotal for each item selected by a customer and any additional fee included in the total cost;

     (2)   a seller that offers goods or services at an auction in which a customer may bid on the goods or services, the total cost of which is indeterminable, shall clearly and conspicuously:

     (a)   disclose any mandatory fees or surcharges associated with a transaction; and

     (b)   provide notice that the total cost of the goods or services may vary;

     (3)   a seller that offers a service for which the total cost is determined by customer selections and preferences or by distance or time shall clearly and conspicuously disclose:

     (a)   the factors that determine the total cost of a service;

     (b)   any mandatory fees or surcharges associated with a transaction; and

     (c)   that the total cost to a customer may vary; and

     (4)   a restaurant or hotel shall, in an offer or advertisement for goods or services that includes pricing information, clearly and conspicuously disclose for each advertised total price the amount of any automatic or mandatory gratuity or mandatory fee or surcharge.

     c.     As used in this section:

     Food delivery platform means an Internet website, mobile application, or other Internet service that offers or arranges for the sale or delivery of food and beverages prepared by, and the same-day delivery or same-day pickup of food and beverage from, a restaurant.

     Mandatory fee or surcharge means a fee or surcharge that:

     (1)   a seller requires a customer to pay to purchase goods or services;

     (2)   is not reasonably avoidable by a customer; or

     (3)   a customer would reasonably expect to be included in the purchase of goods or services.

     The term shall not include a tax or fee imposed by a government entity that is required by law to be collected from a customer on the sale, use, purchase, receipt, or delivery of goods or services.

     Restaurant means an establishment in which the principal business is the sale of food or beverages for consumption on or off the premises.

     Seller means a person who sells, leases, or rents goods or services to a customer.

 

     2.    This act shall take effect immediately.

 

 

STATEMENT

 

     This act requires sellers to include mandatory fees and surcharges in the advertised, displayed, or offered price of goods and services.

     Under the bill, a mandatory fee or surcharge is a fee or surcharge that:

     (1)   a seller requires a customer to pay to purchase goods or services;

     (2)   is not reasonably avoidable by a customer; or

     (3)   a customer would reasonably expect to be included in the purchase of goods or services.

     A mandatory fee or surcharge does not include a tax or fee imposed by a government entity that is required by law to be collected from a customer on the sale, use, purchase, receipt, or delivery of goods or services.

     The bill also establishes certain requirements that food delivery platforms, sellers that offer goods or services at an auction, sellers that offer goods or services based on customer selections and preferences, restaurants, and hotels are to meet in order to comply with the provisions of the bill.

     A violation of the bills provisions would be an unlawful practice under the Consumer Fraud Act, and would be punishable by a monetary penalty of not more than $10,000 for a first offense and not more than $20,000 for any subsequent offense.  Additionally, a violation of the Consumer Fraud Act can result in cease and desist orders issued by the Attorney General, the assessment of punitive damages, and the awarding of treble damages and costs to the injured.
