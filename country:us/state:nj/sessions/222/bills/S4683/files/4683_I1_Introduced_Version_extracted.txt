STATEMENT This bill requires prospective and current employees, volunteers, and organizers of youth serving organizations to annually obtain criminal history record background checks and complete training. The bill defines a youth serving organization as sports team, league, an athletic association, any entity that provides private athletic lessons, or any other corporation, private or non-profit association or other organization that provides recreational, cultural, charitable, social, or other activities or services to persons under 18 years of age.  Youth serving organization does not include public and nonpublic schools or certain licensed youth camps.  These requirements do not apply to local governments administering youth programming that have adopted measures requiring annual criminal history background checks of individuals similarly involved with youth.  Additionally, individuals who have received criminal history background checks in the same calendar year as required by law for their involvement with a facility, center, or other system under the supervision of the Department of Education would only be required to show proof of completion of the criminal history background check in order to serve as an employee, volunteer, or organizer of a youth serving organization. The bill also requires the Commissioner of Children and Families to establish an informational training course to assist youth serving organizations to develop safe environments for children and adolescents who participate in their programming.  The bill requires the curriculum to: recommend models of organizational safety protocols for youth serving organization; provide guidance to individuals who work with youth on lawful and appropriate behaviors during and after an individuals tenure with a youth serving organization; be based on best practices; be triennially revised, as determined by the commissioner; and be available to the youth serving organizations from the website of the Department of Children and Families.  Under the bill, a youth serving organization would require each prospective or current employee, volunteer, and organizer to annually complete the training and adhere to any organizational safety protocols adopted by the youth serving organization. A youth serving organization that fails to request criminal history record background check or maintain records pertaining to informational training required by the bill would be subject to up to $500 for a first offense; up to $750 for a second offense, and up to $1,000 for any subsequent offense to be collected in a summary proceeding under the Penalty Enforcement Law of 1999. Under the bill, it would be a crime of the fourth degree: 1)for an individual to participate in a youth serving organization with the knowledge that the individual is disqualified based on the individual's criminal history; 2) an organizer, coach, or any other person to working in association with a youth serving organization to disregard the results of a criminal history background check or to fail to obtain a criminal history background with the knowledge that an employee, volunteer, or organizer of the organization is disqualified from participating in the organization based on the individuals criminal history.  A crime of the fourth degree is punishable by up to 18 months imprisonment, a fine of up to $10,000, or both.
