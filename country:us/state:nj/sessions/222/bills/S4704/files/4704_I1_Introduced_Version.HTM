STATEMENT

 

     This bill establishes procedures within the "Municipal Land Use Law," P.L.1975, c.291 (C.40:55D-1 et seq.), for the identification and preservation of abandoned cemeteries.

     The bill provides that the existence and proposed locations of abandoned cemeteries are to be included in the land use plan element of municipal master plans and on their official maps.  The bill requires municipalities to include in their site plan ordinances standards and requirements relating to the identification of abandoned cemeteries.  The bill encourages municipalities to include provisions for the preservation and protection of abandoned cemeteries in their site plan and subdivision ordinances.

     The bill provides that a municipality is responsible for the protection and perpetual care of an abandoned cemetery that has been identified in the land use plan element of the municipality's master plan.  Under the bill, a governing body of a municipality:

·        may use monies from its "Municipal Open Space, Recreation, Floodplain Protection, and Farmland and Historic Preservation Trust Fund" for the protection and perpetual care of the abandoned cemetery;

·        may, for a municipality that does not maintain a "Municipal Open Space, Recreation, Floodplain Protection, and Farmland and Historic Preservation Trust Fund" or that elects not to use such monies, utilize any other lawful source of funding available to the municipality, including monies appropriated through a municipal, county, or State open space, recreation, farmland, historic preservation, or related trust fund program, as authorized by law; and

·        be prohibited from charging any additional surcharges or fees for the protection and perpetual care of the abandoned cemetery.

     There exist numerous abandoned cemeteries within the State of New Jersey.  These cemeteries were once part of larger homesteads or former religious congregations and provide a vast resource for family, church, and community history.  Many of these cemeteries are no longer cared for as descendants have died or moved away.

     Additionally, on July 4, 2026, the United States celebrated its 250th anniversary (semquincentennial) of the signing of the Declaration of Independence.  New Jersey played a critical role during the American Revolutionary War, hosting over 600 military engagements -- more than any other colony. Situated between British-held New York and rebel Philadelphia, New Jersey held pivotal battles in Trenton, Princeton, and Monmouth, and key encampments, including Morristown, lead to the victories of the Continental Army.  During this time, General George Washington spent more time in New Jersey than anywhere else.  Many members of the Continental Army died and were buried in New Jersey.  For the acknowledgement of the pivotal role New Jersey played in the nations independence, it is imperative that, whenever a municipality has identified the location of an abandoned cemetery, the municipality preserve and provide perpetual care to the abandoned cemetery.
