SYNOPSIS

     Sadies Law; establishes legal planning services program for individuals with profound disabilities and their families.

 

CURRENT VERSION OF TEXT

     As introduced.

  


An Act concerning legal planning services for individuals with profound disabilities and supplementing Title 30 of the Revised Statues.

 

     Be It Enacted by the Senate and General Assembly of the State of New Jersey:

 

     1.  This act shall be known and may be cited as Sadie's Law.

 

     2.  The Legislature finds and declares that:

     a.  Families of individuals with profound disabilities face urgent and lifelong planning needs, including, but not limited to, decision-making supports when establishing guardianship or standby guardianship arrangements and special needs trusts, creating estate plans, or executing legal instruments designed to transfer, protect or direct the management of property and personal decision-making authority.

     b.  Delays in planning for such needs increase the risk of instability and State intervention if a parent or primary caregiver dies or becomes incapacitated.

     c.  Proactive, State-supported legal planning services can improve continuity of care, protect access to public benefits and public resources, and honor the dignity, autonomy, and safety of individuals with profound disabilities.

     d.  However, the cost and complexity of specialized legal planning may prevent families from completing essential legal documents before a crisis occurs.

     e.  The State has an interest in creating a timely, voluntary, rights-protective pathway to qualified legal planning services.

     f.  Therefore, it is in the best interests of the State to ensure that at, or following, an individuals diagnosis of a profound disability, the individual and the individuals family receive timely, no-cost access to legal planning services to safeguard the individuals long-term health, welfare, safety and financial autonomy.

 

     3.  As used in this act:

     Approved provider means a licensed attorney-at-law in this State, a nonprofit legal services organization, or other legal services providers authorized by law and approved by the Department of Human Services to provide legal services to individuals with a profound disability, with demonstrated competence in disability, benefits, decision-making, guardianship, or estate planning services.

     Core legal planning services means an individualized group of covered legal planning services provided to, and the documents prepared, executed, or filed for, an individual with a profound disability and the individuals family eligible to receive such services pursuant to section 4 of this act.

     Department means the Department of Human Services.

     Family means a parent, legal guardian, or other caregiver of an individual with a profound disability.

     Fund means the Legal Planning Services for Individuals with Profound Disabilities and their Families Program Fund established pursuant to section 6 of this act.

     Profound disability means a chronic disability, diagnosed by a State-licensed health care professional qualified to assess developmental, intellectual, neurological, or comparable conditions, which indicates a profound mental or physical functional limitation, or a combination of both or physical limitations, including, but not limited to, profound autism, as clinically determined that is expected to continue indefinitely and require substantial, ongoing supports of lifelong or extended duration.

     Program means the Legal Planning Services for Individuals with Profound Disabilities and their Families Program established pursuant to section 4 of this act.

 

     4.  a.  There is established the Legal Planning Services for Individuals with Profound Disabilities and their Families Program in the Department of Human Services.  The purpose of the program shall be to:

     (1) develop an intake process which assists the program in ensuring that an individual with a profound disability and the individuals family receive core legal planning services tailored to the legal services planning needs of the individual and the individuals family; and

     (2) cover, at no cost, reasonable legal fees for the preparation, execution, and, where applicable, the filing of legal documents for an individual with a profound disability or the individuals family.

     b.  The core legal planning services provided by the program to an individual with a profound disability or the individuals parent or guardian may include the:

     (1) drafting of a last will and testament addressing guardianship nominations, beneficiary planning, and testamentary special-needs provisions, where appropriate;

     (2) designation of standby guardians or the authorization of emergency caregivers for the individual;

     (3) provision of legal guidance concerning third-party special needs trusts or ABLE accounts established pursuant to P.L.2015, c.185 (C.52:18A-250 et al.), where appropriate, to preserve eligibility for public benefits;

     (4) authorization of a power of attorney or advance directive for health care, or both, where legally and clinically appropriate;

     (5) drafting of letters of intent or care and support plans outlining the history, needs, routines, goals, supports, therapies, communication preferences, decision-making supports, and safety considerations of the individual;

     (6) provision of legal advice concerning supported decision-making, continuity-of-care planning, or other less restrictive decision-making alternatives concerning the individuals long-term health, welfare, safety and financial autonomy;

     (7) filing of required court petitions related to guardianship or supported decision-making when appropriate, including the payment of initial filing fees and other reasonable related legal services costs;

     (8) provision of notary, translation, interpretation, or other services to the individual or the individuals family necessary to provide accessibility to legal planning services; and

     (9) provision of other legal planning services deemed necessary by the Commissioner of Human Services to carry out the provisions of this act.

     c.  (1) An individual with a profound disability and the individuals family shall be eligible to participate in the program if the individual:

     (a) has been diagnosed with a profound disability and has provided documentation from a State-licensed health care professional confirming the diagnosis on a form and in a manner determined by the department;

     (b) was a New Jersey resident at the time of the individuals diagnosis of a profound disability or had established residency in the State within 12 months after such diagnosis; and

     (c) has been referred to the program by the referral network developed pursuant to subsection c. of section 5 of this act.

     (2) An individual with a profound disability and the individuals family who meet the eligibility criteria set forth in paragraph (1) of this subsection may apply to the department to participate in the program. 

     d.  Upon receipt of an application to participate in the program, the department shall review the application to determine if the applicant meets the eligibility requirements of the program, and within 10 business days of verifying the applicants eligibility, the department shall:

     (1) initiate the intake process developed pursuant to paragraph (1) of subsection a. of this section to determine the core legal planning services that the individual and the individuals family can receive through the program; and

     (2) based upon information provided by the individual and the individuals family during the intake process, advise the individual and the individuals family of the specific core legal planning services provided by the program pursuant to subsection b. of this section identified by the intake process that will address the legal services planning needs of the individual and the individuals family.

     e.  (1) If an individual with a profound disability and the individuals family agree to participate in the program, the individual and the individuals family shall receive the core legal planning services identified by the intake process pursuant to paragraph (2) of subsection d. of this section for a period of 90 days beginning upon completion of the intake process.

     (2) The individual and the individuals family may continue to receive such services, at no cost, for an additional 24 months after the end of the initial 90-day period, upon approval by the department.  The individual and the individuals family may reapply to the program for additional services which shall be provided, at cost, to the individual and the individuals family based on their continued eligibility to receive the additional services.

     f.  Participation in the program shall be voluntary.  If an individual or the individuals family elects to decline, postpone, or discontinue receiving services from the program, the individual or the individuals family shall do so without effect on their eligibility for any other State programs or benefits.

     g.  Nothing in this section shall be construed to:

     (1) require the program to promote or encourage the use of guardianship services unless the intake procedure initiated pursuant to paragraph (1) of subsection d. of this section determines that less restrictive legal planning services, including, but not limited to, supported decision-making, are not appropriate to address the legal services planning needs of an individual with a profound disability and the individuals family; or

     (2) waive attorney-client privilege, attorney work-product protection, or other applicable legal confidentiality protections.

 

     5.  In administering the program established pursuant to section 4 of this act, the department shall:

     a. contract with approved providers, to the extent permitted by law, to provide the core legal planning services offered through the program to an individual with a profound disability or the individuals family;

     b.  establish qualification standards, conflict-of-interest rules, quality benchmarks for, procedures concerning corrective action responses by, and the termination of contracts with, the approved providers contracted with the department pursuant to subsection a. of this section;

     c.  develop a referral network of health care professionals, representatives of child study teams, Medicaid managed care organizations, and programs licensed or funded by the department to provide services to individuals with developmental disabilities, contracted providers employed by the States early intervention program, and other appropriate entities to refer an individual with a profound disability and the individuals family, who may be in need of legal planning services, to the program;

     d.  create a public awareness campaign to promote awareness about the program and the legal planning services that may be provided to an individual with a profound disability and the individuals family participating in the program;

     e.  ensure that the referral network developed pursuant to subsection c. of this section distributes to an individual with a profound disability and the individuals family, in print and in languages including, but not limited to, English and Spanish, informational materials about the program at the time a referral is made; and

     f.  guarantee that the information obtained by the program regarding an individual with a profound disability or the individuals family shall be kept confidential and protected from public disclosure, used only: (1) to determine eligibility for, and provide the core legal planning services through, the program; or (2) for statistical and program evaluation purposes.

 

     6.  a.  There is established in the department a fund to be known as the Legal Planning Services for Individuals with Profound Disabilities and their Families Program Fund.  This fund shall be the repository for monies appropriated or approved by the department to support the program established pursuant to section 4 of this act.

     b.  The monies deposited in the fund established pursuant to subsection a. of this section shall be used to pay for the provision of core legal planning services offered through the program pursuant to subsection b. of section 4 of this act.

 

     7.  The department shall annually report to the Governor, and to the Legislature pursuant to section 2 of P.L.1991, c.164 (C.52:14-19.1), on the effectiveness of the program established pursuant to section 4 of this act.  The report shall include de-identified data on the:

     a. number of individuals with a profound disability and their families who apply to the program for core legal planning services;

     b.  number of individuals with a profound disability and their families who participate in the program;

     c.  specific core legal planning services provided to each of the programs participants;

     d.  average time between when an individual with a profound disability or the individuals family becomes eligible to receive core legal planning services and when such services are delivered;

     e.  the average cost per core legal planning service provided to eligible individuals;

     f.  details of the allocations made with the monies appropriated or approved by the department to the program from the fund established pursuant to section 6 of this act; and

     g.  goals of the program and the outcome measures to be used to determine program efficacy.

     8.  The department shall adopt rules and regulations, pursuant to the Administrative Procedure Act, P.L.1968, c.410 (C.52:14B-1 et seq.), to implement the provisions of this act.

 

     9.  This act shall take effect on the first day of the third month next following enactment, except that the Commissioner of Human Services may take any anticipatory administrative action in advance as shall be necessary for the implementation of this act.

 

 

STATEMENT

 

     This bill, to be known as Sadies Law, establishes the Legal Planning Services for Individuals with Profound Disabilities and their Families Program (the program) in the Department of Human Services (the department).  The purpose of the program is to:  (1) develop an intake process to ensure that an individual with a profound disability and their family receive core legal planning services tailored to their legal needs; and (2) cover, at no cost, reasonable legal fees for the preparation, execution and filing of legal documents.

     The program is to provide the core legal planning services enumerated in the bill to individuals with a profound disability and their families who apply to participate in the program and meet specific eligibility requirements as determined by the department.

     Program participants are to receive the core legal planning services, specific to their legal needs, for a period of 90 days which may be continued, at no cost, for an additional 24 months after the initial 90-day period as outlined in the bill.  The individual and the individuals family may reapply to the program for additional services, provided at cost, based on their continued eligibility to receive additional services.

       Participation in the program is voluntary, and an individual or their family may elect to decline, postpone, or discontinue receiving services from the program without effect on their eligibility for any other State programs or benefits.

     The bill establishes the Legal Planning Services for Individuals with Profound Disabilities and their Families Program Fund which is to be the repository for monies appropriated to or approved by the department to support the program.  The monies deposited in the fund are to pay for the programs provision of core legal planning services.

     The bill also:  (1) outlines the departments responsibilities in administering the program; and (2) requires that the department annually report to the Governor and to the Legislature on the effectiveness of the program.
