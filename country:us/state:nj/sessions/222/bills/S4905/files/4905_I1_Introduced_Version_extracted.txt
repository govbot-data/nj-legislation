S4905 SENATE, No. 4905 STATE OF NEW JERSEY 222nd LEGISLATURE INTRODUCED NOVEMBER 23, 2026 Sponsored by: Senator  JAMES W. HOLZAPFEL District 10 (Monmouth and Ocean) SYNOPSIS Provides for practice of dental hygiene without supervision pursuant to collaborative practice agreement with licensed dentist. CURRENT VERSION OF TEXT As introduced. An Act concerning dental hygienists and amending and supplementing various parts of the statutory law. Be It Enacted by the Senate and General Assembly of the State of New Jersey: 1.    (New section)   The Legislature finds and declares that: a.     Oral health and overall physical health are inextricably linked, as oral health is a critical component of overall physical health and systemic health can in turn impact oral health. b.    Poor oral health can not only cause conditions such as cavities and tooth loss, but can also increase the risk of heart attacks, strokes, and Alzheimers disease and cause significant psychological and financial strain. c.     Access to oral health care services, including routine dental cleanings, is key to maintaining optimal oral health, encouraging overall physical health, and preventing avoidable financial and psychological costs. d.    Despite the importance of oral health care, over 200,000 New Jerseyans do not have adequate access to dental care and only three in ten adults in the State reported visiting a dentist or dental clinic in 2024.  Certain areas of the State face workforce shortages, and providers may additionally reserve a significant amount of their availability for specialized or emergent services. e.     Dental hygienists are highly educated and experienced in providing preventative oral health care through dental hygiene services but are currently required to work under the supervision and direction of a dentist. f.     Several states that previously had populations facing inadequate access to oral health care services similar to New Jersey have allowed dental hygienists to independently provide dental hygiene services to help close the gap in access to oral health care. g.    The Legislature therefore determines that it is in the public interest to broaden access to preventative oral health care and subsequently improve the oral and physical health of individuals across the State by providing for the practice of dental hygiene without dentist supervision. 2.    Section 2 of P.L.1979, c.46 (C.45:6-49) is amended to read as follows: 2.    For the purposes of [this act] P.L.1979, c.46 (C.45:6-48 et al.): a.     "Board" means the New Jersey State Board of Dentistry. b.    "Registered dental assistant" means any person who has fulfilled the requirements for registration established by [this act] P.L.1979, c.46 (C.45:6-48 et al.) and who has been registered by the board.  A registered dental assistant shall work under the direct supervision of a licensed dentist. c.     "Dental assistant" means any person who is trained by formal education or office internship to perform, under the direct supervision of a dentist, any routine office procedure, not including an intra-oral procedure, in the office of a dentist. d.    "Dental hygienist" means any person who performs in the office of any licensed dentist or in any appropriately equipped school, dental clinic, or institution under the supervision of a licensed dentist, or in an independent office or in any appropriately equipped school, dental clinic, or institution without the supervision of a licensed dentist pursuant to a collaborative practice agreement, those educational, preventive and therapeutic supplementary act and who has been registered by the board.  A limited registered dental assistant shall be limited to working under the direct supervision of a dentist who conducts a limited dental h.    "Dental clinic" means dental clinic as defined in section 1 of P.L.1951, c.199 (C.45:6-15.1). i.     "Institution" means any nursing home, veterans' home, hospital or prison, or any State or licensed dentist in an independent office or any appropriately equipped school, dental clinic, or institution. k.    Collaborating dentist means a licensed dentist who enters into a collaborative STATEMENT This bill provides for the practice of dental hygiene without supervision by a licensed dentist pursuant to a collaborative practice agreement. Specifically, the bill establishes a certification for collaborative practice, which the New Jersey State Board of Dentistry is to issue to a licensed dental hygienist who submits to the board a signed collaborative practice agreement between the dental hygienist and a licensed dentist and meets certain other requirements.  Under a collaborative practice agreement, a dental hygienist certified for collaborative practice is to: (1)   practice dental hygiene without the supervision of a licensed dentist only at the independent office, dental clinic, or institution named in the collaborative practice agreement; (2)   obtain a signed consent from each patient advising the patient of the collaborative practice agreement and the dental hygienists scope of practice; (3)   consult with, and refer each patient to, the collaborating dentist according to the schedule established by the collaborative practice agreement; (4)   annually review the collaborative practice agreement with the collaborating dentist; and (5)   make the collaborative practice agreement available for inspection upon request. A collaborative practice agreement is to include: (1)   the name of the dental hygienist and the collaborating dentist; (2)   the address of the independent office, school, dental clinic, or institution where the dental hygienist is authorized to practice dental hygiene; (3)   provisions for consultation with the collaborating dentist, including a schedule for the periodic review of patient records, which shall occur at least quarterly; (4) provisions for the referral of each patient to the collaborating dentist, which shall occur at least annually; (5)   provisions for alternative coverage during the absence or incapacity of the collaborating dentist; and (6)   any other information as required by the board. Under the bill, certified collaborative practice dental hygienists and licensed dentists may enter into no more than three collaborative practice agreements at one time.
