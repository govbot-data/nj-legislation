STATEMENT This bill establishes a first-time homebuyer savings program and provides certain gross income tax benefits for account contributions and distributions. First-Time Homebuyer Savings Account Program Under the bill, the first-time homebuyer savings program would be established by the Executive Director of the New Jersey Housing and Mortgage Finance Agency to encourage first-time homeownership by providing certified first-time homebuyers with access to savings accounts for costs related to the purchase of qualified residential property.  Specifically, the executive director would be required to enter into agreements with each financial institution that expresses an interest in establishing and maintaining first-time homebuyer savings accounts created under the program.  Among other requirements set forth in the bill, each agreement entered into by the executive director with a financial institution would be required to: (1)   limit the amount of cash or other marketable securities that may be contributed to an account during each taxable year to no more than $20,000 for an individual accountholder, and $40,000 for a joint account owned by two certified first-time homebuyers who are married; (2)   limit the aggregate amount of cash or other marketable securities that may be contributed to an account during all taxable years to no more than $200,000; and (3)   allow a certified first-time homebuyer to make withdrawals or distributions of assets in a first-time homebuyer savings account, without penalties, to pay for an eligible expense incurred by the certified first-time homebuyer for a qualified home purchase. The bill defines an eligible expense as a payment made to a mortgagor, a construction contractor, or other vendor hired by a certified first-time homebuyer to perform work at a qualified residential property.  The bill defines qualified residential property to include various types of dwellings commonly used as residences.  The bill defines a qualified home purchase as the acquisition, by sale, of a qualified residential property located in this State which is occupied as a certified first-time homebuyers principal residence; or the construction of a new qualified residential property located in this State paid for by the certified first-time homebuyer and occupied as the certified first-time homebuyers principal residence. In addition, the executive director would also establish an application process to certify individuals seeking to open first-time homebuyer savings accounts as certified first-time homebuyers. An individual or individuals would be certified as a first-time homebuyer if they: (1)   reside in the State or will reside in the State after making a qualified home purchase; (2)   have not made a qualified home purchase, or owned residential property in this State, or any other state or jurisdiction; (3)   will use assets maintained in a first-time homebuyer savings account to pay eligible expenses incurred by the certified first-time homebuyer for a qualified home purchase; (4)   have completed a homebuyer education course approved by the executive director during the taxable year in which the application was filed, or any of the three taxable years immediately preceding such year; and (5)   do not maintain any other first-time homebuyer savings accounts through any comparable program in this State, or any other state or jurisdiction, or will terminate an account maintained through a comparable program within 90 days of the date written certification is issued. A certified first-time homebuyer would be required to provide a copy of the written certification to the financial institution administering a first-time homebuyer savings account before the financial institution would be able to open an account. The bill also requires the executive director to establish a process by which a financial institution participating in the program would provide monthly reports on certain information concerning each first-time homebuyer savings account maintained by the institution for certified first-time homebuyers.  The report would include, for each account, the date the account was opened, the amount of assets transferred to the account, the date and amount of each withdrawal or distribution, and the fair market value of the assets in the account.  The report would be filed with the executive director and a copy transmitted to the Director of the Division of Taxation. The executive director would further be required to establish a process for certified first-time homebuyers to submit reports within one month following each withdrawal or distribution made from their account.  A 10 percent penalty would be imposed by the executive director if, upon review of a report, it is determined that a withdrawal or distribution of assets was for any purpose other than to pay eligible expenses incurred for a qualified home purchase, transfer assets to another similar first-time homebuyer savings account, pay fees charged by a financial institution that maintains the account, or pay expenses attributable to the death or disability of a certified first-time homebuyer or their spouse. The penalty would also be imposed if this report is not filed, regardless of the purpose of a withdrawal or distribution. Gross Income Tax Benefits for Certified First-Time Homebuyers with Program Accounts The bill also provides certain gross income tax benefits for certified first-time homebuyers with accounts created under the program.  The bill provides a gross income tax deduction for certain contributions made to a first-time homebuyer savings account. Specifically, an individual taxpayer would be allowed to deduct up to $20,000 in contributions made to an account during a taxable year while taxpayers who are married and filing jointly would be allowed to deduct up to $40,000 during a taxable year. The bill also excludes qualified distributions from the gross income of the taxpayer making the distribution.  The bill further specifies that the net profits from the sale of any property held in a first-time homebuyer savings account would not be considered taxable income until the year in which a distribution is made, except as otherwise excluded if the monies are withdrawn for a qualified distribution.  For purposes of the exclusion, a qualified distribution is defined as a withdrawal or distribution from a first-time homebuyer savings account to: (1)   pay eligible expenses incurred by the certified first-time homebuyer for a qualified home purchase; (2)   transfer assets to another first-time homebuyer savings account or to another similar account maintained through a comparable program in this State or another state or jurisdiction; (3)   pay fees charged to the certified first-time homebuyer by the financial institution for the establishment or maintenance of the account; or (4)   make a withdrawal or distribution attributable to the death or disability of a certified first-time homebuyer or their spouse. When filing a return for any taxable year in which a withdrawal or distribution is made from a first-time homebuyer savings account, a taxpayer would be required to attach to their return copies of each report submitted to the New Jersey Housing and Mortgage Finance Agency for a withdrawal or distribution that occurred during the taxable year.
