STATEMENT Currently, various public officials in the criminal and civil justice systems for this State and for the federal government, are covered under Daniels Law, P.L.2020, c.125 (C.56:8-166.2 et al.).  Daniels Law:  (1) prohibits the disclosure, by both governmental entities and private parties, of the home addresses of any covered person; (2) expands an existing crime concerning the disclosure of home addresses and unlisted telephone numbers of covered persons; and (3) permits criminal prosecutions and statutory civil actions concerning any prohibited disclosure.  Covered persons includes active, formerly active, or retired federal, State, county, or municipal judicial officer, prosecutor, or law enforcement officer. This bill expands the definition of covered persons under Daniels Law to include persons who attained the status as a covered person, whether in this State or in any other state.  Thus, a law enforcement officer who qualified as a law enforcement officer or as a covered person in any other state, could qualify for the privacy protection under Daniels Law. Under Daniels Law, a failure to comply with a request to cease disclosure and removal of information of a covered person would subject the person, business, or association to possible criminal penalties for committing a third degree crime (punishable by three to five years imprisonment; a fine of up to $15,000; or both) or fourth degree crime (up to 18 months imprisonment; a fine of up to $10,000; or both), or civil penalties (including either liquid damages of $1,000 for each violation, or actual damages if greater). It is the intention of the sponsor to ensure that the privacy protections for a covered person under Daniels Law shall apply, regardless of where that status, or a substantially similar status, was obtained.
