A5616 ASSEMBLY, No. 5616 STATE OF NEW JERSEY 222nd LEGISLATURE INTRODUCED OCTOBER 15, 2026 Sponsored by: Assemblyman  GERRY SCHARFENBERGER District 13 (Monmouth) SYNOPSIS Hire New Jersey Act; provides corporation business tax and gross income tax credits to small businesses for hiring and retaining certain New Jersey residents. CURRENT VERSION OF TEXT As introduced. An Act providing corporation business tax and gross income tax credits for small businesses hiring and retaining certain New Jersey residents, designated as the Hire Jersey Act, and supplementing P.L.1945, c.162 (C.54:10A-1 et seq.) and Title 54A of the New Jersey Statutes. Be It Enacted by the Senate and General Assembly of the State of New Jersey: 1.    a.  (1)  A taxpayer that is a qualified small business shall be allowed a credit against the tax imposed pursuant to section 5 of P.L.1945, c.162 (C.54:10A-5) in amount equal to the qualified wages paid by the taxpayer to an eligible employee during the privilege period, subject to limitations set forth in paragraph (2) of this subsection, except that total credit allowed to a taxpayer during any privilege period shall not exceed $50,000. A taxpayer may only be allowed the credit once per eligible employee and may not claim a credit for the qualified wages paid to the same eligible employee in any successive privilege period. (2) The amount of the credit allowed pursuant to this subsection for each eligible employee shall be subject to the greater of the following limitations: (a)   $2,500 for an eligible employee; (b)   $4,000 for an eligible employee who is a veteran, is a person with a disability, or a recent graduate; or (c)   $5,000 for an eligible employee who is paid at least 125 percent of the local median wage. b.    The order of priority of the application of the credit allowed pursuant to this section, and any other credits allowed by law against the tax imposed pursuant to section 5 of P.L.1945, c.162 (C.54:10A-5), shall be as prescribed by the director.  The amount of the credit applied under this section against the tax imposed pursuant to section 5 of P.L.1945, c.162 (C.54:10A-5) for a c.162 (C.54:10A-5).  The amount of the tax credit otherwise allowable under this section which cannot be applied for the privilege period due to the limitations of this subsection may be carried forward, if necessary, to the three privilege periods following the privilege period for which the tax credit was issued, after which the tax credit shall expire. c.     When filing a return that STATEMENT This bill provides corporation business tax and gross income tax credits to qualified small businesses that employ certain State residents for at least 12 months. Under the bill, a qualified small business would be allowed a tax credit, up to $50,000 per taxable period, based on the wages paid to an eligible employee during the taxable period. However, the bill provides that a qualified small business may only claim the tax credit once per eligible employee.  Specifically, the amount of the credit allowed for each eligible employee may not exceed the greater of: (1) $2,500 for an eligible employee; (2) $4,000 for an eligible employee that is a veteran, a person with a disability, or a recent graduate of a State community college; or (3) $5,000 for an eligible employee that is paid at least 125 percent of median wage paid to full-time employees in the county in which the employee principally performs their work duties. Under the bill, a qualified small business includes any business that is registered to do business in this State, maintains a business location or otherwise derives a majority of its income from business activities or operations conducted within the State, and has no more than 75 employees in any calendar month during the taxable year for which the credit is claimed.  The bill also defines eligible employee as an individual who resides in the State and is employed by a qualified small business for consideration for at least 35 hours a week, or who renders any standard service generally accepted by custom or practice as full-time employment, and who has been employed in such capacity for not less than 12 consecutive months.
