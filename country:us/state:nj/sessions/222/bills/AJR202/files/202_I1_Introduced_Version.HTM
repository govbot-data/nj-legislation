A Joint Resolution designating October 7 of each year as Antisemitism Day of Remembrance in New Jersey.

 

Whereas, On October 7, 2023, the terrorist organization Hamas carried out a brutal attack against the Jewish people and the State of Israel; and

Whereas, This barbaric attack took place just after the 50th anniversary of the start of the multi-front 1973 Yom Kippur War against Israel; and

Whereas, The attack began in the early morning when Hamas launched thousands of rockets from the Gaza Strip that reached as far as Tel Aviv and the outskirts of Jerusalem, and then infiltrated Israeli towns and army bases in the south, including a gathering of young people at a music festival; and

Whereas, The armed terrorists launched a coordinated strike arriving in boats, paragliders, motorcycles, and other vehicles; and

Whereas, News reports, survivor accounts, images, and videos from the scenes of this horrific attack show that Hamas terrorists slaughtered ordinary civilians and entire families, including babies and elderly people, set houses on fire, raped women, and took hostages; and

Whereas, In the immediate aftermath of the attack, 1,200 people were confirmed dead in Israel, including American citizens, and another 3,400 were wounded; and

Whereas, The October 7th attack was one of the deadliest terrorist attacks in modern history, resulting in more Jews killed that day than on any single day since the Holocaust; and

Whereas, Hamas held 251 men, women, and children hostage in Gaza, including American citizens; and

Whereas, The hostages were kept in inhumane and torturous conditions, including in cramped underground tunnels, since their kidnapping on October 7th; and

Whereas, Hamas executed many of these hostages, including six individuals who were shot at close range on August 29, 2024, as Israeli Defense Forces approached their location; and

Whereas, Of the 251 hostages captured by Hamas, only 168 survived and returned home and Hamas used the return of the dead bodies as part of its negotiations with Israel; and

Whereas, The October 7th terror attack prompted a defensive war against Hamas by the Israeli Defense Forces; and

Whereas, Many, including prominent political leaders, have falsely accused Israel of committing genocide in Gaza; and

Whereas, Such false accusations of genocide have resulted in increased anti-Israeli rhetoric, a malicious delegitimization of the State of Israel, and a surge in antisemitic attacks on Jewish people in this State and around the world; and

Whereas, The events of October 7, 2023, the false accusations of genocide, and the subsequent surge in antisemitic attacks against Jewish people around the world demonstrate the need to fight against the evil of antisemitism; now, therefore,

 

     Be It Resolved by the Senate and General Assembly of the State of New Jersey:

 

     1.  October 7 of each year is designated as Antisemitism Day of Remembrance in New Jersey to remember those who were killed or captured during the Hamas terror attacks and to continue the fight against antisemitism.

 

     2.  The Governor is respectfully requested to annually issue a proclamation calling upon public officials and citizens of the State to observe Antisemitism Day of Remembrance with appropriate activities and programs.

 

     3.  This joint resolution shall take effect immediately.

 

 

STATEMENT

 

     This joint resolution designates October 7 of each year as a Antisemitism Day of Remembrance in New Jersey.

     On October 7, 2023, the terrorist organization Hamas carried out a brutal attack against the Jewish people and the State of Israel.  This barbaric attack took place just after the 50th anniversary of the start of the multi-front 1973 Yom Kippur War against Israel.  The attack began in the early morning when Hamas launched thousands of rockets from the Gaza Strip that reached as far as Tel Aviv and the outskirts of Jerusalem, and then infiltrated Israeli towns and army bases in the south, including a gathering of young people at a music festival.  The armed terrorists launched a coordinated strike arriving in boats, paragliders, motorcycles, and other vehicles.

     News reports, survivor accounts, images, and videos from the scenes of this horrific attack show that Hamas terrorists slaughtered ordinary civilians and entire families, including babies and elderly people, set houses on fire, raped women, and took hostages.  In the immediate aftermath of the attack, 1,200 people were confirmed dead in Israel, including American citizens, and another 3,400 were wounded.  The attack was one of the deadliest terrorist attacks in modern history, resulting in more Jews killed that day than on any single day since the Holocaust.

     Hamas held 251 men, women, and children hostage in Gaza, including American citizens, in inhumane and torturous conditions.  Hamas executed many of the hostages, as only 168 survived and returned home, and used the return of the dead bodies as part of its negotiations with Israel. 

     The terror attack prompted a defensive war against Hamas by the Israeli Defense Forces.  Many prominent political leaders, such as New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani, have falsely accused Israel of committing genocide in Gaza.  Israel has strongly rejected all accusations that it committed genocide in Gaza, insisting that the Israeli Defense Forces only target Hamas and other terrorist groups while actively taking substantial measures to minimize civilian casualties.  Such false accusations of genocide have resulted in increased anti-Israeli rhetoric, a malicious delegitimization of the State of Israel, and a surge in antisemitic attacks on Jewish people in this State and around the world.  The events of October 7, 2023, the false accusations of genocide, and the subsequent surge in antisemitic attacks against Jewish people around the world demonstrate the need to fight against the evil of antisemitism.  Consequently, it is important to remember those who were killed or captured during the Hamas terror attacks and to continue the fight against antisemitism.
