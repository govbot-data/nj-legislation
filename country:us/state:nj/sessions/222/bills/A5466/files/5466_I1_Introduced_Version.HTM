     Authorizes podiatric physicians to supervise physician assistants.

     b.    A podiatric physician who permits a physician assistant under the podiatric physicians supervision to practice contrary to the provisions of P.L.1991, c.378 (C.45:9-27.10 et seq.) shall be deemed to have engaged in professional misconduct in violation of subsection e. of section 8 of P.L.1978, c.73 (C.45:1-21) and shall be subject to disciplinary action by the board pursuant to P.L.1978, c.73 (C.45:1-14 et seq.);

     c.     (1)        In the performance of all practice-related activities, including, but not limited to, the ordering of diagnostic, therapeutic, and other podiatric medical services, a physician assistant shall be conclusively presumed to be the agent of the podiatric physician under whose supervision the physician assistant is practicing. 

     (2)   A physician assistant under the supervision of a podiatric physician shall be authorized to prescribe controlled substances equivalent to the level at which a podiatric physician is authorized to prescribe controlled substances. 

     d.    A podiatric physician who supervises a physician assistant may maintain a written delegation agreement with the physician assistant.  A physician assistant shall sign a separate written agreement with each podiatric physician who delegates podiatric medical services pursuant to regulations as determined by the board.  The agreement shall:

     (1)   state that the podiatric physician will exercise supervision over the physician assistant in accordance with the provisions of P.L.1991, c.378 (C.45:9-27.10 et seq.) and any rules adopted by the board;

     (2)   be signed and dated annually by the podiatric physician and the physician assistant, and updated as necessary to reflect any changes in the practice or the physician assistant's role in the practice; and

     (3)   be kept on file at the practice site, be provided to the Physician Assistant Advisory Committee, and be kept on file by the committee.

     e.     The delegation agreement shall include, but need not be limited to, the following provisions:

     (1)   The physician assistant's role in the practice, including any specific aspects of care that require prior consultation with the supervising podiatric physician;

     (2)   A determination of whether the supervising podiatric physician requires personal review of all charts and records of patients and countersignature by the supervising podiatric physician of all podiatric medical services performed under the delegation agreement.  This provision shall state the specified time period in which a review and countersignature shall be completed by the supervising physician.  If no review and countersignature is necessary, the agreement shall specifically state as much; and

     (3)   The locations of practice where the physician assistant may practice under the delegation agreement, including licensed facilities in which the podiatric physician authorizes the physician assistant to provide podiatric medical services.

STATEMENT

 

     This bill authorizes podiatric physicians to supervise physician assistants in the performance of podiatric medical services.  Under the bill, podiatric physicians are incorporated into various provisions of current law regulating the oversight provided now by State-licensed physicians when supervising a physician assistant.  The State Board of Medical Examiners is to determine, among other items, the podiatric medical and surgical procedures and services a physician assistant is to provide and perform in a podiatric medicine office, a health care facility, an institution, a veterans' home, or a private home.  The bill further stipulates that (1) it is misconduct for a podiatric physician who permits a physician assistant under the podiatric physicians supervision to practice contrary to the provisions of the current law governing physician assistants; and (2) in the performance of all practice-related activities, a physician assistant is to be conclusively presumed to be the agent of the podiatric physician under whose supervision the physician assistant is practicing. 

     The bill also provides that a podiatric physician who supervises a physician assistant is given the option to maintain a written delegation agreement with the physician assistant but a physician assistant is required to sign a separate written agreement with each podiatric physician who delegates podiatric medical services pursuant to regulations as determined by the board.  The bill delineates what is to be included in the written agreement.
