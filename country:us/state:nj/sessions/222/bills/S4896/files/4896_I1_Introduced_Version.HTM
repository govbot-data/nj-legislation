SYNOPSIS

     Establishes "Toxic-Free Cosmetics Act."

 

CURRENT VERSION OF TEXT

     As introduced.

  


An Act concerning certain ingredients in cosmetic products, and supplementing Title 56 of the Revised Statutes.

 

     Be It Enacted by the Senate and General Assembly of the State of New Jersey:

 

     1.  This act shall be known and may be cited as the "Toxic-Free Cosmetics Act."

 

     2.  As used in this act:

     "Cosmetic product" means an article for retail sale or professional use intended to be rubbed, poured, sprinkled, or sprayed on, introduced into, or otherwise applied to the human body for cleansing, beautifying, promoting attractiveness, or altering appearance.

     "Ingredient" means any single chemical entity or mixture used as a component in the manufacture of a cosmetic product, but shall not include an incidental ingredient as described in 21 C.F.R. s.701.3(l).

     "Intentionally added" means an ingredient that a manufacturer has intentionally added to a cosmetic product that has a functional or technical effect on the product, which includes an intentional breakdown product of an added chemical.

     "Intentionally added PFAS" means PFAS that a manufacturer has intentionally added to a cosmetic product that have a functional or technical effect on the product, including PFAS that are intentional breakdown products of an added chemical.

     "Manufacturer" means a person that manufactures a cosmetic product or whose brand name is affixed to the cosmetic product.

     "Perfluoroalkyl and polyfluoroalkyl substances" or "PFAS" means a class of fluorinated organic chemicals containing at least one fully fluorinated carbon atom.

 

     3.  a.  Except as provided in subsection b. of this section, no person shall knowingly manufacture, sell, offer for sale, deliver, hold, or distribute for sale or use in this State a cosmetic product that contains any of the following intentionally added ingredients:

     (1) dibutyl phthalate (CAS No. 84-74-2);

     (2) diethylhexyl phthalate (CAS No. 117-81-7);

     (3) formaldehyde (CAS No. 50-00-0);

     (4) paraformaldehyde (CAS No. 30525-89-4);

     (5) methylene glycol (CAS No. 463-57-0);

     (6) quaternium-15;

     (7) mercury (CAS No. 7439-97-6);

     (8) isobutylparaben (CAS No. 4247-02-3);

     (9) isopropylparaben (CAS No. 4191-73-5);

     (10) m-phenylenediamine and its salts (CAS No. 108-45-2);

     (11) o-phenylenediamine and its salts (CAS No. 95-54-5); or

     (12) intentionally added PFAS.

     b.  A person shall not be in violation of subsection a. of this section if the cosmetic product was manufactured through a process intended to comply with this act and contains only a technically unavoidable trace quantity of an ingredient prohibited pursuant to subsection a. of this section that results from an impurity of a natural or synthetic ingredient, the manufacturing process, storage, or migration from packaging.

     c.  The provisions of this section shall not apply to a cosmetic product that contains intentionally added PFAS only in an electronic component or other internal component that is designed and intended not to come into contact with the human body during the intended use or handling of the product.

 

     4.  a.  A violation of section 3 of this act shall constitute an unlawful practice pursuant to P.L.1960, c.39 (C.56:8-1 et seq.), and shall be subject to all remedies and penalties available pursuant to that act.

     b.  In an enforcement action under this section involving the sale or distribution of a cosmetic product by a retailer, it shall be a defense that the retailer relied in good faith on a written certification from the manufacturer or supplier that the cosmetic product complied with section 3 of this act, unless the retailer knew or reasonably should have known that the certification was false or misleading.

 

     5.  The Attorney General, in consultation with the Commissioner of Health, may adopt rules and regulations, pursuant to the "Administrative Procedure Act," P.L.1968, c.410 (C.52:14B-1 et seq.), as may be necessary to effectuate the purposes of this act.

 

     6.  This act shall take effect immediately, but section 3 shall remain inoperative until the first day of the twenty-fifth month next following the date of enactment.  The Attorney General and the Commissioner of Health may take such anticipatory administrative action in advance thereof as shall be necessary for the implementation of this act.

 

 

STATEMENT

 

     This bill establishes the "Toxic-Free Cosmetics Act."

     This bill would prohibit the manufacture, sale, offer for sale, delivery, holding, or distribution for sale or use in New Jersey of cosmetic products containing certain intentionally added ingredients.  The prohibited ingredients include dibutyl phthalate, diethylhexyl phthalate, formaldehyde, paraformaldehyde, methylene glycol, quaternium-15, mercury, isobutylparaben, isopropylparaben, m-phenylenediamine and its salts, o-phenylenediamine and its salts, and intentionally added perfluoroalkyl and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS).

     The bill provides an exception for technically unavoidable trace quantities resulting from impurities, manufacturing, storage, or migration from packaging.  The bill also provides a limited exemption for PFAS present only in electronic or internal components that are not intended to contact the human body.

     A violation of the bills provisions would constitute an unlawful practice under N.J.S.A.56:8-1 et seq., the New Jersey consumer fraud act.
