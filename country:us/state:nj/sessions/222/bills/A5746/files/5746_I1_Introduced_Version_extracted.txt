A5746 ASSEMBLY, No. 5746 STATE OF NEW JERSEY 222nd LEGISLATURE INTRODUCED NOVEMBER 16, 2026 Sponsored by: Assemblywoman  MELINDA KANE District 6 (Burlington and Camden) SYNOPSIS Youth Heat Safety Act; establishes heat-related illness prevention program for youth serving entities. CURRENT VERSION OF TEXT As introduced. An Act concerning youth serving entities and supplementing Title 26 of the Revised Statutes. Be It Enacted by the Senate and General Assembly of the State of New Jersey: 1.    This act shall be known and may be cited as the Youth Heat Safety Act. 2.    The Legislature finds and declares that: a.     New Jersey is experiencing more frequent, longer, and more intense episodes of extreme heat, including higher nighttime temperatures, as a result of climate change. b.    Children and adolescents are physiologically more vulnerable to heat-related illnesses than adults and may be less able to recognize or report early warning signs of heat stress. c.     Heat-related illnesses, including heat exhaustion and heat stroke, are largely preventable with appropriate training, environmental monitoring, activity modification, hydration, acclimatization, and emergency response protocols. d.    Structured youth activities, including interscholastic athletics, band and performing arts practices, physical education, and summer camps and recreational programs, often involve vigorous outdoor exercise during the warmest months and after school hours, when heat risk is highest. e.     Current protections for New Jersey youth are inconsistent and depend on local resources, the presence of athletic trainers, and voluntary policies, resulting in unequal protection for children based on geography and socioeconomic status. f.     Establishing Statewide minimum standards for heat illness education and extreme heat protocols for youth serving programs will reduce preventable morbidity and mortality, promote health equity, and serve as an important climate adaptation measure. 3.    For purposes of this act: Coach or supervising staff member means a person, whether compensated or serving as a volunteer, who organizes, instructs, or directly supervises participants in a structured activity on behalf of a youth serving entity. Emergency action plan means a written plan establishing the procedures, responsibilities, and actions to be followed in response to a suspected heat-related illness or other medical emergency occurring during a structured activity. Extreme heat means environmental conditions that create a substantial risk of heat-related illness, as determined using the heat index, WetBulb Globe Temperature, or another science-based environmental heat metric approved by the Commissioner of Health. Heat-related illness means heat cramps, heat syncope, heat exhaustion, heat stroke, or any other condition designated by the Commissioner of Health as resulting from excessive heat exposure. Structured activity means an activity conducted by or on behalf of a youth serving entity including, but not limited to, interscholastic athletics, physical education, band, performing arts, recreational activities, and other activities involving physical activity or exposure to outdoor heat. Youth serving entity means an entity including, but not limited to: (1) a public or nonpublic school, charter school, renaissance school, or county vocational school; (2) a school district subject to the provisions of P.L.2019, c.293 (C.18A:11-3.10); (3) a local educational agency; (4) a youth camp licensed pursuant to the New Jersey Youth Camp Safety Act, P.L.1973, c.375 (C.26:12-1 et seq.); (5) any municipal or county recreational program; or (6) any private youth sports organization that organizes, coordinates, or supervises structured activities for participants under 18 years of age. 4.    a.  The Commissioner of Health, in consultation with the Commissioner of Education and the Commissioner of Environmental Protection, shall establish a mandatory heat-related illness prevention and response training program for coaches and supervising staff members of youth serving entities who oversee structured activities during the period from May 1 through October 31. b.    The training program established pursuant to subsection a. of this section shall include, but not be limited to: (1)   risk factors for heat-related illness among children and adolescents, including medical conditions, medications, protective equipment, and environmental conditions; (2)   the signs and symptoms of heat cramps, heat syncope, heat exhaustion, heat stroke, and other heat-related illnesses; (3)   the use of environmental monitoring tools, including heat index or WetBulb Globe Temperature, and air quality alerts, to assess heat risk; (4)   activity modification and cancellation guidelines based on environmental conditions, including work-to-rest ratios, hydration breaks, and adjustments to protective equipment; and (5)   immediate on-site management of suspected heat-related illness, including activation of emergency medical services and rapid cooling techniques, when appropriate. c.     Beginning with the first full school year following the effective date of this section, each youth serving entity shall provide the training program established pursuant to subsection a. of this section to each coach or supervising staff member who oversees or may oversee structured activities during the period from May 1 through October 31, as part of initial onboarding and at least once every two years thereafter. d.    The Commissioner of Health shall make the training program established pursuant to subsection a. of this section available to youth serving entities at no cost.  The training program may include online modules developed by national or State agencies and professional organizations, including the New Jersey Interagency Council on Climate Resilience. 5.    a.  Each youth serving entity shall adopt and implement a written heat safety policy and emergency action plan governing structured activities during the period from May 1 through October 31 and during periods of extreme heat, consistent with the rules and regulations adopted pursuant to section 10 of this act. b.    The heat safety policy and emergency action plan shall, at a minimum: (1)   identify the environmental metric or metrics to be used to assess heat risk, the method by which environmental conditions shall be measured, and the frequency of measurements; (2)   establish graduated requirements for modifying or canceling structured activities based upon environmental conditions, including requirements concerning rest and hydration breaks, practice duration and intensity, scheduling, and the use of shade or other measures to reduce heat exposure; (3) designate the person or persons responsible for monitoring environmental conditions and for making or implementing decisions concerning the modification or cancellation of structured activities; (4)   establish procedures for the prompt recognition and management of suspected heat-related illness, including immediate removal from activity, on-site first aid, rapid cooling, and activation of emergency medical services when appropriate; and (5)   establish procedures for communicating with parents or guardians concerning heat-related risks and significant modifications or cancellations of structured activities. c.     Each youth serving entity shall review its heat safety policy and emergency action plan at least annually and shall revise the policy and plan as necessary, including following a serious heat-related illness incident. 6.    The Department of Health, in consultation with the Department of Children and Families and the Department of Community Affairs, and in coordination with other State agencies having jurisdiction over youth serving entities, shall incorporate the requirements of this act into existing licensing, inspection, registration, or oversight processes, as appropriate, in a manner that minimizes duplicative administrative requirements. 7.    a. The Commissioner of Health, in consultation with the Commissioner of Education, the Commissioner of Environmental Protection, and relevant professional organizations, shall establish and periodically update guidelines for the implementation of this act. b.    The guidelines established pursuant to subsection a. of this section shall include, but not be limited to: (1)   standardized environmental metrics and threshold values for modifying or canceling structured activities; (2)   model heat safety policies and emergency action plans that may be adopted by youth serving entities; and (3)   recommendations concerning environmental monitoring equipment, shade structures, cooling supplies, and other equipment appropriate to the size, type, and resources of the youth serving entity. c.     The Department of Health shall make the guidelines, established pursuant to subsection a. of this section, publicly available on its Internet website and may provide technical assistance to youth serving entities concerning the implementation of the guidelines, with priority given to schools and youth programs located in low-income or high-heat-burden communities. 8.    a.  Each youth serving entity shall maintain documentation demonstrating compliance with the training requirements established pursuant to section 4 of this act and the adoption and implementation of the heat safety policy and emergency action plan required pursuant to section 5 of this act. b.    The documentation required pursuant to subsection a. of this section shall be made available, upon request, to the Department of Health or another State agency having jurisdiction over youth serving entities. 9.    Within three years following the effective date of this act, and periodically thereafter as determined by the Commissioner of Health, the Department of Health shall review available data concerning heat-related illness among children and adolescents in this State and evaluate the implementation and effectiveness of this act.  The Department of Health shall submit a report of its findings and recommendations to the Governor and, pursuant to section 2 of P.L.1991, c.164 (C.52:14-19.1), to the Legislature.  The report shall include, to the extent practicable, information concerning compliance with the requirements of this act and recommendations for improving heat safety for structured activities for youth. 10.  The Commissioner of Health, in consultation with the Commissioner of Education and the Commissioner of Environmental Protection, shall, in accordance with the "Administrative Procedure Act," P.L.1968, c.410 (C.52:14B-1 et seq.), adopt rules and regulations as necessary to implement this act. 11.  This act shall take effect immediately, except that sections 4, 5, and 6 shall take effect on the first day of the 13th month next following the date of enactment. STATEMENT This bill establishes the Youth Heat Safety Act, which creates a heat-related illness prevention program for youth serving entities. The provisions of this bill require the Commissioner of Health, in consultation with the Commissioner of Education and the Commissioner of Environmental Protection, to establish a heat-related illness prevention and response training program for coaches and supervising staff members who oversee structured activities during the period of May 1 to October 31.  The training is to address the risk factors, signs, and symptoms of heat-related illness; environmental monitoring; activity modification and cancellation; and the appropriate response to suspected heat-related illness, including the use of rapid cooling techniques and activation of emergency medical services when appropriate.  The training program is to be made available to youth serving entities at no cost. Under the bill, each youth serving entity is required to adopt and implement a written heat safety policy and emergency action plan for structured activities.  The policy and plan are required to address the use of environmental metrics to assess heat risk, activity modification and cancellation, responsibility for making decisions concerning structured activities, emergency response procedures, and communication with parents or guardians. The bill requires the Department of Health to coordinate with other State agencies to incorporate the requirements of the bill into existing licensing, inspection, registration, and oversight processes, as appropriate.  The bill also directs the Commissioner of Health to establish and periodically update guidelines addressing environmental metrics and thresholds, model heat safety policies and emergency action plans, and recommendations concerning heat safety equipment and resources. Additionally, the bill allows the Department of Health to provide technical assistance to youth serving entities concerning the implementation of the established guidelines, with priority given to schools and youth programs located in low-income or high-heat-burden communities.  Under the bill, youth serving entities are required to maintain documentation demonstrating compliance with the training and heat safety policy requirements of the bill. The bill also requires the Department of Health to review available data on heat-related illness among children and adolescents and evaluate the implementation and effectiveness of the bill.  The department is required to report its findings and recommendations to the Governor and Legislature within three years after the bill takes effect and periodically thereafter.
