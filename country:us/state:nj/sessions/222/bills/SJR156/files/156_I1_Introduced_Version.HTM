A Joint Resolution designating July 10 of each year as Lake Denmark Disaster Remembrance Day in New Jersey.

 

Whereas, The Picatinny Arsenal, located in Morris County, was originally established in 1880, and by 1911, it became a major site for gunpowder and munitions manufacturing as well as research and development work; and

Whereas, On the arsenal was the Lake Denmark Naval Ammunition Depot, which by the 1920s became overstocked with ammunition left unused during the First World War; and

Whereas, On Saturday, July 10, 1926 at approximately 5:15 p.m., lightning struck one of the above-ground temporary magazines, causing a fire that triggered the detonation of over 660,000 pounds of explosives and started a massive chain reaction that consumed millions of pounds of explosives across the arsenal in the following days; and

Whereas, The enormity of the explosions was such that the ground shook and glass shattered in Rockaway Borough, residents reported embers and ash fifteen miles away in Passaic County, and debris could be found over 20 miles away; and

Whereas, These explosions killed 19 individuals attempting to quell the fires, including 12 Marines, four sailors, one soldier, and two civilians, and injured about another 50 people; and

Whereas, The blasts caused the complete and total annihilation of structures within a radius of approximately 2,700 feet and damaged buildings up to 8,700 feet away, destroying 187 of 200 buildings at the depot and resulting in damage that would be worth more than $700 million today; and

Whereas, Investigations by the Army and Navy revealed that the storage of unused ammunition in the magazines surpassed reasonable limits and the initial explosion was due to an excess of 1.6 million pounds of trinitrotoluene, also known as TNT, stored in a single building; and

Whereas, The catastrophe at the Lake Denmark Naval Ammunition Depot led to the establishment of the Armed Forces Explosives Safety Board as well as a new set of standards concerning the design of ammunition depots and storage safety requirements for all arsenals across the nation; and

Whereas, While the tragic disaster that claimed the lives of those bravely serving their country continues to be one of the worst in New Jersey history, it became an impetus for significant safety reforms in the Armed Forces; and

Whereas, In July 2026, the Army signaled its enduring commitment to protecting its workforce through continuous improvements in safety, risk management, and operational excellence during its 100th anniversary commemoration of the 1926 explosion at the Picatinny Arsenal; and

Whereas, It is altogether fitting and proper for the people of New Jersey to mark the 100th anniversary of the Lake Denmark Naval Ammunition Depot disaster by honoring those who perished and recognizing the paramount importance of workplace safety; now, therefore,

 

     Be It Resolved by the Senate and General Assembly of the State of New Jersey:

 

     1.    July 10 of each year shall be designated as Lake Denmark Disaster Remembrance Day in the State of New Jersey, in recognition of its significance to New Jersey history and its impact on modern workplace safety standards established by the Armed Forces.

 

     2.    The Governor is respectfully requested to annually issue a proclamation calling upon public officials and citizens of this State to observe Lake Denmark Disaster Remembrance Day with appropriate activities and programs.        

 

     3.    This joint resolution shall take effect immediately.

 

 

STATEMENT

 

     This joint resolution designates July 10 of each year as Lake Denmark Disaster Remembrance Day in New Jersey (NJ).

     The Picatinny Arsenal, located in Morris County, was originally established in 1880, and by 1911, it became a major site for gunpowder and munitions manufacturing as well as research and development work.  On the arsenal was the Lake Denmark Naval Ammunition Depot, which by the 1920s became overstocked with ammunition left unused during the First World War.

     On July 10, 1926 at approximately 5:15 p.m., lightning struck one of the above-ground temporary magazines, causing a fire that triggered the detonation of over 660,000 pounds of explosives and starting a massive chain reaction that consumed millions of pounds of explosives across the arsenal in the following days.  The enormity of the explosions was such that the ground shook and glass shattered in Rockaway Borough, residents reported embers and ash fifteen miles away in Passaic County, and debris could be found over 20 miles away.  These explosions killed 19 individuals attempting to quell the fires, including 12 Marines, four sailors, one soldier, and two civilians, and injured about another 50 people.  The blasts caused the complete and total annihilation of structures within a radius of approximately 2,700 feet and damaged buildings up to 8,700 feet away, destroying 187 of 200 buildings at the depot and resulting in damage that would be worth more than $700 million today.

     Investigations by the Army and Navy revealed that the storage of unused ammunition in the magazines surpassed reasonable limits and the initial explosion was due to an excess of 1.6 million pounds of trinitrotoluene, also known as TNT, stored in a single building.  The catastrophe at the Lake Denmark Naval Ammunition Depot led to the establishment of the Armed Forces Explosives Safety Board as well as a new set of standards concerning the design of ammunition depots and storage safety requirements for all arsenals across the nation.

     While the tragic disaster that claimed the lives of those bravely serving the country continues to be one of the worst in New Jersey history, it became an impetus for significant safety reforms in the Armed Forces.  In July 2026, the Army signaled its enduring commitment to protecting its workforce through continuous improvements in safety, risk management, and operational excellence during its 100th anniversary commemoration of the 1926 explosion at the Picatinny Arsenal.
